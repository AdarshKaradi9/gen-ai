## 1. Which mechanics have hard data versus opinion (streaks, leagues, daily drops, events, progress, XP/badges, social, AI feedback, future-self, notifications, variable rewards)

### Takeaway
The strongest public evidence is for **streaks and streak-protection** (Duolingo A/B tests plus a 2023 JCR paper showing broken streaks lower later engagement and repair softens the drop), for **leaderboards and streak-saver notifications** (Duolingo's growth model), and for **future-self imagery**, **fresh starts**, and **gamification in learning**. Only the gamification-for-learning evidence is meta-analytic, and it is modest and fragile. Most other mechanics (daily drops, time-limited events, badges, variable rewards, AI feedback) rest mostly on expert opinion and case narratives in the sources gathered here.

### Cited Findings
**Streaks: company A/B data (Duolingo)**
- [HARD, company blog] Separating the daily goal from the streak raised Day-14 retention by 3.3% (relative), daily active learners by 1%, and the share of daily learners on a streak by 10.5% over 20 days. — [Duolingo blog, "Improving the streak"](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak) (reported via search extract; full text not fetched)
- [HARD, company engineering blog] The "Streak Wager" test produced statistically significant gains in D1, D7, and D14 retention, with the largest at D7 (+14%). — [Duolingo engineering, "How streaks keep Duolingo learners committed"](https://making.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)
- [HARD/EXPERT, PM interview] A failed test: letting users extend a streak with one exercise instead of a full lesson did not raise DAU and mostly captured the least engaged users. Jackson Shuttleworth (Group PM, Retention) also discussed building Streak Freeze, but the search extracts give no numbers for Streak Freeze. — [Lenny's Newsletter, "Behind the product: Duolingo streaks"](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks); [Recall summary of the podcast](https://www.recall.it/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)
- [HARD, company growth narrative] Jorge Mazal (former CPO) credits leaderboards, more flexible push notifications, and the streak system with raising CURR. A user who reached a 10-day streak was much less likely to drop off. The first big win was a **streak-saver notification** sent to users about to lose a streak. His first gamification push was "completely neutral" (no change to retention or DAU). DAU grew 4.5x over four years. One secondary summary says "450%", which conflicts with "4.5x". — [Lenny's Newsletter, "How Duolingo reignited user growth"](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth) (search extract); [Duolingo blog, growth model](https://blog.duolingo.com/growth-model-duolingo)
- [HARD, 2026 shareholder letter] Duolingo said a **one-time event to revive lost streaks** contributed to its DAU acceleration in Q2 2026. Eligible learners could restore their longest-ever streak, and nearly 8 million participants had no active streak when the event began. — [Duolingo Q2 2026 shareholder letter](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/3c8277ee-bc94-4f5d-9b77-0db3e46f88b8)

**Streaks: academic evidence**
- [HARD, peer-reviewed, 2023] Silverman & Barasch, "On or Off Track: How (Broken) Streaks Affect Consumer Decisions," *Journal of Consumer Research* 49(6):1095–1117 (DOI 10.1093/jcr/ucac029). Highlighting intact streaks increases later engagement compared with highlighting broken streaks. A broken streak lowers the chance of continuing, **even when the break was caused by outside events**. The proposed mechanism is lost momentum, so later decisions become more deliberate and less automatic. The effect is stronger when people blame themselves for the break and weaker when they can **repair** it. People will pay costs to repair a broken streak. — [UDel repository](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb/full); [UDaily 2024](https://udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/power-of-streaks-motivation-jackie-silverman); [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation)
- [EXPERT] Silverman's advice: apps should avoid dwelling on broken streaks and should offer other ways to keep streaks going, such as repair. — [UDaily](https://udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/power-of-streaks-motivation-jackie-silverman)

**Streaks: harms and monetized repair (Snapchat)**
- [HARD-ish, advocacy survey, Mar 2026; seen only via secondary summary] Bits of Freedom surveyed 300 teens aged 13–21. 86% actively maintain Snapstreaks, 25% have paid about €0.99 to restore a broken streak, and 42% would remove Snapstreaks if it were optional. The group recommends that regulators make gamification opt-in. — [Pulse summary](https://www.pulse.bot/consumer-tech/blogs/new-study-reveals-how-young-people-are-influenced-by-gamification-features-on-snapchat-386b9eae-9eb9-4fb1-8d38-08092e9e2791) (verify against the original report)
- [HARD, peer-reviewed, small regional study, 2025] In a study of Egyptian teens, 35.4% rated streaks "very important". Researchers also reported late-night use, putting streaks ahead of sleep or study, and distress when streaks were lost. — [Media Research and Studies](https://mjsm.journals.ekb.eg/article_422365.html)
- [ANEC/secondary] Snapchat offers one free streak restore, then charges $0.99 per restore. It made its "Solar System" friend ranking opt-in after reports of teen anxiety. — [Screenwise guide](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/what-parents-need-to-know-about-snapchat-streaks); [Yahoo/UK report](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/snapchat-turned-off-controversial-friend-090000656.html)

**Leagues and leaderboards**
- [HARD, qualitative] Mazal names leaderboards as one of three vectors that raised CURR. The specific uplift figures (learning time, highly engaged users) are in the Lenny's post section "leaderboards vector" but could not be fetched. — [Lenny's Newsletter, leaderboards vector](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/i/104096876/leaderboards-vector)
- [HARD, meta-analysis] In learning contexts, adding social interaction and game fiction significantly moderated gamification's effect on behavioral outcomes. The authors treat combining competition with collaboration as promising but uncertain. — [Sailer & Homner, Educ Psych Review (2019 online / 2020 print)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)

**Gamification in general (XP, badges, levels, points)**
- [HARD, meta-analysis, 2020] Sailer & Homner found small effects: Hedges' g = 0.49 for cognitive outcomes (k=19, N=1,686), g = 0.36 for motivational outcomes (k=16, N=2,246), and smaller, unstable behavioral effects. Only the cognitive effect held up in high-rigor studies. The authors say the analysis was "highly likely to be underpowered". — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w); [Augsburg repository PDF](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/109056/109056.pdf)
- [HARD, company] Duolingo's first gamification push had no effect on retention or DAU. Points and badges alone are not enough; what worked was mechanics tied to a specific metric (CURR) and tested one at a time. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)

**Future-self visualization**
- [HARD, peer-reviewed, 2011, pre-2023] Hershfield et al., *JMR*: people who saw age-progressed renderings of themselves allocated more to retirement. In Study 4 (40 adults on MTurk, the only working population), allocations were 6.17% vs 4.41% of pay. A U.S. Department of Labor CLEAR review notes that Study 1's allocation result was **not statistically significant** at standard levels. Press coverage inflates the effect ("nearly twice as much"; "~33% more"). — [DOL CLEAR review](https://clear.dol.gov/study/increasing-saving-behavior-through-age-progressed-renderings-future-self-hershfield-et-al-2011); [NYU Stern](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/faculty-research/better-decisions-senior-self); [Financial Planning](https://www.financial-planning.com/news/to-boost-retirement-savings-advisors-should-help-clients-face-the-facts)
- No replication was found in this research pass (see Gaps).

**Fresh-start effect (useful for timing: Mondays, birthdays, the new year)**
- [HARD, peer-reviewed, 2014, pre-2023] Dai, Milkman & Riis, *Management Science*: Google searches for "diet", university gym card-swipe visits, and goal commitments on stickK all rose after temporal landmarks (new week, month, year, semester, birthday). One exception: students went to the gym less after turning 21. The data show more motivation and first steps, not lasting goal achievement. A circulating "+7.5% gym visits after birthdays" figure could not be verified. — [Paper PDF (Wharton)](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf); [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/minds-business/why-monday-is-the-best-day-for-setting-new-goals.html)

**Push notifications**
- [HARD, qualitative] Duolingo credits "more flexible push notifications" and the streak-saver notification as major CURR levers. No public lift percentages were retrieved. — [Lenny's Newsletter, push notifications vector](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/i/104096876/push-notifications-vector)

**Manipulative engagement in AI companions (relevant to "personalized AI" mechanics)**
- [HARD, working paper, not peer-reviewed, 2025] De Freitas et al., "Emotional Manipulation by AI Companions": 37% of 1,200 real farewells on top companion apps drew one of six manipulative tactics (guilt, FOMO hooks, "premature exit", metaphorical restraint, ignoring the goodbye). These tactics raised post-goodbye engagement up to **14x**. They also raised perceived manipulation, churn intent, and perceived legal liability. Flourish, a wellness app, showed no such tactics. Some outlets report 43% and five apps rather than six; the arXiv paper and Harvard Gazette figures are more reliable. — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/09/i-exist-solely-for-you-remember/); [aitopics/arXiv listing](https://aitopics.org/doc/arxivorg:FB6579AB)

### Inferences
- The best-supported "healthy streak" design: show the intact streak, de-emphasize breaks, and offer free or cheap repair and freezes. Shame-based or paywalled repair aimed at teens (Snapchat) is the model regulators are starting to target.
- Mechanics only work when tied to a north-star metric and tested one at a time. Duolingo's neutral first gamification attempt and Sailer & Homner's small, unstable behavioral effects both say "add badges" is not a retention strategy.
- For an AI app, the De Freitas result shows a short-term engagement gain from guilt and FOMO comes with higher churn intent and liability. That is a usable, data-backed ethical line.

### Gaps
- Not researched here (tool budget): goal-gradient (Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006), endowed progress (Nunes & Drèze 2006), variable-reward research, loss-aversion meta-evidence, social proof, Wordle-style daily drops, time-limited events, countdowns. These classic findings exist, but no URLs or numbers were verified in this pass.
- No public A/B figures were retrieved for Duolingo Streak Freeze or leagues. They are likely in the Lenny's post, but that host was unreachable.
- No independent replication of Hershfield's age-progression effect was found. Whether one exists or has failed is unknown.
- No hard data was found on "personalized AI feedback" as a retention lever.

## 2. Case studies with numbers

### Takeaway
Duolingo is the only case with audited, recent, public engagement data: DAU of 64.9M in Q3 2026, up 29% YoY, and a stated streak-revival boost. Apps built on novelty or anonymous social mechanics (BeReal, Gas, tbh) rose fast and collapsed. Self-care and fitness apps (Finch, Strava) show durable scale, but mostly through third-party estimates.

### Cited Findings

Q4 DAU growth is guided to be similar to Q3. MAU figures of 135.3M and 153.1M appear in the extracted text but were not clearly labeled by period. — [Q3 2026 shareholder letter](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/e1ee336e-b024-486f-875c-afd722653067)

### Inferences
- Durable apps (Duolingo, Strava, Finch) pair a daily ritual with real progress toward a self-improvement goal and offer forgiveness (repair, freeze). Collapses (BeReal, Gas, tbh) relied on novelty or social-anxiety loops without cumulative personal value.
- Duolingo's 2026 growth acceleration shows that **forgiveness** (restoring lost streaks) brings back lapsed users at scale, consistent with Silverman & Barasch's repair moderator.

## 3. Expert frameworks and benchmarks

### Takeaway
Public retention benchmarks conflict, mostly because of definitions (N-day vs rolling retention). Median D30 for mobile apps is single digits, while "strong consumer social" anchors are about D1 40%, D7 20%, D30 10%. The Lenny's/Casey Winters survey covers 6-month retention, not D30.

### Cited Findings
- [EXPERT, survey of about 20 practitioners, via secondary write-up] Lenny Rachitsky & Casey Winters: for consumer transactional apps, 6-month user retention of 30% is "good" and 50% is "great". This could not be verified against the original. — [Marketplace Guide summary](https://themarketplaceguide.pages.dev/articles/retention-benchmarks-decoded-what-good-actually-looks-like-for-marketplace-founders-and-why-your-bar-is-different/)
- [3P-EST] D30 benchmarks conflict: a median of 3.9% (top decile 10.9%) in one dataset, an industry median of 7% in another, and an anchor of D1 >40% / D7 >20% / D30 >10% for strong consumer social apps. Blog benchmarks of 15–30% D30 are usually rolling retention or top-decile figures. — [Plotline 2025](https://www.plotline.so/blog/retention-rates-mobile-apps-by-industry); [Unstar 2026](https://unstar.app/vi/blog/app-retention-benchmarks-2026); [Lovable guide](https://lovable.dev/ja/guides/what-is-a-good-retention-rate-for-an-app)
- [EXPERT/HARD] Duolingo's growth model (Mazal) is the most-cited worked example of a retention-state framework: users move between new, current, reactivated, and resurrected states, and the team optimizes the transition rates. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/growth-model-duolingo)

### Inferences
- When the report states a D30 target, it should name the definition used. Duolingo's roughly 41–42% DAU/MAU (derived) is an outlier-level benchmark for a daily self-improvement app.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: Nir Eyal's Hooked model sources, Andrew Chen, Elena Verna, Reforge, a16z DAU/MAU benchmarks and "smile curve", and original Lenny's retention posts (host unreachable).

## 4. Academic and behavioral research on habit formation

### Takeaway
Habit formation takes about two months on median but varies hugely between people (4–335 days). The evidence comes from very few studies, so any "X days to habit" product claim should be presented as a rough guide.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD, systematic review, 2024] Singh, Murphy, Maher & Smith, "Time to Form a Habit," *Healthcare* 12(23):2488. Of 20 included studies, only 4 reported time to habit. Medians were 59–66 days, means 106–154 days, and individual range 4–335 days. Pooled habit-score change SMD = 0.69 (95% CI 0.49–0.88). Timing was synthesized narratively, not meta-analyzed. In one included study only 23% of participants reached the habit threshold. — [Adelaide repository](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/fe6ba93e-1d6b-42b8-9ee7-a231a64ff006); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1071242); [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-make-habit-stick)
- [HARD, 2010, pre-2023] Lally et al. (the source of the "~66 days" figure) is included in this review's range. The original paper was not fetched separately.
- See Section 1 for streaks (2023), fresh start (2014), future self (2011), and gamification meta-analysis (2020).

### Inferences
- A daily app has to keep users through a 2+ month window before a behavior is plausibly automatic. Week-1 to month-3 retention mechanics (streak protection, fresh-start re-entry prompts) matter most.

### Gaps
- Goal-gradient, endowed progress, variable-ratio reward, and social-proof studies, plus gamification replication critiques beyond Sailer & Homner's own caveats, were not retrieved.

## 5. Ethics and regulation

### Takeaway
U.S. federal "click-to-cancel" was vacated in July 2025, but state auto-renewal laws remain. AI companions face the most active scrutiny: the FTC 6(b) study (September 2025), California SB 243 (in effect January 2026), and the Character.AI settlements. The EU's Digital Fairness Act, still pending, explicitly targets "penalties for disengagement", which arguably includes shame-based streaks.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD, court] The Eighth Circuit (Custom Communications v. FTC) vacated the FTC's Negative Option ("click-to-cancel") Rule on July 8, 2025, days before the July 14 compliance date, because the FTC skipped a required preliminary regulatory analysis. The older prenotification-plan rule remains. State auto-renewal laws still apply, including California's click-to-cancel law. Whether the FTC appealed or re-proposed the rule was not confirmed for 2026. — [Gunderson](https://www.gunder.com/en/news-insights/insights/client-insight-eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule); [AGG](https://www.agg.com/news-insights/publications/eighth-circuit-strikes-down-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule/)
- [HARD, agency action] In September 2025 the FTC issued 3-0 Section 6(b) orders to Alphabet, Character.AI, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap, and xAI about companion chatbots: safety testing, monetization, use of conversation data, and disclosures to parents. A 6(b) order is a study, not enforcement. No staff report was found. — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-sg/insights/publications/2025/09/ftc-ai-chatbots); [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102l4u3/ftc-launches-inquiry-into-ai-chatbots-acting-as-companions-what-it-means-for-adv)
- [HARD, statute] California SB 243 was signed October 13, 2025 and took effect January 1, 2026. It requires companion-chatbot operators to disclose that users are talking to an AI and adds safeguards for known minors. A private right of action of $1,000 per violation is reported by a secondary source only. AB 1064, which would have broadly restricted companion bots for minors, was vetoed. — [CA Lawyers Association](https://calawyers.org/privacy-law/regulatory-focus-on-ai-companion-character-chatbots/); [AI News](https://www.ainews.com/p/california-and-ftc-target-ai-chatbots-with-new-safeguards); [Kelley Drye PDF, Jan 2026](https://s3.amazonaws.com/cdn.kelleydrye.com/content/uploads/pdf-snapshots/ai-chatbots-face-rising-legal-and-legislative-scrutiny-20260116114458.pdf)
- [HARD, policy process] The EU Digital Fairness Act targets dark patterns, addictive design ("infinite scrolling, autoplay and **penalties for disengagement**"), and unfair personalization. Most consultation respondents favored switching addictive features off by default. Proposal timing is reported as Q3 or Q4 2026, with reports of internal Commission splits. Article 25 of the DSA already bans dark patterns on online platforms. — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/07/eu-consults-on-digital-fairness-act-big-changes-ahead-for-consumer-facing-platforms); [Slaughter and May](https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102m222/digital-fairness-act-european-commission-publishes-responses-to-consultation); [PinMeTo](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/eu-digital-fairness-act-scope-split-2026/)
- [HARD, regulatory precedent] Robinhood paid $7.5M (Massachusetts, 2024) over gamified celebrations of trading (Section 2). This is the clearest U.S. precedent that celebratory variable rewards tied to risky financial behavior are actionable.
- [HARD, litigation] Character.AI teen-harm suits were settled in January 2026, and the company banned minors from open-ended chats (Section 2).
- [HARD, working paper] Emotionally manipulative farewells in AI companions raise usage up to 14x but also churn intent and perceived liability (Section 1).

### Inferences
- A practical line between healthy engagement and dark patterns, built from these sources: (a) forgiveness over punishment (free repair, no shame for breaks); (b) no paywalled loss-recovery aimed at minors; (c) no celebration tied to risky or financial actions; (d) no guilt- or FOMO-based exit friction in AI conversations; (e) easy cancellation, which state law still requires.
- A GenAI companion-style product in health or wellbeing must plan for SB 243 compliance, age gating, and FTC 6(b)-type scrutiny from day one.
