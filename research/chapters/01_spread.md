## How Apps Spread: virality, creators, communities and the app store

Across this corpus, almost every founder who reports real revenue says the same thing in different words: building is now the easy part and distribution is the job. That claim is opinion, not data ("marketing is 90%" [vid:PX30Fb8TlcE], "95%" [vid:z_fARFqjLoY]). But the operating details underneath it are specific and repeat from one video to the next. Most numbers below are self-reported by founders or by agencies selling the method. Where a figure comes from a third party (CNBC documents, Sensor Tower estimates, an A/B test), the text says so.

### 1. The 3-Second Aha

**What it is.** The core value has to show up within seconds of opening the app, and the whole product is built around that one moment. Nikita Bier's rule is to "invert the time to value" and prove the value in about three seconds [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY]. Dupe.com does it with a URL trick: prefix a shopping link and you see the cheapest look-alike. Nikita says it reached millions in ARR within about 60 days (self-reported via Nikita) [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY].

**Why it works.** A fast aha keeps the user, and it also gives you the content. When the value fits in one shot, that shot becomes the video. Cal AI's founders call this "marketing first": they picked one eye-catching feature (photograph food, get the calories) and marketed only that [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M]. NGL's onboarding takes 15-20 seconds, with "one flow, one funnel" and one north-star metric [vid:DTree0unT_0].

**How to apply.** Cut features until the aha fits in a 2-second screen recording. Blake Anderson's test is whether the value can be said in three to four words (his written framework, not confirmed in the episode) [vid:d3FSoXHjEfs].

**Caveats.** Nikita says virality can be learned but durable retention in consumer social is a "black swan" that happens maybe once a decade [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY]. An instant aha gets you spread, not stickiness.

### 2. Invites Decay With Age

**What it is.** Invite loops work best with young, densely connected groups, and you seed them one dense group at a time. Nikita's data point: invitations per user fall about 20% for every year of age from 13 to 18, and adults "basically require paid acquisition" (self-reported) [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY]. TBH seeded one school at a time with a dedicated Instagram account and targeted ads, citing a leaked memo that people need to see a message about three times before downloading. He reports 40% of a school downloaded within 24 hours and peak installs of about 360K a day (self-reported) [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY].

**Why it works.** Adoption has to be synchronous inside one group, and every invite is a peer endorsement. Hunter Isaacson says a friend texting you an app "lowers effective CPI" [vid:DTree0unT_0].

**Examples.**
- NGL piggybacked on Instagram the day story links opened to everyone. The CTA on received messages is "get your own messages," not "download this app" [vid:DTree0unT_0].
- NGL's north-star metric was users who post the link *and* at least two replies. Hunter says the tight loop produced a high redownload rate that offset churn (self-reported 150M MAU) [vid:DTree0unT_0].
- Smaller-scale versions: Adam Lyttle's achievement images carry a logo watermark [vid:o_T4jXZSS3Q]. Macaron uses share codes that let a viewer import the exact mini-app from a video [vid:AagyxP9xoSw]. Duolingo temporarily tracked screenshots to find moments users already shared, then improved those moments, which Albert Cheng says can 5-10x sharing [vid:2BKmNmnEj9w].

**How to apply.** Add an invite or share screen at the point of delight. Make the CTA sell the result, and make the shared asset carry the path back into the app.

**Caveats.**
- Social apps have a cold-start problem. Two founders moved to utilities after failed social apps [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M][vid:CwHD6Fg-Mjs].
- iOS 18 partial contact access makes friend-finding harder [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY].
- Spammy invite SMS is unethical and now regulated. TBH never sent SMS on votes, and Gas had to rebuild its growth systems under new SMS rules [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY].

### 3. Hook, Demo, Then the Comments

**What it is.** The winning short-form unit is a hook about the viewer's problem followed by a fast demo. It should not look like an ad, and the sale often closes in the comments.

**Examples.**
- Steven Cravotta's best Puff Count video was a vape teardown that showed the app for about 2 seconds. It got 476K likes (self-reported) [vid:e_t1gYkvZkI].
- Kelechi Onyeama's Social Wizard clip opens on a "pause" image and shows the app generating a reply line. "They don't even need to finish the clip" [vid:9WWvLj-NqEE].
- Playkit's "long text" format often never names the app. Playkit calls it the highest-converting format because the reveal happens when people ask "what app is this?" [vid:gUL6q-FndRE][vid:9r4kS9zZj9s].
- Format conversion to install, as reported by the Flamme founder (self-reported): memes 0.05-0.2%, question slideshows 0.2-0.5%, straight demos 0.5-1%. A 100K-view demo beat a 2M-view meme [vid:pb4JIH8FubE].
- Nomadtable judges hooks by whether 75-80% of viewers watch past 3 seconds. Its "view trap" example got 300K views and 174 likes because the hook was clickbait [vid:RVnnqTrSKZY].

**How to apply.**
- Sell the transformation, not features [vid:e_t1gYkvZkI].
- Give the video one job: send people to the store listing [vid:e_t1gYkvZkI].
- Reply to top comments with product-heavy reply videos [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s][vid:q4pSLc13OTs].
- Match the store screenshots to the exact words in the video [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s].

**Caveats.** Formats die as audiences learn to spot them. Playkit's Julia says plain hook-and-demo is fading in favour of stories, series and slideshows [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s].

### 4. Volume Is the Strategy

**What it is.** Hits come from posting at high volume with fixed rules, not from genius.

**Examples and cadence.**
- Playkit's ritual: warm a fresh account for 2-3 days by searching what your user searches. Scroll and engage for about 2 minutes before each post. Post 2-3 times a day, at least 2 hours apart [vid:gUL6q-FndRE].
- Playkit's typical curve (self-reported): low thousands of views in weeks 1-2, the first 10K+ video in week 3, multiple 1M+ videos by week 8 [vid:gUL6q-FndRE].
- In one Playkit campaign, 1% of videos drove about 70% of views [vid:gUL6q-FndRE].
- Cal AI (Sebastian Stef's analysis of public data): about 1,000 posts in 12 months, 2.6 reels a day, most under 2M views [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc].
- Kelechi starts at 3 videos a day across 2 TikTok accounts and scales to 20 a day [vid:9WWvLj-NqEE].
- Lyttle's floor math: about 200 targeted viewers per video at 1% conversion is 2 downloads per video [vid:o_T4jXZSS3Q].

**Why it works.** TikTok tests every post on a small cohort, so each post is a cheap experiment. Flamme's founder puts it as 1 video a day being 30 data points a month versus 6,000 at 200 a day [vid:pb4JIH8FubE].

**Caveats.** The extreme version is a phone farm: 20-30 accounts on separate iPhones with their own Apple IDs and proxies [vid:pb4JIH8FubE]. It breaks the spirit of the platform's rules. Playkit's own newsletter reports account-level restrictions since about March/April 2026 [vid:gUL6q-FndRE]. Adam Lyttle admits he tried daily posting, quit in the zero-traction phase, and it "hasn't worked for me" [vid:HjnXnssrj4M].

### 5. Pay for Views, Not Followers

**What it is.** Pay small, audience-matched creators on terms tied to views, and copy proven formats across many of them.

**Pay rates in the notes (all self-reported).**
- Playkit: $125-175 a week for 15 posts, plus bonuses of $100 at 100K views, $500 at 500K and $1,000 at 1M [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s].
- Nomadtable: $1-2 CPM across 60-70 creators. One creator earned $8,000 for an 8M-view video [vid:RVnnqTrSKZY].
- Kelechi: about $120 to a micro-streamer for one video that hit 2M views [vid:9WWvLj-NqEE].
- RizzGPT: two creator promos at about $50 each, credited with 200K downloads. This is disputed and best treated as anecdote [vid:mGMDyPTR7QU].
- Cal AI's approach differs: flat upfront fees, judged by RPM against CPM. RPM is $5-20 per 1,000 views, with 250+ influencers and six figures a month [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].

**How to apply.**
- "Followers don't matter at all. It's only the views they get" [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].
- Test with your single ideal-fit creator first. Zach's rule: spend $100, make $1,000, keep going. If it fails with that creator, "it's not going to work with anyone" [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].
- Recruit creators who reach your audience, not your topic. Kelechi used Fortnite streamers for a dating app [vid:9WWvLj-NqEE].
- Expect about 100 outreach messages per yes [vid:9WWvLj-NqEE]. Zach describes about 100 DMs a day for weeks [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].
- Make content yourself first. Then hand only validated formats to creators [vid:RVnnqTrSKZY][vid:9WWvLj-NqEE].

**Caveats.** Zach says some 10K-follower creators ask "absurd" rates, such as $10K for 1,000 views, and that most influencers refuse CPM-only deals [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].

### 6. The Margin Ladder: UGC, Influencers, Paid

**What it is.** Each channel keeps a different share of the revenue it brings in. Blake Anderson's estimates (opinion, from running three apps) are internal UGC 50-80% margin, influencer marketing 25-70% and paid ads 0-30% [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA].

**Supporting numbers (all self-reported).** Playkit reports its average CPM at $3.87, against about $20 on Meta and about $119 for micro-influencers [vid:gUL6q-FndRE]. It also cites paid Meta installs at $2-5 each at the early stage [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s].

**The usual sequence.** Find winners organically, then put paid spend behind them. Start ads only after about 20 good organic pieces exist, and optimise for trial or subscription events rather than installs [vid:q4pSLc13OTs]. Flamme's founder: "Organic first, then UGC, then paid. Most founders run it backwards" [vid:pb4JIH8FubE].

**The third-party picture.** CNBC-reviewed documents put Cal AI at about $1.4M a month gross profit, about $770K a month on marketing and about $274K a month net operating income [vid:OsN_MJopMxc]. Scale is real, and so is the cost of buying it.

### 7. CPI Below LTV, or Nothing

**What it is.** Every paid channel reduces to one inequality: cost per install below lifetime value per install. Cravotta reads "realized LTV per customer" in RevenueCat with an unbounded lifetime. For Puff Count that comes to about $1.00-1.50 per install, so any CPI under about $1 is profitable (self-reported) [vid:e_t1gYkvZkI][vid:q4pSLc13OTs].

**Why monetisation decides marketing.** "The higher your LTV, the more you can spend on paid ads" [vid:PX30Fb8TlcE]. Paywall split tests can raise revenue per download 1.5-2x [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA]. Cal AI chose a hard paywall after the freemium Grind Clock got downloads but little revenue [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc].

**What fails (all self-reported).**
- Lyttle spent $500 on a UGC rewards test: 131K views, about 100 downloads and 8 trials. Much of the budget went to a bot-driven account [vid:QP4-qJQqER0].
- Flamme got 50M organic views and about 150K users. Its true paid conversion was only 0.3-0.5%, and the founder says more than half the views were "wasted" [vid:pb4JIH8FubE].
- Yorby reached 10K users and about $4K in revenue [vid:PZylEWhBDqo].
- Nomadtable says paid ads do not work for low-ARPU social apps [vid:RVnnqTrSKZY].
- At the top end, Nikita estimates reaching #1 in the US now takes about 80K-300K installs a day [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY].

**How to apply.** Measure realized LTV per install before you buy a single install. Treat views as an "ego metric" [vid:pb4JIH8FubE].

### 8. Reddit: Story First, Link Last

**What it is.** Value-first story posts in niche subreddits, with the product mentioned in passing.

**Examples (all self-reported).**
- Diego Roshardt took AppAlchemy to $17K MRR in about 4 months from Reddit alone, with under 100 X followers. He found about 30 subreddits using Reddit's ads targeting tool without spending anything, and cross-posted to 10+ of them. He posted 2-3 times a week to stay under spam thresholds [vid:BaWUPamqWlA].
- Roman (Goji Berry) warms a new account for 7-14 days with comments only. His post "I paid five influencers... here's what $1,250 got me" drew 160K views, about 2,000 visitors and 10-15 customers [vid:pvjalHFNM9Q].
- One YC founder got about 1,000 waitlist signups in 24 hours from a Reddit post [vid:NZp5j5hvn9I].

**Caveats.**
- Pat Walls caps Reddit at the first 10-100 customers, not scale [vid:pvjalHFNM9Q].
- Both Reddit case studies are web SaaS, not consumer mobile [vid:BaWUPamqWlA][vid:pvjalHFNM9Q].
- Roman's 15-person upvote group is vote manipulation and a ban risk [vid:pvjalHFNM9Q].
- Lyttle's GIF-repost scheme ended with "getting found out" [vid:o_T4jXZSS3Q].

### 9. The Name Is the Keyword

**What it is.** App Store Optimisation (ASO), done as a slow, compounding search channel.

**Example.** HabitKit's founder says about 98% of his users come from store search, at $50K+ a month across four apps (self-reported) [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI]. His rules:
- Name and subtitle matter most, "nothing else comes close." Put the primary keyword first ("Habit Tracker - Habit Kit").
- Use the 30-character subtitle for secondary keywords and never repeat a word between name and subtitle.
- Fill all 100 keyword characters, with no spaces and no competitor names.
- Make the first screenshot your most striking real UI. His designer-made screenshots lost an Apple Product Page Optimization test to his own originals.
- Ask for a review at a happy moment, such as right after the first habit is completed, never during onboarding [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI].

**More examples (all self-reported).**
- Lyttle renamed a fasting app to target the easier "fast tracker" keyword, and downloads went from about 50 to 861 a month [vid:o_T4jXZSS3Q].
- Rootd's founder reuses the words users write in reviews as keywords [vid:VfNRd5Rk0cM].
- Kaching says about 95% of its reviews come from customer support (Shopify App Store, not iOS) [vid:vLLBzUZr6-s].
- About half of Umax downloads came from App Store featuring or algorithmic promotion (secondary recap) [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA].

**How to apply.** Do keyword research before you name the app [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI]. Have the listing ready for Apple's roughly two-week launch boost [vid:MnF-zJhyUtE].

**Caveats.**
- ASO is slow: HabitKit took about 6 months to reach the UK/German top 10 and nearly 3 years to hold the US top 5 [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI].
- Lyttle now says the "golden age" of ASO is over [vid:HjnXnssrj4M].
- Cal AI's rating prompt before the paywall is a gray area under Apple's guidelines [vid:PX30Fb8TlcE].

### 10. Distribution Before Code

**What it is.** Line up an audience or a working channel before, or while, you build.

**Examples (all self-reported).**
- Nomadtable built a waitlist of 12-15K, which produced about 6,000 day-one downloads [vid:RVnnqTrSKZY].
- Cal AI ran a $2,000 social test before the founders committed (CNBC article) [vid:OsN_MJopMxc].
- Kaching posted an unbuilt design screenshot in merchant groups and got 100+ asks for a download link [vid:vLLBzUZr6-s].
- Ben Guez launched a concept page that did not feature him, to test demand separately from his ~1M personal followers [vid:KgS3hbpxTzE].
- Build in public: Andrew Codesmith suggests spending 20-30% of build time on content [vid:7u6b52RaQSc]. Peek's founder replied to a viral tweet with a joke feature, then built it overnight [vid:j2w4y7pDi8w].
- Nikita geofenced TBH's launch to keep growth controlled and servers up [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY].

**Caveats.**
- Zach warns that a friend's mock-up TikToks got millions of views, then lost the hype and spawned competitors before the app shipped. If you pre-validate, be ready to ship immediately [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].
- Product Hunt is "a bit meh" for downloads [vid:o_T4jXZSS3Q], although Peek reports reaching #1 there [vid:j2w4y7pDi8w].
- Most YC founders' first customers came from their own network [vid:NZp5j5hvn9I].

### 11. Fish Where Money Already Swims

**What it is.** Pick niches where people already spend money and already make content.

**Examples.**
- Blake Anderson: "When you find a niche that's heavily monetized with physical products, that's a good indicator that the niche converts." Looksmaxxing became Umax [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA].
- Cravotta only studies apps above $50K a month in Sensor Tower [vid:q4pSLc13OTs]. He treats paying competitors as a green flag and controversial topics like vaping as a content advantage [vid:z_fARFqjLoY].
- Nikita reduces downloads to a few motives (a mate, money, escape) and looks for "latent demand" in clunky existing behaviour [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY].
- Kelechi's first check: does the niche already have a large volume of content [vid:9WWvLj-NqEE]?

**Caveats.** Julia says organic UGC "does not work" for B2B SaaS or crypto, and men over 50 are hard to reach with creators [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s]. Playkit also says UGC needs a visual hook (scanning, swiping, transformation) [vid:gUL6q-FndRE].

### Where sources disagree

- **Organic only, or organic then paid.** Lyttle's 2026 view is that indie growth is daily organic content with no ads [vid:HjnXnssrj4M], and Nomadtable rejects paid for social apps [vid:RVnnqTrSKZY]. Cravotta says paid ads on organic winners are where scale comes from [vid:e_t1gYkvZkI]. Blake rates paid as the lowest-margin channel [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA].
- **Is ASO dead?** Lyttle says its golden age is over [vid:HjnXnssrj4M]. HabitKit reports 98% search traffic [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI].
- **Flat fee or CPM.** Cal AI pays upfront and says creators refuse CPM-only deals [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M]. Nomadtable and Macaron pay on performance [vid:RVnnqTrSKZY][vid:AagyxP9xoSw].
- **Is hook-and-demo still alive?** Playkit says it is fading [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s]. Flamme's data ranks demos as the best-converting format [vid:pb4JIH8FubE].
- **AI faces versus authenticity.** Yorby says the algorithm does not care whether content is AI-made [vid:PZylEWhBDqo]. Playkit insists viewers must not detect an ad [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s].
- **Skill or luck.** Kelechi calls distribution "deterministic" [vid:9WWvLj-NqEE], and Nikita says virality can be learned [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY]. YC founders describe viral launches as "lightning in a bottle" [vid:NZp5j5hvn9I].

### Checklist

1. Cut the app to one feature whose aha fits in a 2-second clip [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].
2. Add a share or invite screen whose CTA sells the result ("get your own messages") [vid:DTree0unT_0].
3. Do keyword research before naming the app, and put the primary keyword first in the name [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI].
4. Ask for reviews at a happy moment, never in onboarding [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI].
5. Warm a fresh TikTok account for 2-3 days, then post 2-3 times a day, 2+ hours apart, for 8 weeks [vid:gUL6q-FndRE].
6. Track watch-past-3-seconds (target 75-80%) and installs, not views [vid:RVnnqTrSKZY].
7. Prefer demo formats, which convert 0.5-1% versus 0.05-0.2% for memes [vid:pb4JIH8FubE].
8. Test one ideal-fit creator with $100 and continue only if it returns $1,000 [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].
9. Pay small creators a weekly base plus view bonuses, or $1-2 CPM [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s][vid:RVnnqTrSKZY].
10. Read realized LTV per install in RevenueCat and buy installs only below it [vid:e_t1gYkvZkI].
11. Turn on paid ads only after about 20 proven organic videos, optimised for trials [vid:q4pSLc13OTs].
12. Write value-first Reddit posts for your first 10-100 users, 2-3 times a week [vid:BaWUPamqWlA][vid:pvjalHFNM9Q].
