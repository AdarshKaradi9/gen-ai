## Applied Playbook: the nutrient habit tracker

This section applies the patterns above to one specific product. The app is a daily habit tracker where the habits are nutrients. The user declares which essential nutrients they paid attention to today and optionally tags the food they used. A body map lights up the organs and systems those nutrients support. It never measures, recommends or diagnoses. Fact cards quote published reference intakes with sources. A weekly heatmap shows which rows stay blank. Streaks are per nutrient, with freezes. Free covers the grid, tagging and seven days of history. A paid annual plan unlocks full history, the heatmap, export to a nutritionist, and themes.

### 1. The aha: the first tap lights the body

The product's aha must fit in a two-second clip [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M]. For this app, that clip is a finger tapping "Iron" and the blood glowing on the body map. Everything in onboarding exists to get the user to that tap fast.

- First screen after launch: "Which nutrients do you want to pay attention to?" with sensible defaults already on the grid. No account yet [vid:Ug_EMizZaGk].
- Second screen: "Tap one you covered today." The body lights up. This is the aha, inside thirty seconds [vid:bhnfZhJWCWY][vid:Aa89MC8jX2c].
- Only then ask for an account, framed as saving today's map [vid:Qsq-Sj_rojU].

### 2. Onboarding questions that visibly pay back

Ask five to seven questions, broad first, hardest last [vid:QMo2T5apdYw][vid:MZDvGzxr3gY]. Each one must change something the user can see.

- Diet pattern, such as vegetarian, vegan, or mixed. This reorders the grid so B12, iron and omega-3 surface first for plant-based eaters.
- Region. This picks the reference-intake source the fact cards quote.
- What made them start, such as low energy, a doctor's comment, or curiosity. This chooses which body region the first-week summary highlights.
- "Where did you hear about us?" This costs nothing and tells you which channel works [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc].

End with a goal screen: "Commit to touching 5 nutrients a day," with a "Commit to my goal" button and an easy opt-out, not a preselected default [vid:_CCwoQZH5hI].

The notification ask comes on its own custom screen that previews the actual reminder, before the system prompt [vid:Qsq-Sj_rojU][vid:Xvjxr0lkcjE].

### 3. The reward loop: body map, streaks, day-seven insight

**Define the unit.** A "day" counts when the user touches at least their goal number of nutrients. Per-nutrient streaks sit underneath as a second layer [vid:_CCwoQZH5hI].

**Bend, don't break.** Start every user with two streak freezes and an earn-back path that requires a real check-in [vid:_CCwoQZH5hI].

**Reminder timing.** Send the daily nudge about 23.5 hours after the last check-in, and one late-evening saver only when the streak is at risk. Slow down for users who ignore reminders [vid:_CCwoQZH5hI][vid:9KqrnBiyBQ8].

**Win the first week.** The day-seven summary is the single most important screen after the aha. "This week you lit up muscles six days and bones once." It turns novelty into insight before novelty fades [vid:_CCwoQZH5hI][vid:VNxBZ7ka5J0]. Hold any new mechanics until after day seven.

**One central measure.** Do not add badges for everything. The body map is the one progress measure, and the heatmap is its history [vid:yBpv5rZoBjA][vid:LXX_qOA5D8E].

**A character worth missing.** Consider a small companion that lives in the body map, brighter when attended to and quieter when ignored. Emotional stakes retain better than numbers [vid:pb4JIH8FubE][vid:yBpv5rZoBjA]. Keep the copy encouraging on missed days, never shaming [vid:2BKmNmnEj9w].

**Stay white hat.** Every mechanic should pass one test: does it help the user actually pay attention to their food, or just keep them tapping [vid:v5Qjuegtiyc][vid:LXX_qOA5D8E]? For a health app, this line matters more than usual.

### 4. Feel: the tap has to feel good

- A light haptic and a short glow animation on every tap, and a heavier celebration at streak milestones [vid:8mMH6Pq8qnE][vid:_CCwoQZH5hI].
- Progress starts above zero. Pre-light one region from onboarding answers so the first map is never empty [vid:2TlIg3VokY8].
- One font, at most six sizes, one icon style, consistent spacing [vid:EcbgbKtOELY][vid:AH_ugxmLeUM].
- Illustrated empty states, never blank text [vid:8mMH6Pq8qnE].

### 5. The paywall: soft, honest, after value

This app grows by word of mouth and content, not paid ads, which points to freemium or a soft onboarding wall rather than a hard paywall [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc][vid:DTree0unT_0].

- Show the paywall at the end of onboarding, right after the first lit-up map, because most trials start on install day. Make it dismissible [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI][vid:9ypqs_2fAl8].
- Show it again at the day-seven summary, which is the moment the heatmap and full history become obviously valuable.
- Two or three screens: what your month will look like, then "we'll remind you before the trial ends," then the price [vid:24QMRDXck70][vid:PX30Fb8TlcE].
- Two plans, annual preselected and framed per week, with others behind "view all plans" [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8].
- "No commitment, cancel anytime" and a one-line statement of exactly when the charge happens [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8][vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].
- One moderate offer of 25 to 33 percent off when the user closes the paywall, nothing deeper [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8].
- No trial toggle, which Apple now rejects, and generous refunds to protect the rating [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8][vid:vLLBzUZr6-s].
- Wire up RevenueCat or Superwall from the start so paywalls can change without an app update [vid:RGT7PX4TQ8w][vid:i5UqNMZUUKM].

### 6. The shareable moment

The body map is the screenshot. Design for it on purpose.

- Watch which screens users capture in the first few weeks, then build a square, watermarked share card of the weekly body map [vid:2BKmNmnEj9w][vid:o_T4jXZSS3Q].
- The share card's call to action sells the result, not the app, such as "see your own body map" [vid:DTree0unT_0].
- Ask for an App Store review right after the first completed week, never during onboarding [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI].
- The first App Store screenshot is the lit-up body, not a feature list [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI].

### 7. Distribution: where the first thousand users come from

- **Name for search.** Do keyword research before naming the app, and put the main keyword first [vid:I2GG0lyb_RI].
- **Short-form video.** A fresh account, warmed for a few days, then two to three demo posts a day for eight weeks. Each post is a problem hook such as "I'm vegetarian and had no idea I never touch B12," followed by a fast demo of the map lighting up [vid:gUL6q-FndRE][vid:pb4JIH8FubE]. Track watch-past-three-seconds and installs, not views [vid:RVnnqTrSKZY].
- **Communities.** Value-first posts in vegetarian, vegan, Indian home-cooking and fitness communities, two to three times a week, for the first hundred users [vid:BaWUPamqWlA][vid:pvjalHFNM9Q].
- **Creators.** Test one well-matched creator with a small budget, and continue only if it returns many times that [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M]. Pay small creators a weekly base plus view bonuses [vid:9r4kS9zZj9s].
- **Paid ads.** Only after about twenty organic videos have proven which hooks work, and only while cost per install stays below realized value per install [vid:q4pSLc13OTs][vid:e_t1gYkvZkI].
- **Search pages.** A fact page per nutrient with cited reference intakes is the same playbook as the comparison pages that grew your friend's exam-prep apps. This last point is our own extension, not something the videos tested.

### 8. Build plan

- Write the data model as sample JSON before the first prompt to any coding tool. Nutrients, tags, daily entries and streaks [vid:CwHD6Fg-Mjs].
- Build a wall of about twenty competitor onboarding screens before designing your own [vid:CwHD6Fg-Mjs].
- Popular stack, commit after every working step, API keys behind your own backend [vid:Tw18-4U7mts][vid:9atd5lczG2k].
- Time-box the first version to three or four weeks and cut scope when you slip [vid:1hHMwLxN6EM][vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].
- Analytics and a feedback board before launch. Launch publicly only when beta users stick for several days [vid:MnF-zJhyUtE].
- Answer support yourself. Every ticket is a roadmap item [vid:sz_LgBAGYyo].

### 9. The one metric

Define "active" as completing the daily check-in, then chart weekly cohort curves and watch for them to flatten [vid:VNxBZ7ka5J0]. Health and fitness apps typically keep only a few percent of users at day thirty, so the bar is low and the signal is clear.

The success test from the original idea still holds. Are you, and twenty strangers, still opening it on day thirty? If the curve flattens there, the body map outlives its novelty and the app deserves real time and money. If it doesn't, the day-seven summary and the reward loop are the first places to change.
