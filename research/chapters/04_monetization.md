## What Makes Them Pay: paywalls, pricing and conversion

"Everybody that I know that makes a lot of money on apps uses subscriptions," says the founder behind RizzGPT, Umax and Cal AI [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA]. A paywall consultant calls the paywall "the only part of the app that makes money" [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8].

A warning about evidence. Most numbers here come from paywall tooling vendors (Superwall, RevenueCat, Adapty) or from founders describing their own apps. The big "studies" are pattern catalogues, not controlled research. Mobbin's 2,995-paywall video and Superwall's 4,000-paywall interview feature the same expert, Jonathan Parra [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8][vid:lkwX_kc0NS8]. Tim Gabe's "10,000 paywall screens" video takes its headline statistics from RevenueCat and Adapty reports [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI]. DesignerUp's 300-paywall review has no conversion data [vid:WvOhpkAcHno].

### 1. Pick Your Wall On Purpose

**What it is.** Nik Zechner reviewed 100+ top-grossing iOS apps and argues they all use one of three designs: freemium, hard paywall, or onboarding wall (a paywall at the end of onboarding) [vid:qVRQIKFpkdQ]. His verdict on which converts best did not survive in written sources, so treat the taxonomy as the useful part.

**Why it matters.** Cal AI moved to a hard paywall (billing before any use, no free tier) after the founders' earlier freemium app, Grind Clock, got downloads but little revenue. The hard wall filters for high-value users, which makes paid ads affordable [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc]. Steven Cravotta's study of 100 viral AI apps sums up the winners as "extensive onboarding, hard paywall, one core feature" [vid:PX30Fb8TlcE].

**The freemium counter-case.** NGL kept a huge free user base and monetized "a very small percentage" with a $1 to $7 weekly subscription, priced by country GDP per capita, for multi-eight-figure revenue (self-reported) [vid:DTree0unT_0]. Duolingo keeps about 91% of users free; roughly 9% pay and subscriptions make about 80% of revenue (WSJ reporting) [vid:9KqrnBiyBQ8]. Albert Cheng reports that showing a capped sample of paid suggestions to free Grammarly users "nearly doubled" upgrades [vid:2BKmNmnEj9w]. These products have huge audiences. A small utility with freemium can stall: one Chrome extension with verified revenue shows about 36 subscribers from 6,700 to 8,000 users, around 0.5% [vid:xLwgYzdOD4A].

**How to apply.** If you will grow through paid acquisition, use an onboarding wall or a hard paywall so revenue per install is high enough to buy installs. If you grow through word of mouth and daily use, keep the core free and sample premium inside it. Mobbin's onboarding study found only 22% of 986 apps show a paywall during onboarding, so the onboarding wall is common among top grossers but far from universal [vid:Qsq-Sj_rojU].

### 2. You Have One Session

**What it is.** Most paying decisions happen on install day. Gabe cites RevenueCat (82% of trial starts on install day) and Adapty (89.4% on day 0) [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI]. Adam Lyttle says 70%+ of his trial signups happen right after onboarding [vid:_Fiwx6lm5YU], and that "most users sign up for the free trial immediately after onboarding, before using any features" [vid:dKif3Eo3frk]. Connor Burd puts it this way: "90% of users will probably only ever see your onboarding if you have a paywalled section" [vid:CwHD6Fg-Mjs].

**How to apply.** Put a paywall at the end of onboarding, then cover the other moments too. Lyttle names three best-converting placements: after onboarding, when the user taps the core action, and on relaunch [vid:dKif3Eo3frk]. Vahe Baghdasaryan adds a once-per-day session-start offer, a paywall after every X actions (for example after 5 scans), near-limit reminders, and a transaction-abandonment paywall [vid:24QMRDXck70]. Cal AI reportedly runs 46 trigger points; 87% of new users see a paywall (Superwall case figures) [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc].

**Caveats.** "Even if you have a well-designed paywall, if it appears at the wrong moment, it's still a bad paywall" [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. DesignerUp argues for contextual gates that appear exactly where the locked value would appear, and names the common failure as gating "before the user understands why the feature matters" [vid:WvOhpkAcHno]. Lyttle's "pattern interrupt" (a forced 3-second wait on free actions) produces subscribers [vid:dKif3Eo3frk], but Vox calls the same mechanic in free games manipulation [vid:fKK9nVLvhGM]. In a habit app, deliberate annoyance fights retention.

### 3. Sell The Future First

**What it is.** A paywall works best as the last step of a story, not a wall. "A paywall isn't just a screen. It's a flow" [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8].

**Examples.** Opal spent onboarding on "getting 8 years of your life back," and showing users the years they could save moved trial signups from 7% to 17% (company case relayed by Mobbin) [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. Parra says multi-page paywalls "almost always do better" than single pages [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]; Baghdasaryan claims 20 to 40% higher conversion for 2 to 3 screen paywalls (self-reported) [vid:24QMRDXck70]. His three-screen paywall for the Claim app (visuals and "Try Claim for free," then a reminder promise, then a timeline with CTAs) hit a 34% trial start rate against a 15% benchmark (self-reported) [vid:24QMRDXck70]. Cal AI runs the same three steps: "We want you to try Cal AI for free" with "Try now for $0," then "We'll send you a reminder before your free trial ends," then the real paywall [vid:PX30Fb8TlcE].

**Why it works.** In Parra's "closed loop" idea, the best converters show who you are now and who you will become [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8]. Habit apps have this material built in.

**How to apply.** End onboarding with "your plan is ready," one emotional outcome screen, then the price [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8].

### 4. Say Exactly When You Charge

**What it is.** Transparency about the trial raises conversion. "Your paywall is a trust screen as much as it is a pricing screen" [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].

**Examples.** Blinkist added one front-and-center line on when and what users are charged: +23% conversion, -55% complaints, no change in churn (company-reported, relayed) [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI]. Its trial-timeline paywall also raised push opt-ins [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. Parra's cheapest reliable tweak is the subtitle "No commitment, cancel anytime" under the CTA; with simplified plan names it lifted conversion 10% (Superwall A/B, self-reported) [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8]. Lyttle points to an honesty screen in Cal AI's onboarding ("it takes time to build momentum") that he says reduces refunds [vid:QMo2T5apdYw].

**The cost of hiding it.** Real user reviews of a calorie app include being "charged a full year after cancelling a 3-day trial with no support reply" [vid:SI8T1yvmoRs].

**Caveat.** Baghdasaryan deliberately leaves "3-day" off the plan card, because it triggers "what if I forget to cancel?", while keeping the timeline visible elsewhere on the screen [vid:24QMRDXck70]. He also repeats "free trial" 5 to 7 times across screens as "risk reversal through repetition" [vid:24QMRDXck70].

### 5. Shape The Trial, Not Just Offer One

**What it is.** The trial is often the biggest lever. Lyttle spent a year with almost no payers; "the free trial was the game-changer, not the subscription model or the pricing" (self-reported) [vid:dKif3Eo3frk]. Gabe cites vendor data putting a weekly plan's 12-month LTV at $7.40 without a trial versus $54.50 with one [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].

**Length and placement.** Headspace tested 7, 14 and 30 days; the 14-day trial on the annual plan won [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. Many winners put the trial only on the annual plan: Moonly [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8], Reframe at $99/year, Lazy Fit at $40/year and Cal AI at $30/year with a 3-day trial [vid:PX30Fb8TlcE]. Short trials cancel fast: 55% of 3-day-trial cancellations happen on day 0 (Adapty, via Gabe) [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].

**Friction can help.** Outsider required card details to start a trial: signups fell by more than half, conversion rose 5x, and paying customers more than doubled (company case via Mobbin) [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. In the other direction, Slopes replaced its paywall with a single "redeem your free week" action and got +25% trial starts [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8].

**The toggle.** Baghdasaryan's trial toggle for Rizz offered $9.99/week with a 7-day trial or $6.99/week paid now; he says about 10% chose to pay upfront, helping ad payback (self-reported) [vid:24QMRDXck70]. That pattern is now a platform risk (see pattern 12).

### 6. Weekly Is The New Monthly (Except When It Isn't)

**What it is.** Gabe's headline claim, from RevenueCat and Adapty data: weekly plans grew from 43% to 55.6% of subscription revenue in two years while monthly fell from 21.1% to 11.7%, and weekly converts 1.7 to 7.4x better than annual across price tiers [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI]. "$7 a week feels like nothing," where $38 to $400 a year does not; upsell to annual later [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].

**Examples.** Social Wizard priced about $10/week, $20/month, $80/year, so monthly is only 2x weekly (self-reported) [vid:9WWvLj-NqEE]. Lyttle's portfolio used weekly plans around $4.99 to $7.99 or annual around $29.99 [vid:3sLP2bcD5Xg]. NGL's weekly plan is a "low impulse buy" [vid:DTree0unT_0].

**Counter-evidence.** Many of the biggest apps push annual. Cal AI says 95% of its subscriptions are yearly; its A/B winner was $10/month or $30/year with a 3-day trial, and $70/year converted fewer people and made less per download (self-reported) [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M]. Canary sells $10/month or $79/year and gets 60% on annual by promoting it [vid:KgS3hbpxTzE]. Parra says default to yearly for the highest LTV [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. The catch with annual: Cal AI could not measure churn at recording because no yearly subscriber had renewed yet [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].

**How to apply.** Show two plans, annual preselected plus a weekly or monthly option, and hide the rest behind "view all plans" [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8][vid:lkwX_kc0NS8]. Then test weekly-first against annual-first in your own category.

### 7. Anchor, Reframe, Then Ask

**What it is.** The same price presented differently converts differently. "The offer didn't change. The way it was presented did": Tipstop emphasized the trial, added a discount badge and simplified wording, and direct conversions almost tripled (company case via Mobbin) [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8].

**Examples.** Tide breaks price into weekly amounts; Ahead compares the subscription to coffee or therapy [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. Parra reframes $39/year as "$0.76/week" [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8]. Cal AI shows monthly at $10 ($120/year) so the annual plan reads as an 80 to 90% discount [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc]. Quittr shows annual as $3.33/month, and Baghdasaryan argues "random" prices feel more authentic than .99 endings [vid:24QMRDXck70]. uxpeak's rule: never show a price in isolation, because "the first number seen becomes the ruler" [vid:2TlIg3VokY8].

**Structure beats the number.** Adapty's ranking, via Gabe: localization (language and currency) +62.3% LTV, trial structure +59.6%, and plan-count changes drive 63% more conversion uplift than any price test [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].

**Price level is disputed.** Gabe cites RevenueCat medians of 2.8% download-to-paid for high-price apps versus 2% for mid-price, with top-quartile high-price apps at 6.1%, and warns "the bottom is exactly where conversion goes to die" [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI]. He admits this may be correlation. Blake Anderson deliberately chose low prices for more users and better word of mouth [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA]. Krithika Shankarraman calls competing on cheapness a race to the bottom [vid:QaDsk4iH1aw].

### 8. The Polite Second Chance

**What it is.** A follow-up offer when the user closes the paywall.

**Examples.** Parra's ladder: if the user closes an annual offer, show a drawer ("Not ready to commit for a year?") with the per-week reframe, then a one-time offer at 25 to 33% off. Save 50%+ for Black Friday so the brand is not cheapened. He calls this "gray hat" himself [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8]. A simpler exit-intent sheet offers the monthly plan instead [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. Cal AI offers decliners a one-time $20/year downsell [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc]. Baghdasaryan discounts only the annual plan on abandonment, never weekly or monthly, with 80% off with trial or 90% off without [vid:24QMRDXck70]. His spin-the-wheel reveal reportedly beat a static "80% off" by double digits (self-reported) [vid:24QMRDXck70].

**Caveats.** Users are learning the trick: "more and more users are getting comfortable closing paywalls because they know they're going to get some kind of offer afterwards" [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. For Apple compliance he suggests a larger trial instead of a deeper discount [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. Seibel's alternative is a time-boxed discount tied to a real reason, not discounting out of fear [vid:C27RVio2rOs].

### 9. Ugly Can Win

**What it is.** Clean, familiar and simple often beats beautiful and detailed. "There is literally no universal best paywall, only better experiments" [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8].

**Examples.** A stripped-down paywall (big image, headline, Continue) beat a detailed comparison chart by 111%; native SwiftUI-style paywalls beat custom designs "by quite a long shot" at Photoroom (Superwall, self-reported) [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8]. An unnamed AI girlfriend app's ugly, text-heavy squeeze page beat everything Parra designed [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. His baseline: headline, 3 to 5 benefit bullets, social proof, one big CTA, which gets you "roughly 80% there" [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8].

DesignerUp's six types (Access, Free Trial, Feature, Usage, Upgrade, Add-on) are a useful map for in-app gates, but they are design judgments without test data [vid:WvOhpkAcHno].

**Caveat.** Parra praises comparison tables in one video [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8] but says elsewhere they rarely beat bullets [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8]. Baghdasaryan warns that copying a famous paywall fails because intent differs by category [vid:24QMRDXck70].

### 10. Fifteen Tests A Year

**What it is.** The apps that make the most run the most paywall experiments. Gabe cites a vendor figure that apps running 50+ paywall experiments a year earn 18.7x those running one, and top performers average about 15 a year [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI]. Most founders, he says, only test $9.99 against $12.99 for three weeks [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].

**Results reported.** Cal AI: 123 experiments, 424 variants, 61 on the onboarding paywall alone, trial-to-paid +31% (Superwall case figures) [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc]; an onboarding-animation test lifted conversion about 10% [vid:OsN_MJopMxc]. An unnamed entertainment app went from 1.1% to about 4% install-to-paid in five months with no product or UA changes (self-reported) [vid:24QMRDXck70]. Puff Count went from $3K to $30K MRR through Superwall testing (third-party account) [vid:RGT7PX4TQ8w]. Lyttle copied the structure of an app making about $300K/month and saw +230% trial subscriptions; his portfolio went from about $10K to $50K a month (self-reported) [vid:3sLP2bcD5Xg]. Blake Anderson says split-testing offers and placements takes revenue per download to "1.5 or 2X" [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA].

**Tooling.** Superwall changes paywalls remotely without App Store updates [vid:RGT7PX4TQ8w]. RevenueCat handles cross-platform purchases and remotely managed offerings [vid:i5UqNMZUUKM]; one solo founder pays about $260/month for it [vid:KgS3hbpxTzE].

**Caveats.** Expect most tests to lose: Cheng puts a healthy win rate at 30 to 50% [vid:2BKmNmnEj9w]. Judge on retention and LTV, not trial starts [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. One Superwall guest saw 15% apparent paid conversion that cleaned up to 0.3 to 0.5% once renewals were separated from new signups [vid:pb4JIH8FubE]. Nearly all success figures here come from vendors selling testing tools.

### 11. Know Your Real Margin

**What it is.** Gross revenue is not what you keep.

**Apple's cut.** Apple takes 15% up to the first $1M, then 30%, which Anderson says is effectively about 20% and 33% [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA]. AI backend costs are "sub 3%" unless you do image generation or advanced voice [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA]. Cal AI's CNBC-reviewed documents show about $1.4M/month gross profit after store fees, about $274K/month net operating income, and about $770K/month on marketing [vid:OsN_MJopMxc]; one teardown estimates about 50% margin [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc].

**Margin by channel.** Anderson's estimates: internal UGC 50 to 80%, influencer marketing 25 to 70%, paid ads 0 to 30% [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA]. Organic-heavy founders report more: Kelechi Onyeama claims over 90% after Apple's cut (self-reported) [vid:9WWvLj-NqEE].

**Unit math.** Cravotta reads LTV per install from RevenueCat; Puff Count earned about $1.00 to $1.10 per install, so any CPI under about $1 was profitable [vid:e_t1gYkvZkI]. "The higher your LTV is, the more you can spend on paid ads" [vid:PX30Fb8TlcE].

**Platform.** Several founders say Android users do not pay: under a tenth of Cal AI's revenue [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M], and Anderson says Android "notoriously" does not convert [vid:mGMDyPTR7QU].

### 12. Refunds, Reviews And The Rulebook

**Refunds and reviews.** Kaching gives full refunds with no questions because "the risk of getting one star review is just too big" [vid:vLLBzUZr6-s]. Rootd treats its 4.8 rating as the most important metric [vid:VfNRd5Rk0cM]. Honest expectation-setting in onboarding reduces refunds, per Lyttle [vid:QMo2T5apdYw]. Cal AI asks for a rating inside onboarding before the paywall, which Cravotta calls "cheeky" and a gray area under Apple's guidance [vid:PX30Fb8TlcE].

**What platform policy now rejects.** In early 2026 Apple started rejecting paywalls with free-trial toggles judged confusing or misleading [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8], and began rejecting Lyttle's toggle pattern in January 2026 per a RevenueCat post [vid:dKif3Eo3frk]. A profile reports Lyttle's high-converting paywall style "started getting rejected by Apple" [vid:3sLP2bcD5Xg]. Parra recommends a bigger trial over a bigger discount to stay compliant [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8]. The notes are thin on exact guideline numbers and on Google Play policy; check Apple's current review guidelines before you ship any toggle, wheel or close-triggered offer.

### Where sources disagree

- **Hard wall or free first.** Cal AI and Cravotta say paywall early [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc][vid:PX30Fb8TlcE]; NGL, Duolingo and Cheng say monetize a small share of a big free base [vid:DTree0unT_0][vid:9KqrnBiyBQ8][vid:2BKmNmnEj9w]. Lyttle once advised "users first, revenue second," then reversed it [vid:_Fiwx6lm5YU]. Rootd reportedly raised revenue about 5x by moving the paywall to the start of onboarding (search snippet from a different interview) [vid:VfNRd5Rk0cM].
- **Weekly or annual.** Gabe says weekly first [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI]; Cal AI, Parra and Canary push annual [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M][vid:9ypqs_2fAl8][vid:KgS3hbpxTzE].
- **High or low price.** Gabe ties higher prices to better conversion [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI]; Anderson chose low prices for growth [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA]; Cal AI found $70/year lost to $30 [vid:qDuzzrQKP_M].
- **Show the trial length or hide it.** Gabe says state charge timing up front [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI]; Baghdasaryan hides it from the plan card [vid:24QMRDXck70].
- **Copy or don't.** Lyttle's 230% jump came from copying a top app [vid:3sLP2bcD5Xg]; Baghdasaryan says copying fails [vid:24QMRDXck70].

### Checklist

1. Decide hard wall, onboarding wall or freemium based on whether you grow by paid ads or word of mouth [vid:wMk_t8F1rOc][vid:DTree0unT_0].
2. Place a paywall at the end of onboarding, since most trials start on day 0 [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI][vid:_Fiwx6lm5YU].
3. Build a 2 to 3 screen paywall: outcome, reminder promise, then price [vid:24QMRDXck70][vid:PX30Fb8TlcE].
4. Add "No commitment, cancel anytime" and a one-line statement of when the charge happens [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8][vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].
5. Show two plans with annual preselected, others behind "view all plans" [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8].
6. Test the trial on the annual plan and a longer trial before testing price [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8][vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].
7. Localize paywall language and currency early [vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].
8. Add one moderate close-triggered offer (25 to 33% off annual); save deep discounts for Black Friday [vid:lkwX_kc0NS8].
9. Wire up Superwall or RevenueCat so you can change paywalls without an app update, and aim for about 15 tests a year [vid:RGT7PX4TQ8w][vid:i5UqNMZUUKM][vid:sYRhXB_ZcLI].
10. Track realized LTV per install and keep CPI below it [vid:e_t1gYkvZkI].
11. Budget for Apple's 15 to 30% cut and compare channel margins before scaling paid ads [vid:5u9u8yzPEpA].
12. Drop trial toggles and refund generously to protect your rating [vid:9ypqs_2fAl8][vid:vLLBzUZr6-s].
