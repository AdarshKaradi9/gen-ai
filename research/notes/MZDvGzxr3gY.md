## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Onboarding is the user's first experience and the sales pitch; it is what turns visitors into paying customers (self-reported: Puff Count ~$40K/30 days, ~$100K/90 days, $500K ARR at recording).
- Onboarding has three jobs: educate the user about the product, gather data (email, phone, demographics, problem severity) for product and marketing, and prime the user to think "I need this" before the paywall.
- Make it long on purpose: users should "build value for themselves" and invest time; dropping them straight onto a paywall ("hey welcome to my app, pay right now") converts nobody.
- The survey is the most important part: strategically ordered questions walk users through their own problem so they arrive at the paywall already motivated.
- Everything is measured and A/B tested (Mixpanel per-step funnel); decisions are data-driven, never guesses.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Puff Count flow in order: (1) "Congratulations, you're on the way to a healthier lifestyle" screen reaffirming the download decision; (2) short sign-in; (3) value screens explaining features; (4) social proof ("500,000+ users"); (5) user survey; (6) more social proof; (7) hard paywall where they decide to pay or not.
- Research step: browse competitor onboardings and subscription screens on Mobbin (examples he scrolls: Marcus, a mental-health journal app with a survey, Bumble); mirror the top apps in your category and the data they collect.
- Survey design: ask "What brings you to the app?" (quit vaping / understand my habit / not sure), side effects, how long you've vaped, how often you feel the desire, whether desire is increasing, which area of life is most affected, how much you spend on vaping. These both segment likely payers (heavy users pay, once-a-day users don't) and make the user dwell on the problem.
- He hired a behavioral scientist and lifted validated questions from National Drug Strategy surveys so the data is scientifically usable.
- Brain-dump all screens and questions into one Google Doc first, then design.
- Design via a 99designs contest (upload sketches and the doc, pay only the winning designer); the winner of his paywall contest, "Zach", was then hired for the whole onboarding.
- Optimize: Mixpanel funnel per screen; move or cut screens to maximize completion rate and conversion-to-paid; ~7% drop-off on a step is shown as normal.
- Put the hardest, most intense questions at the end of the survey: users have already committed time and are more likely to answer.
- Description links a free "app onboarding blueprint" doc and Superwall for paywall A/B testing.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Argues for deliberately long onboarding and a hard paywall, the opposite of Google's "fast, minimal input, register later" guidance (Xvjxr0lkcjE) and of uxpeak's reciprocity principle (give value before asking).
- Consistent with Mobbin's finding that successful apps average 25 screens, but Mobbin frames the goal as reaching the aha moment, while Cravotta frames it as sunk cost and priming to pay.
- Social proof "500,000+ users" is displayed before the user has seen any product; works for him but is unverifiable.
- Opens with "I have absolutely nothing to sell you in this video" yet links his coaching funnel and affiliate Superwall link in the description.
