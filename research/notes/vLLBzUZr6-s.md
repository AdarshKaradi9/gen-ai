## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Build on a platform marketplace (Shopify App Store) so you inherit a built-in customer base: "You don't need a big audience. You don't need to spend money on ads." All of Kaching's growth is organic (app-store ranking + reviews), ~$0 marketing spend.
- The gap in app marketplaces: solo indie developers ship technical, clunky UX; VC-backed/corporate apps have good UX but ship slowly (months to a year for a feature). A designer-led small team can beat both.
- Reviews are the growth engine: five-star reviews drive ranking, ranking drives installs, installs drive revenue. The whole company is optimized toward getting reviews, mostly via customer support.
- Launch free to remove install friction; monetize only after you rank for keywords and have reviews; grandfather existing users.
- Stay tiny and lean: two people (designer + developer) is enough to start; ~90% profit margin since day one; async remote team.
- Idea filter: easy to build (couple of months max), broad market (useful to any Shopify store), low competition.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Validation before building: joined dropshipper Discords and e-commerce Facebook groups; posted a screenshot of the unpublished app design; it got 100+ likes and comments asking where to download it. He also recommends a landing page collecting emails, or pre-orders if you can charge.
- Design process: study competitors' UX, note likes/dislikes, then design something "objectively better."
- Finding a co-founder: posted in a local developer Facebook group pitching (1) Shopify experience, (2) design skill, (3) existing Shopify clients to sell to.
- Cold start: manually onboarded everyone he knew (clients, friends, community members) to install the free app; installs -> reviews -> ranking -> revenue -> feedback.
- Review machine: in-product prompt asks for a review right away; monthly email shows the merchant how much revenue the app generated for them and asks for a review; ~95% of reviews come from customer support; support is 24/7, agents are technical and can go into the merchant's code to fix things.
- Gamified support bonuses: monthly leaderboard of reviews per agent, raffles, bonus per review, and a "Persuader of the Month" award computed as conversion rate = reviews received / review asks.
- Monetization sequence: introduced subscriptions only after initial reviews and keyword rankings; later tiered pricing (the more you use it, the more you pay). Every new app launches free; once organic installs begin, existing users are grandfathered free and only new users pay. Always a free trial. Full refunds, no questions asked, because "the risk of getting one star review is just too big."
- Tooling: Slack, Intercom (support), FeaturesVote (public feature board with upvotes), Linear (tasks), Mantle (Shopify metrics, funnels, affiliates, customer management).
- Personal workflow: to-do list split into quick wins (<30 min) done first, hard tasks later.
- Advice: start with two people, do your own support until ticket volume forces hiring; hang around builder communities to learn and maybe find a co-founder.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- "Launch free, monetize later" works here because the Shopify App Store ranks on reviews/installs; contrasts with Adam Lyttle's later advice to put the paywall right after onboarding on iOS.
- Grandfathering early users free forever trades revenue for reviews/goodwill; most pricing advice says raise prices on everyone.
- Full no-questions refunds prioritized over revenue protection, explicitly to avoid one-star reviews.
- Monetizing support agents per review could raise platform-policy concerns; he presents it as straightforward.
- The video includes a sponsored segment steering viewers to the monday.com marketplace; the "next Shopify" claim is advertising.
