## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- After "months analyzing over 100 viral AI apps," the winners are not complex: each has one core feature, a long onboarding and a hard paywall; the differentiator is marketing.
- All are subscription apps (weekly/monthly/yearly) pushing the yearly plan and offering the free trial only on yearly to collect cash upfront.
- Winners run hundreds of Facebook ad creatives at once; they can afford it because paywall A/B testing has maximized LTV - "the higher your LTV, the more you can spend on paid ads."
- "Marketing is 90% of the success of any mobile app"; building is the easy part now that vibe-coding tools exist.
- Roadmap: validate a painful, marketable problem → build an MVP in 2-4 weeks with vibe-coding tools + an Upwork dev → produce content at volume, test organically, scale on paid → charge as much as possible on a yearly plan.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Cal AI onboarding template: intro screen → "Where did you hear about us?" → long questionnaire customizing the experience → user spends 3+ minutes before any paywall (sunk cost). "If they open your app and the first thing they see is a paywall, you're not going to convert anyone."
- Ask for an App Store rating inside onboarding, before the paywall - the reason Cal AI sits at 4.8 stars with a huge rating count.
- Then push-notification prompt, referral code entry, goal-setting/commitment screen, "your custom plan" screen.
- Three-screen paywall: screen 1 "We want you to try Cal AI for free" with button "Try now for $0"; screen 2 "We'll send you a reminder before your free trial ends"; screen 3 the real paywall with the process recap, and only there is payment info requested. The two priming screens build trust and make the trial "an absolute no-brainer."
- Reframe (quit drinking) runs the identical structure and even the same visual design.
- Pricing observed: Cal AI pushes yearly at $30/year, charged after a 3-day trial; Reframe free trial only on the $99/year plan; Lazy Fit free trial only on the $40/year plan.
- Ad volume observed in Facebook Ads Library: Learna 700 active ads, Cal AI 500, Lazy Fit 640.
- Build: pick a problem, validate via Sensor Tower competitor revenue, Google Trends search volume, and whether the concept already goes viral on social; don't spend more than 2-4 weeks building; vibe-code the MVP in Rork, Emergent or Bolt (gets ~70% there), then pay an Upwork developer $500-$2,000 for the last 30% (bugs, App Store compliance, analytics, Superwall integration). Don't make it perfect - every app has bugs and bad reviews.
- Content: post a lot, test organically on your own profiles, run what gets views as paid; source bulk UGC via Posted (his product; free to launch a deal, pay only for approved content, CPM option, you own the content for TikTok/Meta ads forever).
- Free "viral apps breakdown" doc linked in description with each app's Sensor Tower link, onboarding, paywall and Facebook ads.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Cal AI (calorie tracking, "$2M/month"): the onboarding and paywall masterclass; 4.8 stars; $30/year.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Third-party estimates (Sensor Tower, as read out by Cravotta; he says they are "probably a little bit low"): Learna $2M/mo, Cal AI $2M/mo, Lazy Fit $700K, Coin Snap $700K, Impulse $700K, I Am $600K, Rock Identifier $300K.

- Self-reported: Puff Count $1,300/day; Posted $150K+/month; dev cost $500-$2,000 on Upwork; 2-4 week build.
- "Marketing is 90%" is opinion, not data.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Hard paywall + long onboarding is the opposite of Google's minimal-onboarding guidance (Xvjxr0lkcjE) and uxpeak's "give value first" principle; Mobbin's data (22% of apps paywall in onboarding) shows it is common but not universal.
- Rating prompt before the paywall is a gray-area tactic (Apple's guidelines expect prompts after meaningful use); he calls it "cheeky."
- Revenue figures are third-party estimates; he hand-waves that they're "probably low."
- Pitches his own product (Posted) as the content engine mid-video.
- Same core playbook as his other videos; the "100 apps" is more a framing than a study with a published methodology.
