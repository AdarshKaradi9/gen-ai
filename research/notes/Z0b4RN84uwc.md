## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- (Title) Onboarding is a one-time captive moment that most developers waste; the lever that converts users into payers is the onboarding flow, "not the marketing... not adding new features."
- Onboarding is the app's "hook," like a YouTube intro: you have a few seconds with a captive audience ("you've just jumped onto a plane and you've put your seatbelt on").
- Every converting flow has the same elements: congratulate/affirm the user, social proof, show benefits (not features), demonstrate premium functionality, then the paywall.
- Benefits over features: "Benefits, benefits, benefits... You want them to feel like this is the right app... ready to subscribe right there and then, before they've even used the app."
- Long questionnaire flows (Cal AI, ~20 steps) work by mixing questions, stats, affirmations and personalization; a stripped-down 6-8 screen version (Gen Z Bible) is easier to ship.
- Distribution and monetization are separate problems; fixing onboarding/paywalls across his portfolio took him from ~$10K to ~$50K/month (profile, self-reported).

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Adam's own "cinematic onboarding" (4 steps, SwiftUI source shared): (1) big welcome "you've come to the right place"; (2) social proof; (3) jump straight into the core feature with an animated graphic (example: "turn your phone into a Bluetooth mic"); (4) show value-add features (voice filters) they "didn't even know they wanted"; optional review prompt (risk of 1-star reviews); then "unlock premium access."
- Cal AI's ~20-step flow dissected: first screen shows the app in action (photo of food); first question is a generic fitness question ("how often do you work out") to hook into the why, not "how much weight do you want to lose"; "where did you hear about us" and "tried other apps" = free market research even from non-converters; stat screens ("80% of Cal AI users maintain their weight loss after 6 months"; "90% of users say the change is obvious"); personal inputs (weight, height, age, goal, target weight) followed by an affirming screen ("Losing 5 kg is a realistic target"); rapid-fire questions ("How fast do you want to lose the weight?", "What's stopping you?", diet) interleaved with comparison stats ("lose twice as much weight with Cal AI vs on your own"); an expectation-setting screen (results delayed at first, then "after 7 days you can burn fat like crazy") to reduce refunds and build trust; a privacy "trust us" screen; setup phase (Apple Health, goals, calorie rollover, review prompt, notifications, referral code); a performative "generating your custom plan" wait; then the paywall at AU$49.99/year.
- Gen Z Bible's lighter version: "Why are you using the app?" -> affirmation ("We care about you. You're in the right place") -> benefits list ("not your grandma's Bible") -> "What's your vibe right now?" with a tailored response -> demo -> social proof -> review prompt -> paywall.
- His skill's blueprint: define the app's before/after "user transformation," then a confirmed screen sequence with an objective per screen, headlines/options/copy per screen, modeled on Mob, Headspace, Duolingo, Noom.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Adam openly questions whether showing premium features before the paywall "feels a bit bait and switchy."
- He doubts a 20-step flow is necessary ("Do you need that many? Probably not") and suspects the "generating plan" delay is performative.
- Contrasts with Hunter Isaacson's 15-20 second onboarding for social apps; this is the subscription-utility playbook.
- The Cal AI numbers are Adam's reading of public claims, not audited.
