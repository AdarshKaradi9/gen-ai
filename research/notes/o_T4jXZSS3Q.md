# I grew my indie app to 30k+ downloads/month without spending $$$ on marketing (and so can you)

## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- You do not need a marketing budget: eight free channels took one of Adam's apps from ~1,000 downloads/month to ~30,000/month (self-reported).
- "Become your own influencer": short-form video platforms give every post a baseline audience, so a tiny conversion rate per video compounds; a viral hit does the rest.
- App Store Optimization is the foundation: target keywords with low competition but decent popularity; it has put his apps at the top of their categories.
- Design shareable moments inside the app (awards, achievements) so users do the promoting, with a watermarked 1:1 share image.
- Evergreen channels (Quora answers indexed by Google, Product Hunt backlinks, App Store feature submission) keep paying after the initial effort.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- TikTok math: each video gets an automatic reach of ~200 targeted viewers; converting 1% = 2 downloads per video; post daily and wait for a viral hit. For the Pokemon card scanner he "drew a pokemon card every day" to build the initial audience.
- Reddit: subreddits hate self-promotion, so sneak it in subtly. Past trick: built a video-to-GIF site, watched the "new" tab of popular subreddits, converted videos to GIFs and reposted them elsewhere; hit the Reddit front page "most days (with tens of thousands of upvotes)". Downside: "getting found out."
- Product Hunt: a popularity contest ("builders with the most friends"), results "a bit meh", but a feature gives backlinks and credibility and forces a disciplined release cycle and marketing assets.
- ASO: find keywords with low competition and decent popularity. Worked example from his X thread: "intermittent fasting tracker" scored 82/100 competitiveness and only 15/100 popularity (Appfigures), so he rebranded the app to "Fast Tracker & Timer" to target "fast tracker" (competitiveness low 20s in 2022), reached #2 for the term, downloads went from ~50/month to 861 in June 2023 (+1,622%), then planned to pivot to "fasting tracker" (double the popularity) once reviews and history accumulated.
- Build in public on Twitter/X with #buildinpublic to gather feedback and a following that converts to downloads.
- Shareable moments: unlock achievements/awards that users want to post; generate a 1:1 image with the app logo and website URL watermark so viewers can find the app.
- Quora: answer related questions with helpful, credible answers that subtly mention the app (used for his intermittent fasting app); add the download link to the bio; once indexed by Google it is an evergreen download source.
- Submit the app for App Store feature consideration via Apple's form; cheap to do and a feature can produce a big download spike.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- ~1,000 -> ~30,000 downloads/month for one app with $0 ad spend (self-reported in title/description/newsletter).
- TikTok baseline ~200 viewers per video; 1% conversion -> 2 downloads per video (Adam's rule of thumb, self-reported).
- Fasting app: ~50 -> 861 downloads/month (+1,622%) after keyword rebrand; ranked #2 for "fast tracker" (self-reported, X thread, June 2023). Previously only $3.26 MRR on Google Play 12 months after release (self-reported).
- "An app every month for the past four years" at the time of the episode (self-reported).

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Several tactics are explicitly grey-hat (Reddit GIF reposting, "gaming Quora for links") and Adam notes the Reddit approach ended with "getting found out".
- Product Hunt is framed as mostly useless for downloads, contrary to launch-playbook advice; its value is process discipline and backlinks.
- Later Adam content (2026, provenstartups/indiemakerlist profiles) says the "launch a new app, get hundreds of downloads in a day" organic boost has largely disappeared and he has shifted toward apps that can get featured, so the ASO-first tactics here are time-sensitive.
- The video is pre-transcript here: specific numbers for the Reddit, Quora and Product Hunt channels are not available in the written sources.
