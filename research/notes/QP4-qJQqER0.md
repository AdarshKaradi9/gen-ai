## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- You can do meaningful app marketing in a single afternoon with zero budget using three channels: (1) ad exchanges (cross-promotion networks where indie apps show each other's ads), (2) press releases, (3) SEO outreach (getting mentions/backlinks from relevant sites).
- Lyttle's broader stance (Substack, podcast): indie apps can grow from ~1,000 to 30,000 downloads/month without paid marketing; "become your own influencer" rather than paying influencers.
- Distribution and monetization are separate problems (third-party profile summarizing his talks): he solved distribution first via volume + ASO, then monetization via paywall redesign.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
From this video: only the three channel names are known (ad exchanges, press releases, SEO outreach). Specific networks, PR distribution services, email templates, and outreach targets were not found in writing.
From his related free-growth write-up (same creator, different video):
- TikTok: automatic reach of ~200 targeted viewers per video; at 1% conversion that's 2 downloads/video; he drew a Pokemon card every day to seed his Pokemon card scanner app.
- Reddit: subtle only; he once built a video-to-GIF site and reposted converted GIFs to hit the Reddit front page "most days" with tens of thousands of upvotes, until found out.
- Product Hunt: "a bit meh" for him, but useful for backlinks, credibility and forcing a release checklist.
- ASO: target keywords with low competition and decent popularity; reached top category spots.
- Build in public on X with #buildinpublic.
- Shareable moments: achievements/awards users want to share; render a 1:1 image with logo and website watermark.
- Quora: answer related questions with a subtle app mention and a download link in bio; Google-indexed answers become evergreen traffic (worked for his intermittent fasting app).
- Submit your app for App Store featuring.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- His apps referenced in related material: Card Value Scanner for Pokemon, Subscriber Count for YouTube+, Intermittent Fasting+ Tracker, Pushups Counter, poop tracker, OB Wheel for Pregnancy, AI scanner apps (fish, insect, vegan ingredient).

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- 1,000 -> 30,000 downloads/month with free tactics (self-reported).
- Paywall/onboarding redesign took monthly revenue from ~$10k to ~$50k; +230% trial subscriptions from one paywall change (self-reported, via podcast and profile).
- 50+ apps built; $100k+ revenue in one year (2024, self-reported); crossed $1M cumulative App Store sales; "$700k/year" app business (self-reported X post).
- Sold one app at 2x annual revenue: $11k/month app for $224,000 (self-reported).
- UGC content-rewards test: $500 spend, 131k views, ~100 downloads, 8 trial signups, much of the budget eaten by a bot-driven account (self-reported X post).
- No numbers for ad exchanges, press releases, or SEO outreach found.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Press releases are widely considered low-yield for indie apps; Lyttle presents them as a free afternoon task, but his results are unknown from written sources.
- He admits his Product Hunt results were "meh," contrary to launch-playbook advice.
- His Reddit GIF tactic is explicitly described as gaming the platform and ended when he was found out.
- Other Lyttle content emphasizes that the biggest revenue jump came from the paywall, not from free promotion, so these three tactics are top-of-funnel only.
