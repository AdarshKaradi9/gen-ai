# I turned a viral idea into a $25K/month app

## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- A viral social post, not a business plan, is the cheapest way to validate an app idea. Ben Guez posted himself singing in French with translated lyrics; the shares and follows signaled demand for learning languages through music.
- He de-risked the "you only succeeded because you had an audience" objection by starting a separate, non-personal page about learning with songs; it grew faster than his own (100K followers in 5 months per press; ~200K in under a year per the episode).
- Ship an ugly MVP fast; "perfectionism is the enemy of good performance."
- Growth is a repeatable loop: publish lots of content, find organic winners, make variations, boost winners with Meta Ads, kill losers, repeat.
- Non-technical founder taking over coding (with AI tools) was the turning point for iteration speed.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Validation: use your existing audience as a test bed, then launch a concept-only page that does not rely on you personally; treat shares and follows as the demand signal.
- Product: pick a popular song (Stromae for French, Bad Bunny for Spanish), see synced lyrics + translation, tap unknown words to save to a vocab list, quizzes, karaoke mode, a fast word-translation game, and a social layer matching you with a native speaker learning your language. Music is played via YouTube (no hosted audio).
- Pricing: $10/month or $79/year; the team "actively promotes" annual, so 60% of subscribers choose annual.
- Build: hired a programmer for a ~1-month MVP ("very ugly. And very bad."), then ~4 months after launch started coding himself with Cursor/Codex.
- Stack and monthly costs (as stated, ~$1,350/mo): Firebase $200, RevenueCat ~$260 (subscriptions, paywall tests), PostHog ~$250 (analytics/experiments), Cursor ~$200, Beehiiv ~$169, OpenAI ~$150, AppsFlyer $97 (attribution), ManyChat $30 (DM automation to turn comments into app traffic).
- Channels: tried every platform/format, then concentrated on Instagram (Reels performed best) and TikTok to avoid being "overwhelmed"; Meta Ads used to test creative, audience and captions separately before scaling.
- Learning routine: listen to Starter Story episodes at the gym, note things to test (that is how he tried Google Ads), and DM past guests (Nicole, Pedro) for onboarding/product advice.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Canary (Ben's app): 50,000 downloads, 2,000 paid subscribers, 23,000 monthly active users, ~9 months post-launch.
- Duolingo: positioned against; users who "can't produce a sentence" but remember song lyrics ("Voulez-vous coucher avec moi").
- Stromae, Bad Bunny: example content.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Self-reported (dashboard shown, not audited): ~$25,000 revenue in trailing 28 days; ~$14,000 MRR (gap due to annual prepay); 50K downloads; 2K subscribers; 23K actives; 60% annual; ~1M personal followers (750K per press); concept page 100K in 5 months / ~200K in <1 year.
- Derived by the briefing: ~4% download-to-paid, not stated by Ben. No CAC, churn, paid-vs-organic split or margin disclosed.
- A PeerPush listing seen in search snippets shows "$300 MRR," clearly a stale/early figure.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- The "$25K/month" title is gross collections, not MRR (~$14K); annual-plan push inflates monthly cash.
- Founder had ~1M followers, so "zero audience" lessons do not directly apply; his counter-evidence is the separate page's growth, which measures content virality, not app retention.
- Unlike the ASO-only (Sebastian) or Reddit-only (Diego) stories, this depends on paid Meta amplification of organic winners.
