# How Top 1% Apps Keep You Hooked

- Confidence: high (full transcript read)

## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Ordinary apps (route planners, meditation, fitness trackers) use the same psychological principles as TikTok/Instagram to drive return visits.
- The apps people stick with win because they are "psychological engines" aligned with how the brain decides, anticipates reward and seeks completion, not because of features or aesthetics.
- Six principles, each tied to one app: real-time feedback loops (Perplexity), Hick's law / fewer choices (Apple Fitness), Fitts' law / big reachable targets (Waze), emotional anchoring (Headspace), social proof (Discord), Zeigarnik effect / visible unfinished progress (Stompers, a Tamagotchi-style step app).
- Do not stack too many loops: Hick's law applies to progress mechanics too.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Feedback loops: make waiting states active (loader animation, voice dots reacting in real time); add micro-rewards like flickers or pulses to validate input; "treat in-between moments as intentional, not empty". He notes this polish usually comes later in an app's life.
- Activation: steer new users to one action (Apple Fitness: press "Let's Go" and move, not 30 workouts). Pick one clear focus across the app; strip friction from the first moments.
- Ergonomics: core buttons large and placed for one-handed thumb reach; convey priority with size; design for distracted, limited-attention use (Waze: one hand on the wheel).
- Emotional design: set the emotional state at onboarding via color palette, animation and sound (Headspace slows your pace before the session starts). "Visuals guide behavior."
- Social proof: show, don't tell. Real-time activity, lit-up avatars, reaction counters, "2,312 people online" labels; treat community as a UX feature and surface it rather than bury it.
- Progress loops: stack visible progress, keep thresholds close, make next levels feel achievable, treat completion as a reward loop and show it visually; loops that "never fully close, but always feel within reach". Limit: don't show 20 loops on one screen.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Perplexity mobile: loader animation and voice dots as emotional reassurance.
- Apple Fitness: single "Let's Go" entry point (Hick's law).
- Waze: large, thumb-adjacent core buttons (Fitts' law).
- Headspace: "invested millions" into color/animation/sound to create a calm space from onboarding.
- Discord: online counts, avatars, reactions as ambient social proof.
- Stompers: fun packs, streaks, encouragement; Zeigarnik effect.
- Mentions Duolingo, Revolut, Phantom as subjects of a companion video on emotional design. Promotes free strategy calls at his agency (Zips/"SIPs").

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Treats social-media-style hooks as neutral tools for ordinary apps, versus Nir Eyal-style talks that spend time on the ethics of manipulation.
- Says responsiveness polish can wait until later in the app's evolution, while activation simplicity comes first; many "delight" guides invert this.
- Explicitly cautions against over-gamifying (multiple loops per screen), unlike gamification-maximalist content.
