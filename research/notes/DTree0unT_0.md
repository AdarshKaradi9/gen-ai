## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Virality comes from understanding group psychology: "what are they going to feel and then how do those emotions dictate the action that we're requesting them to do."
- Platform arbitrage: build on top of an existing distribution graph (Instagram, Snapchat, X, Facebook) and "augment" it rather than compete. NGL was started the day Instagram opened story links to everyone (end of Oct 2021; previously 10K followers required).
- Simplicity wins: NGL onboarding is 15-20 seconds, one flow, one funnel, one north-star metric. The app today looks almost the same as when it went viral; competitors over-complicated with feeds and profiles.
- Sell the end result in the CTA: "get your own messages" beats "download this app."
- Monetize a tiny fraction of a huge free user base with cheap weekly subscriptions.
- Tight viral loops create a "very high redownload rate" that offsets churn.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- NGL home screen: two steps, copy link then share. Share screen uses animated GIFs instructing: tap sticker, tap link, add to story, frame the link; the app auto-generates the asset with the user's profile picture. Message screen has a dice button for prompt ideas and a persistent download button at the bottom; after sending, it says "get your own messages." Replies are copied straight onto the user's story. "Get a hint" reveals phone type / rough location.
- North-star metric for NGL: how many people post the link AND post at least two replies.
- Monetization: weekly subscription priced by country GDP per capita, $1-$7, a "low impulse buy"; only power users pay (insights on who sent messages/viewed profile, extra "link games"). Multi-8-figure annual revenue from a very small paying %.
- Activation after 6 months of nothing: $100-here-$100-there influencer tests; one paid post lit up a school in a lower-income country, which exposed the need for Android (built by May 2022); then one TikTok (a girl reading a provocative NGL message like "I was with your boyfriend last night" and trying to guess who sent it) hit ~1M views fast and activated the first 1,000 users in one day. They tested many message texts; "funny, controversial, edgy" won.
- If a user is already sold on the value, a multi-step tutorial (3-4 taps to add an app to the iOS share menu, 5-6 steps for a Safari extension) does not kill conversion; "sell them effectively in the beginning, then sure, stick up a tutorial," and measure drop-off per step.
- Always add invite/share screens: a friend texting you an app or posting it on a story is an endorsement; it lowers effective CPI.
- With a $20K/month budget at $10K MRR: spend most on UGC/influencers or ads, split into many small experiments (a dozen creatives, cohort tracking by ad/video), and pair with product-led growth so X% of paid users invite a friend; then scale the winning format.
- Bags (his crypto app): 1% transaction fee, most passed back to the inviting friend or the group-chat poster; token launchpad with 1% royalty on every trade ("$2 million of volume and you'll make $20,000"); Apple Pay on-ramp via MoonPay.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- NGL: 150M MAU three years in, 100M+ downloads, built in ~1 month then 6 months of no usage; nobody cloned it until after it went viral.
- Wink: swipe-to-add-on-Snapchat app, ~70M downloads (he cites "over 70 million downloads on other apps" pre-NGL).
- Zoom University: double-dating video app for college kids during lockdown; 40,000 waitlist, 7:1 girl-to-guy ratio, "quadruple opt-in," top 10 social, a couple hundred thousand downloads; seeded via meme pages and every college Facebook group.
- Unnamed PvP mini-games + voice chat app: close to an hour average time spent per user.
- PopStream: pay to FaceTime a celebrity; killed because top influencers charged ~$20K per link post.
- Leadr: first app, 2.5 years, died in TestFlight ("spectacular failure").
- Faves: save Instagram/TikTok videos via the share menu; went near #1 (Hunter advised).
- Recipe-from-video share-menu app and a Safari-extension credit-card recommender paying up to $500/referral discussed as validated ideas.
- 9Count (Alex Hofmann's studio): "Make New Friends" and "Summer" totaled 30M+ downloads (beehiiv source).

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Self-reported: 150M MAU; multi-8-figure revenue; $1-$7 weekly pricing; 15-20s onboarding; first 1,000 users in one day from one TikTok; 70M downloads pre-NGL; ~1 hour/user on the games app; 40K waitlist / 7:1 ratio for Zoom University.
- Download totals conflict across sources (Superwall recap says 70M; beehiiv says 100M+ for NGL alone; other coverage claims 250M+); treat as approximate.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Argues friction (multi-step tutorials) is fine once value is sold, against the usual "remove every tap" advice.
- "A very small percentage of our downloads actually monetize" is the opposite of the hard-paywall, high-ARPU model other creators (e.g., Cravotta) push.
- Pushes back on the idea that TikTok virality just churns out: redownload loops matter.
- Says gaming is "double hard" for small founders (retention + paid-dominated UA) despite the appeal of PvP mechanics.
- Platform windows close: his assumption that "everyone" would build on the Instagram link feature was wrong, but it still took 6 months to catch.

## Memorable framings (quotable one-liners or named frameworks, verbatim if available)
- "It's way easier to get a download by saying get your own messages than saying, hey, download this app."
- "How do you make it like one flow, one funnel, and don't over complicate it and just focus on like a northstar metric."
- "Find existing things that happen on other networks... and just switch the distribution mechanism and improve the product to a certain degree."
- "It doesn't compete with the big networks. I think it does enhance or augment the experience."
- "There's a test flight graveyard."
- Superwall's summary: "Simple wins because simple spreads."
