## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Apps are unusually lucrative right now for two reasons: LLMs let you solve problems that were impossible or too expensive before (RizzGPT could not exist without them), and TikTok/Instagram are "very solvable" distribution because platforms are pushing views to new accounts.
- Find ideas either from a clear observed problem (people don't know what to say on dating apps) or from niches already heavily monetized with physical products (looksmaxxing: skincare, hair care, gua sha) where no software exists.
- Validate by becoming the target user: create a fresh social account, consume only that niche's content, then build what you would want.
- Marketing is "constant iteration": influencer marketing plus internal UGC on app-branded accounts; double down ruthlessly on whatever returns profitably.
- Subscriptions at low price points, split-tested with Superwall, are the monetization model; AI backend costs are a rounding error compared with marketing and staff.
- Urgency (financial pressure, something to prove) was his biggest productivity lever.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Learned to code with ChatGPT in about a month, then built RizzGPT shortly after.
- Idea heuristic: "when you find a niche that's heavily monetized with physical products, that's a good indicator that the niche converts" and that software can be built for it.
- Validation: create an account (e.g., "Cal"), consume only nutrition/dieting/calorie-counting/weightlifting content, "essentially becoming the target market," then build what you want.
- Build stack if starting today: Figma for design (use reference apps with similar function to inform the design; you must be able to work in Figma even if you hire designers from Upwork), React Native with Expo, Cursor as IDE, Node.js backend; CapCut for content; Mercury for banking; Upwork hiring method: hire 5-10 people for 1-2 hours of work, keep the best one.
- Marketing playbook: influencer marketing (pay people with audiences) and internal UGC (you or a hire makes slideshows, faceless videos or face-to-camera content posted to accounts branded around the app). Iterate until a channel returns profitably, then "double down and double down and double down."
- Getting creators to respond: find a Discord link hidden in a creator's Instagram bio, join it, message every 10 minutes until they reply; he even DM'd creators' mothers to get contact. Early RizzGPT growth: paid roughly $50 per account on two faceless YouTube channels/TikTok accounts; 200,000 downloads in five days and $80K MRR (scalenuggets recap, self-reported).
- Monetization: subscription, deliberately low price points (more users, more word of mouth, better social sentiment, more sustainable); use Superwall to split-test weekly/monthly/yearly offers and placements, which "can take you from making x per user that downloads to 1.5 or 2X."
- Cost structure: Apple takes 15% (effectively ~20%) up to the first $1M, then 30% (effectively ~33%); AI backend costs are "sub 3%" unless doing image generation or advanced voice; marketing and staff dominate. Profit margins by channel: internal UGC 50-80%, influencer marketing 25-70%, paid ads 0-30%.
- Apple's free distribution matters: about half of Umax downloads came from App Store featuring/algorithmic promotion; Android users don't convert as well despite lower fees (scalenuggets recap).
- Ideas he'd build now: a consolidated "career AI" platform for students (roll up resume analysis, LinkedIn image generation, etc.), and language learning on advanced voice mode (Duolingo makes ~$33M/month; 1% of that market is a $4M/year app).

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- RizzGPT (now Plug AI): AI dating-reply assistant; ~$2.5M annual revenue; Blake left after a co-founder power struggle ("either you run the company and I keep my equity or I run the company and you keep your equity").
- Umax: looksmaxxing/attractiveness-rating app; "almost $5 million in revenue" in the past year; a little over $100K profit/month "consistently, used to do more"; built while sleeping on his brother's couch, coded through Thanksgiving alone.
- Cal AI: photo calorie tracker co-founded with Zach Yadegari and Henry Langmack; launched ~6 months before the interview; "over a million dollars in revenue per month"; "a few hundred thousand in profit a month."
- Apex: his new all-in-one self-improvement ecosystem (content, free community, free apps, low-margin physical products), started ~1.5 months before the interview.
- Tools: ChatGPT, Figma, Cursor, React Native/Expo, Node.js, CapCut, Mercury, Upwork, Superwall, Duolingo (as market benchmark).

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Argues for low price points, whereas much app-monetization advice (and his own Superwall-testing suggestion) pushes toward higher weekly pricing; he frames low prices as a growth and sentiment strategy.
- Says AI inference cost worries are "significantly overestimated"; contrasts with common "AI wrapper margins are bad" advice.
- Paid ads are treated as the worst-margin channel (0-30%), contrary to many growth playbooks.
- Revenue figures are entirely self-reported and the host's "$20M/year" and the title's "$10M" are different framings of the same claims; Blake declined to share RizzGPT profit.
- Personal narrative (drugs, partying, broke after college) is used as the urgency driver; the practical lesson is "create a sense of urgency," which is not replicable advice for everyone.
