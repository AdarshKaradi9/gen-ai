## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- The single highest-leverage tweak for converting downloads into payers is the onboarding flow, "not the marketing", not new features, not a rewrite.
- Onboarding is the app's "hook", like the first seconds of a YouTube video: you have a captive audience for a few seconds and should use it to educate AND prepare the user for premium.
- Every successful onboarding has the same skeleton: congratulate/affirm the user, social proof, show benefits (not features), demo advanced/premium functionality, then the paywall.
- "Benefits, benefits, benefits" and the feeling the user gets, so they are ready to subscribe "before they've even used the app".
- Long questionnaire onboardings (Cal AI, 20+ steps) work by mixing questions, stats, affirmations and personalization; a stripped-down 6-8 screen version (Gen Z Bible) captures most of the value with far less build effort.
- The long questionnaire doubles as free market research even for users who never subscribe.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Lyttle's own "cinematic onboarding" (SwiftUI source linked in description), 4 steps: (1) big welcome "you've come to the right place" + social proof; (2) jump straight to the core feature with an animated graphic (example: "turn your phone into a Bluetooth mic"); (3) show the value-add features (voice filters) "they didn't even know they wanted"; optional review prompt here ("be cautious", it can produce one-star reviews); (4) "unlock premium access" paywall.
- Cal AI flow as dissected (20+ steps, three phases):
  - Phase 1 (market research + affirmation): first screen shows app in action (photo your food); first question is generic fitness ("how often do you work out") not "how much weight do you want to lose", because some users want to bulk; "where did you hear about us" and "have you tried similar apps" = free market research; benefit stat "80% of Cal AI users maintain their weight loss after 6 months"; personal inputs (weight, height, age, goal, target weight); affirming screen "Losing 5 kg is a realistic target"; stat "90% of users say the change is obvious".
  - Phase 2 (rapid-fire questions + hard stats): how fast do you want to lose weight; "lose twice as much weight with Cal AI vs on your own"; what's stopping you; do you follow a specific diet; affirmation "You have great potential to crush your goal"; an honesty screen that says it takes time to build momentum rather than "you will lose weight, full stop" (Lyttle: reduces refunds, builds trust); privacy "trust us" screen.
  - Phase 3 (setup): link Apple Health, add goals, calorie rollover, write a review, notifications, referral code; then a multi-minute "generating your custom plan" animation Lyttle suspects is "performative"; then back to the first screen; Continue opens the subscription screen at A$49.99/year.
- Gen Z Bible (by "Austinine"/Ammani) condensed version: why are you using the app -> affirmation "We care about you, you're in the right place" -> benefits list -> "What's your vibe right now?" (answer "anxious" -> "God's got my back") -> how-it-works walkthrough -> social proof -> review prompt -> paywall.
- Framing of premium inside onboarding: show the advanced features during onboarding, then "reinforce those special features that were actually not included for free are paid features" at the paywall.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)

- Lyttle's own Bluetooth-mic app: 4-screen cinematic onboarding.
- Apple Health integration as a setup step in Cal AI.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Lyttle openly questions whether showing premium features during onboarding then paywalling them is "a bit bait and switchy".
- He contradicts the "just let people use the app" instinct: users say they want to skip onboarding, but a hook screen still converts better.
- He doubts the 20+ step flow is necessary ("Do you need that many? Probably not") and says the plan-generation delay is likely theatre; recommends the simpler Gen Z Bible pattern.
- Review prompt inside onboarding is flagged as risky.
