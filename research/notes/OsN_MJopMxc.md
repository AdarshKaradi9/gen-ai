## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Profile piece: Zach Yadegari, 18, learned to code at a summer camp at age 7 (inspired by Minecraft), built and sold a gaming site in high school, then launched Cal AI from his parents' home in Roslyn, NY in May 2024.
- Cal AI lets users photograph a meal and get an AI calorie estimate (claimed ~90% accuracy); free download, subscription $2.49/month or $29.99/year.
- The business is big but thin-margined: ~$1.4M/month gross profit after Apple/Google cuts, ~$274k/month net operating income, with nearly $770k/month spent on advertising and marketing (CNBC-reviewed documents).
- The founding approach was personal pain (manual calorie logging was tedious) plus a cheap marketing test ($2,000 social media test) plus speed (code written in a one-month July 2024 sprint at an SF hacker house).
- Yadegari plans to run Cal AI about two more years, then sell or hand off; he says he will likely leave college early.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Spend a small fixed budget ($2,000) on a social-media marketing test before committing; the response funded the decision for two co-founders to bankroll six months of costs until App Store payouts caught up.
- Pricing: $2.49/month or $29.99/year (CNBC article; note the Jack Neel interview quotes $10/month or $30/year with a 3-day trial - price points have changed over time or by region).
- Co-founder sourcing: coding-camp friend (Henry Langmack) plus two people met on X (Blake Anderson, 24; Jake Castillo, 30).
- Manage expectations about AI accuracy in-product: users "expect it to have X-ray vision"; hidden ingredients need manual correction - reviews complain about accuracy.
- Work cadence: 40 hours/week on the app while in school, 4.0 GPA.
- (From The Dock, same company, different interview) "silent integration" influencer videos with the logo at top of screen; outreach of 50+ influencers/day by VAs with a two-word opener ("Paid promo?"); bundle four videos/month for better rates; minimum-view guarantees for big creators; no free plan, trials only; onboarding animations A/B test lifted conversion ~10%; streak notifications ("You've logged 70 days straight!"); users with 10,000+ calories tracked showed 80%+ retention so milestones were gamified toward that point; "dumb" onboarding questions ("Why do you want to lose weight?") added purely to raise commitment.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Cal AI: 30 employees, 8.3M downloads as of July 2025 (spokesperson), $28k revenue in month one, $115k in month two.
- Totally Science: unblocked gaming site sold to Freeze Nova for ~$100k in February 2024.
- MyFitnessPal: the target to beat (270M+ self-reported users).
- Yadegari's Instagram ad for a co-launched app-development course (Lamborghini with "CAL AI" plate, "money does buy happiness") - CNBC notes it as his public persona.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Third-party (documents reviewed by CNBC): ~$1.4M/month gross profit after store fees; ~$274k/month net operating income; ~$770k/month advertising and marketing; one recent $100k dividend to Yadegari; 8.3M downloads (July 2025, spokesperson).
- Self-reported: 90% accuracy; $2,000 test spend; $28k then $115k monthly revenue in the first two months; 40 hrs/week; 4.0 GPA.
- Conflicting headline: The Nightly's syndicated headline says "$2.1 million-a-month" while the body and CNBC say $1.4M; treat $2.1M as an outlier/possibly gross revenue before store cuts.
- The Dock figures (10% onboarding lift, 80%+ retention at 10k calories, 150+ influencers) are self-reported in a different interview.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- "$1.4M/month" is gross profit, not profit: marketing eats more than half; net operating income is ~20% of that.
- Pricing stated here ($2.49/mo, $29.99/yr) differs from the $10/mo-$30/yr paywall described in the Jack Neel podcast (qDuzzrQKP_M); likely A/B variants or later changes.
- The video presents speed-to-launch and influencer spend as the story; the actual video narration could not be sourced, so any on-camera tactics beyond the article are unknown.
- Customer reviews flag accuracy problems - the "aha" feature is also the main churn/complaint driver.
