## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Lyttle struggled for a year to get anyone to pay; the breakthrough was not subscriptions or pricing per se but adding a FREE TRIAL, which let him place paywalls aggressively anywhere in the app.
- The app business model is uniquely about "annoying and disrupting the user as much as possible without making them quit" via ads, locked features and pattern interrupts.
- Three best-converting paywall placements: right after onboarding; when the user attempts the app's core action; on app relaunch.
- "Pattern interrupt": put a wait/queue on FREE core actions so time-valuing users subscribe for instant access.
- Decide free vs paid by asking what the user came for (core = free) vs value-add (paid); imagine rebuilding the app in a few days, what you'd cut is the paid tier.
- Hit the balance "annoying enough to make people want to upgrade, but not annoying enough to make them delete the app" and the first 100 payers follow.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Pricing path: first tried a one-off unlock for "a couple of dollars"; after seeing Flippa listings he switched to subscription with free trial, initially $10/year with a trial (successful Flippa apps were charging ~$10/month).
- Paywall placement #1: immediately after onboarding, "most users sign up for the free trial immediately after onboarding, before using any features."
- Paywall placement #2: on the core action tap, show paywall, then throw the user into a queue/cool-down ("even if it is only 3 seconds, which is enough to disrupt them"); "unlock premium" for instant access or wait.
- Paywall placement #3: on relaunch of the app.
- Worked example, MakeTune (photo to cartoon): paywall right after onboarding; selecting a photo and tapping Generate puts the user in a queue; users who keep generating "eventually get tired of waiting for results and subscribe."
- Don't do what he first did: "a spammy mess of paywalls appearing constantly" (he does not recommend it, but it taught him which spots convert).
- Free/paid split exercise: "imagine rebuilding the entire app from scratch with only a few days"; features that survive are core and stay free; everything cut becomes the premium tier.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- MakeTune (Lyttle's own app): pattern-interrupt queue on generation.
- Flippa: where he observed that selling apps all used paid subscriptions with free trials at ~$10/month.
- Related (RevenueCat post, not in this video): Lyttle's mid-2024 "toggle paywall" doubled his weekly revenue from ~$2,500 to >$5,300 with 63% intro-offer conversion and 68% retention (self-reported); Apple began rejecting the toggle pattern in Jan 2026.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Poll he cites: "less than 10% of people who have an app idea ever generate a single dollar" (informal poll, self-reported).
- Started monetizing July 2021; one year of near-zero payers; after the change: 116 paid subscribers in June 2022, 113 in July, 117 in August (self-reported).
- Pattern-interrupt wait: 3 seconds is enough.
- Early price: $10/year with free trial (self-reported).

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Directly contradicts "let users experience value before asking for money": he paywalls BEFORE any feature use and says most trial starts happen there.
- Deliberately degrades UX of free users (queues on free features), which conflicts with retention-first advice; he frames the entire app industry as built on this.
- He admits his first implementation was spammy and warns against it.
- The free-trial dependence later collided with Apple policy (toggle paywall rejections, per RevenueCat), so the specific UI has shifted since.
