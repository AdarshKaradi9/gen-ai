# How free games are designed to make money
- URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fKK9nVLvhGM | Channel: Vox | Views: 4316250 | Length: 279s

- Confidence: high

## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Freemium has "largely wiped out the market for paid games", so designers must monetize gameplay itself using behavioral psychology.
- Step 1 is a virtual currency so spending doesn't feel like real money, with deliberately confusing exchange rates.
- Payment is made painless (store already has your card) while parts of the game are made deliberately painful; you pay to remove pain the designer inserted.
- Loss aversion is used for timing purchase prompts (offer to save progress at the moment of loss).
- Only a tiny fraction pay, and a tiny fraction of those ("whales") drive roughly half of revenue; most players are unpaid content for a game tuned to hit a susceptible minority.
- Data collection enables per-player price discrimination (discounts for likely quitters, higher prices for likely spenders, by device and country).

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Virtual currency double-layer: pay for boosters with gold bars, pay for gold bars with a credit card ("one more layer of distance from the actual payment"), echoing the cash-vs-card effect.
- Off-kilter exchange rates: "it's not 50 gems for 50 cents, they're always something weird like $1 will get you 12 purple diamonds"; Pokemon Go example: incense costs 80 PokeCoins and 550 coins cost $4.99.
- Loss-aversion prompt: in Puzzle & Dragons, when you die to the boss you can spend Magic Stones to keep the dungeon rewards you just earned.
- Embedded inconvenience: Clash of Clans and Game of War build timers that get progressively longer and are skippable for a price.
- Dynamic pricing: discount if you look like you're about to quit; higher prices for likely spenders; prices set by phone model and country.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Pokemon Go: $14M revenue in under a week after launch, all from optional purchases; monetization described as "unobtrusive... tucked away".
- Candy Crush-style boosters bought with gold bars.
- Puzzle & Dragons: Magic Stones to continue after boss death.
- Clash of Clans, Game of War: skippable wait timers.
- Swrve (marketing firm): whale revenue estimate.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Presents the "insert pain point, sell the painkiller" loop as manipulation, whereas indie monetization videos in this corpus (e.g., Lyttle's pattern interrupt) recommend the identical mechanic.
- Acknowledges the pro-F2P argument: purchases fund updates and data (where players get stuck, close the game) improves the game.
- Suggests rewarding games that monetize unobtrusively (Pokemon Go at launch).

## Memorable framings (quotable one-liners or named frameworks, verbatim if available)
