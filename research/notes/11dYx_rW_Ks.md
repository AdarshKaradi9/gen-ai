## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Per the TED description: about 40% of what you do every day is done purely out of habit; tech companies are "masters of habit-forming products"; it "isn't all negative manipulation" and the techniques "can and should be used for good".
- Per TED Radio Hour's summary of the talk: tech overuse is ultimately the user's responsibility, and people can choose to unplug.
- Eyal's standard 2015 framework (confirmed in the ProductTank write-up and MIT TR profile, not verified line-by-line against this video): the Hook = Trigger -> Action -> Variable Reward -> Investment, repeated until the product attaches to an internal trigger (an emotion like boredom or loneliness) and becomes the "first-to-mind" solution.
- Variable rewards come in three types: rewards of the tribe (social), the hunt (material/information), the self (mastery, completion).
- Investment loads the next trigger and stores value (content, followers, reputation), making switching costly.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- (From Eyal's contemporaneous talk material; verify against the video.) Map the internal trigger your product relieves and pair it with an external trigger; strip the landing page to the one intended action (Twitter's redesign to just sign up/log in).
- Behavior = trigger + motivation + ability (B.J. Fogg); when a trigger fails, increase ability (make it easier) rather than motivation.
- Match reward type to trigger: boredom -> entertainment; loneliness -> connection. Add variability (feed scrolling as a slot-machine lever).
- Ask users for a small investment after the reward (comment, follow, upload, preference) that improves the next cycle and loads the next trigger (a sent message's reply).
- Five questions: what internal trigger, what external trigger, what simplest action, is the reward fulfilling yet leaves wanting more, what investment loads the next pass.
