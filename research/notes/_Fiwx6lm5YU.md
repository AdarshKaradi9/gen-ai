## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Per the description: "Most indie app developers focus on revenue too early... but that can sometimes hurt your app's growth." The video presents his growth strategy for his game PIANORUN: "Launch free, get downloads, get feedback and (hopefully) go viral."
- Distribution and monetization are separate problems; Lyttle's consistent position (OneMillionGoal profile, Launched podcast) is to solve distribution first (downloads, ASO, reviews, feedback) and layer monetization on afterwards.
- Free launch removes install friction, maximizes downloads and reviews, and gives the ranking/feedback base you later monetize (same logic Lyttle applies across his portfolio).
- Game apps are a special case: he treats Piano Run as a "passion vs profit" project and says games are hard to monetize (Launched episode highlights [30:10], [38:10]).

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Launch the app free (no paywall at launch), collect downloads and user feedback, aim for viral/TikTok-driven spread before adding monetization (description; Launched: "release it for free, get some attention, get some downloads on it").
- For Swipe the Cat (his other game) he optimized for a viral TikTok trend rather than App Store keywords (Launched [34:02], [40:15]).
- Sequencing used across his portfolio: ASO-driven distribution first (keyword research with tools like Astro), then onboarding + paywall redesign once downloads exist; that paywall/onboarding rework took his portfolio from ~$10K/month to ~$50K/month (OneMillionGoal, MoneyMakingStory; self-reported).
- Note: in a later video ("How I build $100k apps with $0 marketing budget") he reverses parts of this: he now says to stop over-optimizing small apps, check keywords first (popularity >40, competition <60 in Astro; no keyword -> abandon idea), show the paywall right after onboarding (70%+ of trial signups happen there), and treat 500-1,000 downloads/month as "good" vs 20-30K/month as a breakout.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- PIANORUN (Piano Run), Lyttle's own game (App Store id 6449474440): the subject of the strategy; launched free to chase downloads/virality.
- Swipe the Cat: his viral-TikTok-trend game; monetization "didn't take off as expected" (Launched).
- His broader portfolio (30-50+ small iOS apps: scanners, converters, utilities) grown via ASO with $0 marketing.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- The "users first, revenue second" thesis is contradicted by Lyttle's own later videos, where he says apps stuck at $500/month are not worth optimizing, that you should validate keywords before building, and that the paywall must follow onboarding immediately; a 2026 summary of another Lyttle video has him saying "get users, monetize later" did not work for him in the App Store.
- Also contradicts Erikas (Starter Story) only partially: Erikas also launches free, but grandfathers early users and monetizes new users once rankings/reviews exist.
- Games vs utilities: his free-first/viral approach is tied to games, which he says are hard to monetize anyway.
