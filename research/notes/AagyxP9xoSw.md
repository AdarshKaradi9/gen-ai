## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- KJ (Kaijie) Chen abandoned MidReal, an AI novel/storytelling platform with ~3M users (press says 2M+), to build Macaron, a personal-agent app where anyone builds "mini apps" through conversation; the show cites a $70M valuation and 300K+ users in under six months without traditional paid ads (self-reported).
- Growth engine: user-generated TikTok/Instagram content, with creators paid on actual video performance rather than upfront fees.
- "Absurd" video content converted better than polished product demos; viral content framework: problem -> solution -> code.
- Shareable app codes turned user-created mini apps into a product-led growth loop (users share a code, others import the app).
- TikTok and Instagram drove the most conversions; X brought prestige but fewer daily users.
- Next: 200,000 SEO-optimised mini-app landing pages as free traffic engines; a new launch designed to recreate the first viral wave.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Pay creators per result (views/performance tiers) instead of flat fees so spend scales with what works (show notes; no rates given).
- Make content deliberately "absurd" rather than slick; the show notes say this converted better than product demos.
- Content structure "problem, solution, code": show a relatable problem, show the mini app solving it, end with the share code viewers can type into Macaron to get the same app.
- Build a share/import primitive into the product (app codes) so every piece of UGC carries a direct install/activation path.
- Generate SEO landing pages programmatically for each user-created mini app (plan: 200K pages) as a zero-cost acquisition channel.
- Train custom models instead of shipping a GPT wrapper (Macaron's memory is trained into the model with RL rather than keyword RAG, per 36Kr); scale container infrastructure before a viral wave because thousands of users building apps simultaneously broke it.
- Product stance (36Kr): deliberately no community/plaza feature so users feel safe discussing private topics (love, family, parenting) with the agent.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
