# Why app marketing is changing for indie developers in 2026

## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- The "golden age of App Store optimization" (build an app, add keywords, rank, profit) is over: the algorithm changed, new apps rank harder, and competition from new indie devs keeps rising.
- Some indie devs are still growing with poor ASO and zero ad spend, purely through daily organic short-form content on TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts.
- The approach costs nothing but requires showing up every day; consistency is the key variable, and the "zero traction phase" at the start is where most people (including Adam) quit.
- In 2026 distribution is "part of the job description" for indie developers, not a nice-to-have.
- Adam commits publicly to posting at least one short-form piece per day in 2026, treating it as muscle-building rather than chasing instant results.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Post short-form content daily; Max Blade and Jack Friks each post about two videos a day. Max: "The first few hours of every single day should be making short form content."
- Track views versus new subscribers: Max's graph of TikTok views vs new subs showed fewer users on days he didn't post.
- Jack's extra step: watch what is going viral in your niche, then build features into the app solely to hook into that trend; his most popular video got 4M+ views, 160K likes and 503 comments "mostly asking what's the app."
- Build from your own expertise (Max was a personal trainer with professional nutrition experience → Talk Macros, a voice-based macro logger).
- Push through the no-traction phase: expect a handful of views and likes at first; keep posting, test formats, learn what works.
- Short-form over ASO and over paid ads for indie budgets; no ad spend in either case study.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Jack Friks - Lovely (send doodles to your partner): 50,000 downloads in a matter of months with poor ASO and no ads; features built to ride viral trends; 4M-view video.
- Max Blade - Talk Macros (log calories/macros by talking): $1,000+ MRR within a few weeks of building apps for just over a month, all from TikTok/Reels; Adam used it for a week and found it easier than MyFitnessPal and less awkward than photo logging.
- MyFitnessPal (comparison), TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Self-reported by the featured developers via Adam: Lovely 50K downloads in months; Talk Macros $1K MRR in a few weeks; 4M views / 160K likes / 503 comments on Jack's top video; ~2 posts per day each.
- Adam's own admission: he tried the approach, got few views, stopped, and it "hasn't worked for me" - presented as evidence that consistency, not the tactic, was the gap.
- No third-party verification; no CPI or conversion figures.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Declares ASO largely dead for new apps, contradicting Adam's own earlier ASO-focused videos and ASO-first playbooks elsewhere in the corpus (e.g. App Masters' "ASO + Apple Search Ads first").
- Says "not about ads" while Cravotta (e_t1gYkvZkI, PX30Fb8TlcE) says organic is only the discovery step and paid ads on winners are where scale comes from; the two agree that daily organic content is the starting engine.
- Sample size is two developers, both friends of the channel ("I should probably just call my channel the Jack Fan Club"); survivorship bias is not addressed.
- Very short video; no tactical detail on hooks, formats or posting cadence beyond "two a day."
