# I Studied 10,000 Paywall Screens (THIS Makes People Pay)

## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Claimed research across 10,000 paywalls: top apps make 18.7x more revenue and their paywalls generate 636% more LTV than average, driven by six patterns, not better products or categories.
- Pattern 1: weekly is the new monthly (weekly plans now majority of subscription-app revenue).
- Pattern 2: you have one session; the first-session paywall decides nearly everything, and trust-building before the price (not brevity) is what wins.
- Pattern 3: structure over price; plan count, trial structure and localization move LTV far more than price tests.
- Pattern 4: honest paywalls (explicit charge timing) raise conversion and cut complaints.
- Pattern 5: higher prices correlate with better conversion; racing to the bottom "is exactly where conversion goes to die".
- Pattern 6: the experimentation gap explains the 18.7x: 50+ paywall experiments/yr vs 1.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Offer weekly first: weekly plans convert 1.7-7.4x better than annual across price tiers; "$7 a week feels like nothing" where $38-$400/yr does not; upsell to annual later (by ~month four).
- Put a multi-screen trial explanation before the paywall: say exactly when the user is charged and promise a reminder before the trial ends; headline "No payment due now" (Cal AI, three screens inside a 28-screen onboarding). Lose It dedicates a paywall section to the trial timeline.
- Paywall checklist: does it appear after the user has felt something? does it say exactly when they'll be charged?
- Prioritize tests by Adapty's LTV-uplift ranking: localization (language + currency) +62.3% LTV; trial-structure changes +59.6%; plan-count changes drive 63% more conversion uplift than any price test; price tests rank far lower. Most founders only A/B $9.99 vs $12.99 for three weeks.
- Add a trial to a weekly plan: $7.40 12-month LTV without trial vs $54.50 with (the 636%).
- Yazio order of operations: plan options, trial framing, streak mechanics first; price last.
- Blinkist change: one explicit front-and-center line stating when and what the user is charged -> +23% conversion, -55% complaints, 0 churn change.
- Aim for ~15 paywall experiments per year (top performers' average); tiny surfaces count (a Freecash home-screen widget redesign lifted D7 retention by several points).

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Cal AI: $35M year-one revenue (as stated), 28-screen onboarding, 3-screen trial explanation, "No payment due now".
- Lose It: trial-timeline section on the paywall; $3.3M monthly revenue at 350K installs.
- Yazio: ~$3M monthly revenue at 700K installs; structure-first paywall with streaks.
- Blinkist: documented honest-paywall redesign (+23% conversion, -55% complaints).
- Freecash: widget redesign, D7 retention lift (agency client).
- Data vendors: RevenueCat (82% of trial starts on install day; median high-price app 2.8% download-to-paid, mid-price 2%, top-quartile high-price 6.1%), Adapty (89.4% day-0 starts; 2026 report on $3B revenue; 55% of 3-day-trial cancellations on day 0). Spotify named as a past consulting client.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Third-party (cited from RevenueCat/Adapty reports; not independently checked here): weekly share of revenue 43% -> 55.6% in two years; monthly 21.1% -> 11.7%; weekly converts 1.7-7.4x annual; 82%/89.4% of starts on day 0; 55% of 3-day-trial cancels on day 0; +62.3%/+59.6%/63% uplift figures; $7.40 vs $54.50 LTV; 2.8%/2%/6.1% conversion medians; 18.7x revenue gap between 50+ vs 1 experiment per year; ~15 experiments/yr for top performers.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Directly contradicts "shorter onboarding is always better" (28 screens) and "lower price raises conversion".
- Contradicts friction-reduction-by-hiding-cost advice; Vahe Baghdasaryan (Superwall video) removes trial length from the plan card, while Gabe's Blinkist example argues for front-and-center charge timing. Both agree on "reminder before trial ends" copy.
- Conflates correlation with causation on price (higher-priced apps may simply have higher-intent audiences); he partially acknowledges this ("I'm not saying raise your price tomorrow").
- Headline numbers come from vendor reports (Adapty, RevenueCat) and are not from his own 10,000-screen study.
