## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Guest is Vahe Baghdasaryan, a paywall/growth consultant (company: Tangent) who has designed paywalls for Claim, Cal AI, Quittr, Rizz and others; Tangent claims to have helped apps generate $50M+ from 10,000+ paywall experiments (self-reported).
- Growth is not only acquisition: most apps leak revenue through an unoptimized onboarding + paywall. Fixing the leak can ~4x revenue with zero extra ad spend.
- Copying a "famous" or a friend's paywall usually fails because user intent, demographics and psychology differ by category (high-intent weight loss app vs low-intent utility app).
- Multi-step paywalls that build up the value proposition over 2-3 screens beat single-screen paywalls by 20-40% on conversion.
- Repetition of "free trial" (5-7 mentions across screens) is risk reversal: it kills purchase anxiety.
- Give users control: a trial toggle (trial vs. discounted no-trial purchase) captures demand from both user types.
- Cover every touchpoint with a paywall placement (onboarding, session start, contextual actions, premium gates, transaction abandonment) and keep experimenting.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Claim app 3-screen paywall: screen 1 = visuals + "Try Claim for free"; screen 2 = reassurance that a reminder is sent before the trial ends; screen 3 = timeline + CTAs. "Free trial" mentioned 5-7 times across the three screens. Result: 34% trial start rate vs. a 15% benchmark.
- Payment card copy: label the annual plan simply "Try it free" with billing details underneath; do not write "annual plan"/"yearly subscription"; CTA again says "Try for free". Deliberately omit the trial length (e.g. "3-day") from the selection card itself (the 3-day timeline is shown in the visual flow above), because "3-day free trial" triggers "what if I forget to cancel?".
- Trial toggle (Rizz app): default shows 7-day free trial at $9.99/week; toggling the trial off gives an immediate purchase at $6.99/week (~$3 / ~30% discount). Logic: a good trial-to-paid rate is ~30%, so a ~30% no-trial discount yields the same revenue per user while ~10% of users choose it and pay upfront, which helps payback period on paid acquisition.
- Transaction-abandonment paywall: discount ONLY the annual plan (never weekly/monthly). Offer the same trial toggle: 80% off with trial, 90% off without trial. Add an undiscounted monthly/weekly option below purely as a price anchor (few pick it).
- Spin-wheel discount reveal (borrowed from e-commerce): on session start, abandonment, or after a trial cancel, an animated wheel lands on e.g. 80% or 90%, user taps "claim". Claimed double-digit conversion lift vs. a static "80% off" display.
- Placements: session-start offer once per day; contextual paywall after every X actions (e.g. after 5 scans in a coin-scanning app); near-limit reminders ("you're almost out of free scans"); transaction abandonment.
- Pricing: Quittr annual shown as $3.33/month (= $39.96/yr). "Random" numbers feel more authentic than $2.99/$4.99 because users have learned to distrust .99 pricing; the extra ~$4/yr widens ad-spend margin.
- Sequence for founders: build all placements first, then test multi-step paywall, then add trial toggle, then gamify discounts, then run continuous copy/discount/screen-order experiments (via Superwall).

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Unnamed entertainment app with 40-50K organic installs/month: install-to-paid went from 1.1% (April) to ~4% (September) with no product or UA changes.
- Claim: 3-screen multi-step paywall, 34% trial start rate.
- Rizz (AI dating conversation starters): trial toggle, $9.99/wk with trial vs $6.99/wk without.
- Quittr: $3.33/month annual pricing.
- Cal AI: cited as a high-intent app (weight loss) whose paywall would not transfer to a low-intent utility app.
- An app with an 81% trial start rate was pushed to 90% with "a few strategic changes".

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- All numbers are self-reported by Vahe / Superwall: $50M+ revenue across clients, 10,000+ experiments; 1.1% -> ~4% install-to-paid in 5 months; multi-step paywall +20-40% conversion; 34% vs 15% trial start benchmark; ~30% good trial-to-paid rate; ~10% of users choose no-trial purchase; 81% -> 90% trial start rate; spin wheel "double-digit" lift; $14.99 beats $15 in tests but "random" prices feel more authentic.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Argues for hiding the trial length on the plan card, whereas Tim Gabe's "10,000 paywalls" video and Cal AI's flow stress explicit "when you will be charged" transparency. (Vahe keeps the timeline visible elsewhere on the screen, so the disagreement is about the selection card, not the whole paywall.)
- Explicitly warns against copying competitor/famous paywalls, which conflicts with "teardown and copy" style advice common in paywall content.
- Recommends never discounting weekly/monthly plans; some other creators discount short plans as entry offers.
- Numbers come from a vendor (Superwall) interviewing a consultant; no third-party verification.
