## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Products that become habits win: "the first-to-mind solution wins"; habit-forming companies "get users to cue themselves to action" instead of paying for marketing.
- The Hooked Model: Trigger -> Action -> Variable Reward -> Investment, repeated until external triggers are replaced by internal triggers (emotions such as boredom, loneliness, uncertainty).
- Variable reward is what separates a hook from a plain feedback loop: predictability kills desire ("the fridge light"), variability creates craving.
- Investment (data, content, followers, reputation, skill) loads the next trigger and makes the product better with use, unlike a sales funnel that ends at purchase.
- Habits are a "super power" that can be used for good or to exploit; builders carry responsibility.
- Per the description, the workshop was aimed at PMs, designers and marketers as "a new way for thinking of the necessary components of changing user behavior".

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Trigger: start with external triggers (email, link, app icon, notification) paired with a clear call to action, then identify the internal trigger (a specific emotion) your product should become the answer to.
- Action: raise motivation and lower effort simultaneously (Fogg behavior model: B = MAT); make the intended action the simplest possible (one click/tap).
- Variable reward, three types from the book: rewards of the tribe (social validation), of the hunt (material/information, e.g. feed scrolling), of the self (mastery, completion).
- Investment: after the reward, ask for a small bit of work that improves the next cycle: invite friends, state preferences, build virtual assets, learn features; store value so the product gets better with use.
- Design check (from the book's "Habit Testing"): identify habitual users, find what they do differently, modify the product to push more users down that path.
- Example walk-through: Barbra sees a relative's photo in Facebook (trigger) -> clicks to Pinterest (action) -> scrolls a mix of relevant and surprising images for 45 minutes (variable reward) -> pins, follows, repins (investment).

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Eyal's "manufacturing desire" contrasts with Yu-kai Chou's warning about black-hat drives: the Hooked model deliberately uses unpredictability (a black-hat drive in Octalysis) as its core engine.
- Eyal himself flags the ethics ("If it can't be used for evil, it's not a super power") and later wrote Indistractable as a counterweight.
