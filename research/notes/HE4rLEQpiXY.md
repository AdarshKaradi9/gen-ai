## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- "Most designers focus on making things look good. But genius designers don't, they focus on making things work brilliantly for the user."
- Treat web design as story, not art: the job is to guide someone to an action; flair (3D graphics, scroll effects) is usually in the way.
- Start from user intent; expand functionality only as the set of intents expands. Fonts, colors and icons change aesthetics but "do very little to change the functionality."
- Respect 30+ years of layout conventions (top-to-bottom, left-to-right, nav at top, obvious CTAs); differentiate with micro-interactions and unique features, not with rearranged layouts.
- "Without content structure, design has very little value": decide what content to show, then structure it to survive real-world content (long names, bright images).
- Animation must add clarity or function (progressive disclosure); design systems exist to give the team a shared language and should be bent only with intention.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Worked example (Airbnb-like vacation rental flow): the primary intent is "search for accommodations," so the search bar is the starting point; augment with destination, number of travelers and dates. Adding a header and background image "does nothing extra." For a browsing intent, show listings; for a more specific intent, add filtering UI.
- Layout defaults: information flows top-to-bottom and left-to-right; navigation at the top; CTAs eye-catching and easy to find. Following these makes it easier to add sections and go responsive.
- Content step 1 (what to show): on a listing card show what users scan first - location, rating, price - plus a short description; details live on the listing page after a click.
- Content step 2 (structure for imperfect content): truncate long destination names; put save/heart icons inside a circle so they keep contrast on bright listing photos; otherwise "you end up with unintended consequences."
- Animation rules: consolidate overflowing nav links into a menu that animates in; when browsing takes priority, shrink the search bar and animate it open on click (progressive disclosure). "Buttons should almost always have a small animation, while scroll jacking effects should be used very sparingly, if ever."
- Prefer a "Load more" button over infinite scroll: more user control and it lets people actually reach the footer.
- Design systems: a lean startup wants something lightweight, flexible, quick to update and easy to scrap; Google's Material is massive because it spans dozens of products and billions of users. The process of defining spacing, type scales and interaction patterns matters more than the artifacts. "You don't break the system by accident, you break it with intention."
- Study the best: Mobbin (sponsor) as a searchable library of real app flows and components; he uses the UI elements section.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Pushes against the aesthetic-first culture (and against the Aesthetic-Usability Effect in the Laws of UX video: he argues looks do not change function, while that principle says looks change perceived usability).
- Advocates "Load more" over infinite scroll, contrary to the feed/engagement-maximizing patterns in social and content apps.
- Explicitly allows breaking conventions when it serves the user better, but only intentionally.
- Sponsored by Mobbin; the "study the best" segment is an ad.
