## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Apps fail for two reasons: not enough installs, and poor retention - "half of all apps are either uninstalled within the first month or never used at all."
- Do research first (audience, competitors, market), then build a strategy in three stages mirroring the buyer journey: awareness, acquisition, retention. Strategy is the big picture; tactics are the actions inside it.
- Awareness tactics: landing page, social media on the platforms your audience uses, content/SEO, ASO, influencers, PR, awards.
- Acquisition tactics: paid ads (TikTok, Meta, Google) with one clear action, free trials, promos/competitions, referral marketing (viral loops), social proof via ratings/reviews.
- Retention tactics: a "killer" onboarding, re-engagement campaigns (in-app, push, SMS, email), listening to feedback to prevent churn, plus analytics/measurement throughout.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Build a dedicated website/landing page with a clear download CTA; the store listing alone competes with millions of apps.
- Content/SEO: pick topics and formats your users engage with (Etsy publishes bite-sized social-media-marketing content because sellers want more sales); guest-blog in niche publications for traffic and credibility.
- ASO: research the right keywords and use them across all content.
- Influencers: not the biggest, but an engaged audience in a niche that matches the app; "nearly two-thirds of consumers trust influencers more than brands" (AppsFlyer's stat, no citation in notes).
- PR: write solid pitches/press releases or hire a professional; pro tip: sign up to HARO (Help A Reporter Out). Apply for awards so you can call the app "award-winning."
- Paid ads: focus each ad on one action; implement app analytics/measurement before scaling so you can double down on working channels and cut the rest.
- Free trials: "most people expect a trial before committing"; a free period yields installs and engagement insights to shorten time-to-value.
- Promos/competitions: discounts, limited-time offers, giveaways, partner prizes, automated contest tools, aimed at bringing back users who did not convert.
- Referral marketing: reward users for referring friends to create viral loops.
- Social proof: "eight out of ten users check an app's ratings and reviews before downloading"; request reviews via in-app messages, notifications or email after the user has used the app; influencer endorsement doubles as social proof.
- Onboarding: user-friendly, value-packed, intuitive, good look and feel. Example: eBay lets users search and browse immediately and does not require an account until checkout.
- Re-engagement: timely in-app messages, push, SMS and email with value-added content and clear CTAs that remind users why they installed. Example: Grubhub (33.8M active users "last year") used ordering data for highly personalised messages.
- Feedback: regular surveys via push/email with rewards for completion, but not too often or it backfires. Set up an analytics/measurement framework to monitor and optimise.
