## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- "On average, apps lose 77% of their users within 3 days" - and the lever is one onboarding choice, not features or marketing.
- Most apps "onboard like government forms": sign-up, permission requests, walkthroughs that have little to do with the product's value.
- The default rule: keep onboarding as short as possible, or better, "surface tangible value as fast as possible."
- The paradox: some of the fastest-growing apps have absurdly long onboardings (Cal AI 20+ steps, Noom 113 screens) and still win.
- Long onboarding is justified only when it buys one of two things: (1) a personalised first experience that would be impossible without the data, or (2) a filter that self-selects paying users.
- Onboarding should "do the work for the user, not work for you."

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Granola's two-screen onboarding: screen 1 = sign in with Google; screen 2 = grant microphone access. "That's it." Value lands during the user's next meeting, something they were already going to do.
- If your product is not heavily dependent on personalisation, do not add more onboarding; cut feature tours and goals wizards.
- If you do run a long quiz (Cal AI style, 20+ questions), make it serve two jobs at once: build the "aha moment" via personalised home screen, and qualify buyers - users who bounce on a 20-step quiz "were less likely to convert anyway"; the ones who finish are "showing you they have deep pain and willingness to pay."
- Decision test: ask whether the extra screens buy either personalisation the user needs to stick or a buyer filter. If neither, delete them.
- (From the related 13-minute video, for context) Tim's wider product funnel: 3-second trust test on visuals (losing it costs 20-30% of new users), "friction sweet spot", "win mapping" (ask for upgrades/ratings/shares right after a win, not at moments of confusion), emotional design; Spark app example surfaced the core card-setup feature within the first five screens.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Deliberately contradicts the "always shorten onboarding" rule by showing Cal AI and Noom winning with long quizzes - but also contradicts Connor Burd (CwHD6Fg-Mjs), who says to always build an extensive onboarding because "90% of users will probably only ever see your onboarding." Tim's position: long only if it earns personalisation or filters buyers.
- Tim concedes the 20-step quiz works for calorie tracking because it is a "deep pain niche where personalization really matters" - i.e. the exception is category-specific.
- Granola's own retention story (per third-party coverage) is credited to simplicity and craft, not onboarding alone; the video treats the two-screen flow as the cause.
