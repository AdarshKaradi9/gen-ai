## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Ship an ugly prototype fast ("vibe code" it in hours), then test with real users immediately; perfection on day one is unnecessary.
- Behavioral-science UI choices (one takeaway at a time, conversational UI, narrative daily "stories", actionable next steps) drive engagement far above traditional finance dashboards.
- Financial anxiety comes from seeing problems without solutions; chunk information and always pair an insight with a next step.
- Building in public on X/Twitter (daily updates, challenges, memes) can take an app from zero audience to thousands of users in about a month with no ad spend.
- AI (ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini) should be used for marketing and content creation, not just inside the product.
- "This is the golden age of building AI-native mobile apps": dynamic, personalized experiences replace static dashboards, and the pattern applies to any self-improvement app.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Prototype: first version built in ~3 hours with Next.js; test with real users immediately.
- "Rule of Six": interview exactly 6 people in the target audience ("Less = not enough data, More = too slow").
- UI: show ONE takeaway at a time (no dense dashboards); conversational UI for personalization; daily financial "stories" modeled on Instagram Stories; card-based UI borrowed from wellness apps; every insight ships with an actionable next step.
- Build-in-public content system: daily build updates, 30-day public challenges (e.g. "3X retention in 30 days"), tech insights, finance memes; organize into content buckets (timestamp 35:14).
- "Tweet-jacking": reply to a viral tweet in your niche with your app link plus a joke feature; then actually build the feature overnight and post about it.
- Meme workflow with ChatGPT: brainstorm ideas from trends -> pick the best -> refine visuals through multiple prompt iterations; analyze your own Twitter to find content angles.
- Emotional design (per Runway profile): replace scolding budget alerts with encouraging "money vibe checks"; treat copy/tone as a feature.
- Timestamps: 03:58 behavioral science principles; 06:13 AI chat / "Peek for X"; 12:05 user testing; 14:00 building/launching; 23:04 building in public; 24:42 post formats; 35:14 content buckets; 38:47 memes with ChatGPT.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Peek (peek.money): AI-native personal finance "coach"/CFO; one tab at launch, later integrations with thousands of brokerages/banks; "cute mascot that roasts you" feature built overnight after a viral reply.
- Instagram Stories (inspiration for daily check-ins), wellness apps (card UI), messaging apps (conversational patterns).
- Tools: Next.js, Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT.
- Sherry Jiang's background: Google (behavioral science / launched Google Pay), previously Bluejay Finance (stablecoin, ~$3M raised, pivoted after crypto winter).

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Self-reported (show notes): users open Peek "7X MORE than traditional finance apps"; first tweet got ~500 views; the tweet she replied to had 6.5M views; "thousands of users within a month".
- Self-reported via Runway Ventures profile: #1 on Product Hunt within 30 days of launch; 5,000 users in 30 days with zero paid marketing; later 10,000+ users; CAC under $1 via niche TikTok creators; Iterative accelerator invested $275K.
- Self-reported (Peek substack, early beta): weekly active users grew 10x from 17 to 194 across the beta.
- No third-party verification of any of these numbers.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Argues against feature-rich dashboards (the standard fintech pattern) in favor of a single daily takeaway.
- "Only six user interviews" contradicts the common "talk to 20+ users" advice (the Runway piece separately recommends 20 interviews).
- Title says "viral in 30 days"; sources support "thousands of users within a month" but give no precise download counts.
