## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Ania Wysocka (non-technical, no business background) turned her own panic attack in 4th year of university into Rootd, a panic-attack and anxiety-relief app: 4M+ downloads and $1M+ revenue since 2019 (self-reported).
- Validate by reading competitor app reviews for gaps, then ship an MVP and listen to the first few hundred users.
- Growth came from three organic channels: being genuinely helpful in social comments, cold-pitching journalists (Cosmopolitan, Women's Health, Time), and ASO built on the words users write in reviews.
- The core "aha" feature (a red SOS panic button that walks you through an attack) has not changed since the MVP; everything else was added over years.
- The most important metric for a mental-health app is rating/reviews (held at 4.8/5), not revenue or downloads.
- Bootstrapped, no employees (contractors only), built alongside a 4-day-a-week job for ~2.5-3 years.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Validation: download every competing app, read reviews, look for a repeated unmet complaint (here: "what is a panic attack?" and "how do I get through one right now").
- Non-technical build: sketch flows in a notebook -> wireframes in Photoshop/Illustrator -> an agency was unaffordable -> a student developer built the MVP in a few months using her savings. Design principle: keep everything extremely simple because the user is overwhelmed when they open it.
- MVP scope: panic-attack walkthrough button + simple breathing tool + lessons on anxiety.
- Social tactic: do not lead with "download my app"; answer the person's question using content from a Rootd lesson (e.g. explain why anxiety spiked on a run), then leave a link.
- PR tactic: find journalists who cover mental health, reach them via LinkedIn or email with a pitch; put your own name and story on it ("once you put your name to a story it resonates better"); most never reply but occasional big features land.
- ASO loop: product page keywords -> user downloads and uses app -> user writes a review using the same words -> reuse those words. Ship a release every week to signal active maintenance.
- 2025 playbook: (1) build a product that delivers what the store page promises, (2) read every review, (3) optimise store presence continuously, (4) partner strategically (wellness orgs, B2B contracts, therapists and psychologists recommending the app).
- Monetisation (from eBizFacts, 2023): 7-day free trial then ~$60/year; company/bulk plans are a large revenue chunk. From RevenueCat Sub Club summary: low-volume, highly relevant keywords beat high-traffic ones; repeated submissions eventually won Apple Editor's Choice; a paywall at the start of onboarding reportedly raised revenue ~5x with no negative feedback (search snippet from the Sub Club episode, not the Starter Story video).
- UI: mascot "Ron" greets you; red SOS button asks "feel okay in the moment" vs "face it head-on" and walks through prompts to activate the parasympathetic nervous system; deep-breathing tool; lessons; journal; 100+ visualisations and sleep sounds; simple distraction games (added after years of requests).

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Rootd (the app itself). Press: Cosmopolitan, Women's Health, Time magazine (all from cold email). Apple Editor's Choice (RevenueCat source). B2B: therapy groups, psychologist offices, corporate plans.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Self-reported: 4M+ downloads, $1M+ revenue, launched 2019; year 1 ~10K downloads, year 2 ~100K, year 3 ~1M; rating 4.8/5; users feel better from a panic attack in under 2 minutes (usage data); thousands of users try any new feature within a week; went full-time ~2.5-3 years in once revenue could sustain her for 1+ year.
- Third-party: eBizFacts (Feb 2023) - 1M+ Google Play downloads, 7-day trial then $60/year, crossed $1M revenue per MFM podcast; RevenueCat/Sub Club - 2M downloads, no funding, turned down a $40K offer; episode published Aug 31 2025.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Growth was slow and compounding (10K -> 100K -> 1M over three years) - no viral moment, unlike the Playkit/Macaron playbooks.
- Prioritises rating/reviews over revenue; most monetisation videos put revenue first.
- Warns about burnout and recommends celebrating small wins, in contrast to Altman's "never take your foot off the gas."
- Download totals differ by source/date (2M in 2023 RevenueCat, 4M in 2025 Starter Story) - consistent with growth, but treat as self-reported.
