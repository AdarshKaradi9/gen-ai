## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Social platforms are deliberately designed for attention and habitual use because the business model is ad impressions per minute of attention.
- The techniques exploit predictable human psychology rather than merely making apps enjoyable: dopamine is about anticipation and pursuit, not just enjoyment.
- Feeds work like slot machines: rewards (likes, interesting posts) arrive unpredictably, so users keep refreshing/scrolling in anticipation.
- Algorithms adapt to each user to prolong sessions (compared to modern adaptive slot machines); outrage/disgust content gets amplified because it drives engagement (X cited).
- Apps strip out friction and stopping points so continuing is always the path of least resistance.
- Remedy: add friction back; use social media intentionally rather than rejecting it.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Platform tactics described: red notification badges that feel urgent; "recapture" notifications after absence about activity you missed even if irrelevant; TikTok auto-plays a video the instant the app opens; autoplay + indefinite feed; interaction controls placed within thumb reach; on Android, swiping to leave TikTok can trigger another video and require a second swipe to exit; endless feeds compared to casino layouts designed to discourage leaving.
- Reward mechanics: Sapolsky monkey experiments, dopamine rises in anticipation of a treat and rises further when the reward becomes unpredictable; refreshing a feed = pulling a slot lever.
- User counter-tactics: audit notifications and disable any that pull you in without useful info; disable autoplay wherever possible; use iPhone Scheduled Summary to batch notifications; move distracting apps off the home screen (even one page over makes opening less automatic).
