## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Most apps fail not because of poor execution but because the idea was never validated; founders either have no ideas or too many and no way to choose.
- A four-step framework, drawn from building Puff Count and Posted: (1) identify the idea, (2) validate it, (3) apply a marketing mindset when choosing it, (4) make sure it is feasible to build.
- Solve real, painful problems rather than chasing novel but impractical ideas; pain makes people willing to pay and makes marketing simpler.
- Marketing is ~95% of an app's success: a mediocre app with great marketing can win, a great app nobody can market will not. As AI tools and freelancers lower the barrier to building, marketing becomes the moat.
- Existing competitors making money are a green flag, not a red one; use them to size the market.
- Keep the product simple, build fast, launch early, iterate on feedback; do not perfect every detail before release.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Step 1, find problems: look in the evergreen problem categories - health, wealth, relationships, status, convenience, freedom. Study the top-grossing charts to see which category each hit belongs to (Tinder = relationships, Instagram = status, MyFitnessPal = health) and go after niches with proven demand.
- Prefer controversial or hot-button problem spaces: Puff Count benefited from vaping being a controversial topic that already generated heavy social-media engagement, which made TikTok content easy to get traction with.
- Check Google Trends for the concept; rising search interest over time indicates demand, falling interest is a warning.
- Browse app marketplaces Flippa and Acquire to see which apps are selling, what revenue they make and what makes them work; copy the patterns behind high-revenue listings.
- Step 2, validate with research tools: ViralAdLibrary.com (his own tool, linked in the description) to view viral TikTok/Instagram videos and ads for competing apps, sortable by views and ad type, to see what content already resonates; Sensor Tower to read top charts and competitor revenue - healthy competition (e.g. MyFitnessPal, Tinder in lifestyle) confirms a monetisable niche.
- Step 3, marketing mindset: before committing, search TikTok and Reddit for videos and threads about the problem; high engagement signals demand and gives you ready-made content angles. Ask whether the idea is marketable from day one.
- Step 4, feasibility: can you realistically build it, and can it stay simple? He cites QR-code reader apps generating significant revenue as proof that simple wins. Build quickly, launch early, collect user feedback, adapt.
- Offer in the video: an "app founder group call" for networking with founders earning substantial monthly revenue (link in description).
