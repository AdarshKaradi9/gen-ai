## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- UGC-style organic TikTok is "the next biggest distribution channel" because it replicates word of mouth: "people trust word of mouth more than anything else, UGC replicates that at scale."
- Julia alone, with no influencers and no paid ads, scaled an app to ~500k downloads by posting a couple of times a day and replying to comments (self-reported).
- The content must not look like an ad. Accounts should look like a real person in the target audience (profile picture = a face, bio = the ICP's self-description, e.g. "a woman learning Spanish in 70 days"), and often the app name is never said in the video - conversion happens in the comments.
- "The video serves as a landing page for communities to form"; controlling the comment section matters more than the video itself.
- Formats die fast because consumers learn to spot ads; "hook + demo" and "target audience + pain point = solution" used to work and now you need storytelling, multi-part series, slideshows, talking videos.
- UGC works for B2C utility/value-add apps (relationships, money, career/school); it does not work for B2B SaaS or crypto - "don't waste your time."
- It is a long game: expect ~2 weeks of flops per creator before week 3-4 virality; most founders quit too early.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Step 0: name your user. Write one specific persona (name, age, pains, content they consume); the blog says this takes 15-30 minutes.
- Warm up a fresh account before posting (2-3 days per the blog): search the questions your user would search ("how to make friends in a new city", "events in NYC") - not your product category - like/save/comment so TikTok classifies the account as that user.
- Harvest vocabulary: screenshot captions/comments in the niche, paste into ChatGPT, ask for the slang, emojis, hashtags and sounds the community uses, and mirror them (blog example: "bruh" and the skull emoji for a high-school notes app).
- Replicate what already works: find competitors' and lifestyle creators' most viral videos in the niche and recreate them with your product plugged in; iterate off the comments.
- Three formats (blog): (1) hook & demo - face first with a pain-point hook ("How come nobody told me this is how everyone's getting straight A's?") then product demo; (2) long text - a 6-second face clip with group-chat-style text overlay, often no product name, highest-converting because the reveal happens in comments; (3) talking style - story first, product woven in as a detail (Quizlet coffee-shop story).
- Posting ritual: scroll the FYP ~2 minutes and engage before each post (to pass the 100-500 view threshold); post 2-3x/day, at least 2 hours apart; never dump drafts at once.
- Comment operations: reply to every comment; DM everyone who comments ("thanks for the like - by the way I'm building this app that does XYZ"); plant comments; make video replies to top comments (TikTok pushes those replies to the same viewers, so they can be product-heavy and still convert); PlayKit sends its 150-200 creators to flood viral videos' comments - only on videos that already have lots of comments, and not abused, or TikTok flags it.
- Match the App Store listing to the viral messaging: screenshots and copy should echo the exact words from the video so the funnel feels seamless.
- Failure handling: PlayKit posts 15x/week per creator (3/day, 5 days); first two weeks (~300 videos across clients) are testing; weeks 3-4 are when virality starts. If nothing works after 30 intentional days, suspect product or strategy.
- When a video hits 100k+: repost the same message repeatedly with small tweaks (indoor/outdoor, different product screen) until it stops working; do product-focused video replies; mine comments for new pain-point hooks.
- Timeline (blog): weeks 1-2 low-thousands views; week 3 first 10k+ video; week 4 three or more breakouts; week 5 first truly viral video; week 8 multiple 1M+ videos.
- Cross-posting: TikTok converts better and is where to spend effort; Instagram gets more views but lower conversion (slightly better for male-facing products); auto-schedule the same videos to Instagram.
- Hiring creators: post a TikTok asking for creators, plus outbound; hire people who are TikTok-native, have gone viral before, look like the target user (college students for Gen Z, stay-at-home moms for family apps; 50+ men are hard to source - use influencers/paid there). Do it yourself first, then hire executors, then a strategist/manager once you have 3-5+ creators.
- Pay: industry standard $125-175/week flat for 15 posts/week plus bonuses of $100 at 100k views, $500 at 500k, $1,000 at 1M+.
- Before scaling creators, make sure paid conversion in the funnel is sustainable; model "this many views -> this many dollars" rather than throwing money at the wall.
- Tracking: PlayKit built UGCTrackr (ugctrackr.com) to see who posted, missed posts, approvals and pay; avoid Google Sheets + VA counting.
- Blog comparison: paid Meta installs at this stage cost $2-5 each; the organic system teaches you the pain point, user language and converting format for free.
