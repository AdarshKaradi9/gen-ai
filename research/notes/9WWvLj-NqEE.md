## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Kelechi Onyeama, 22, from Nigeria, arrived in the US with $100 and within 12 months built two apps (Social Wizard and Clean Eats) that generated $1.5M revenue (self-reported) and 700,000+ downloads across App Store and Play Store.
- "In 2025 product development is not enough anymore. If you don't find a way to tell people about your product, then you might as well not even build a product." Distribution is the whole game.
- His distribution edge is a "streamer strategy": paying very small, "degenerate" micro-streamers (Fortnite/reaction content, not dating niche) to recreate a proven viral format that demos the app in-context.
- "Distribution is always a show don't tell game" – demo the product doing the thing rather than selling it.
- Virality is deterministic, not luck: enough volume of content (he describes 100 videos/day = 700/week) means one hits.
- Figure out what you're actually selling; for both apps it was "confidence", not texting help or nutrition data.
- Keep the tech stack simple and shippable; margins >90% after Apple's cut because infra is $1-2K/month and almost all spend is marketing.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Pricing for Social Wizard: weekly subscription ~$10/week, monthly ~$20, yearly ~$80 (weekly plan priced so monthly is only 2x weekly). App Store listing shows a 3-day free trial.
- Viral format: the feature where a user screenshots a girl's Instagram story, uploads it to the app, picks a tone ("Flirty"), hits "Cook up," and gets reply lines. The creator video opens with a pretty girl's picture (a "pause" hook), the streamer says "I don't know what to do... we're about to use the Wizard app," generates the line, uses it. "By this point, we've sold everyone what this product is. They don't even need to finish the clip."
- Creator sourcing: first make content yourself to find the format that "kind of works," then go to micro-streamers who have had maybe 100K-200K-view videos but no viral moment, because they hit the 16-24 male audience. Pay example: ~$120 for one video that hit 2M views and "brought in tens of thousands of dollars."
- Outreach is a volume game: expect to reach out to 100 creators to get one to work. Once you have one creator's video, use it as social proof to recruit more.
- Three-step playbook: (1) Check whether the niche already has a large volume of content being produced. If yes, go to mid-size to large creators; if no, make content yourself. Never outsource this learning. (2) Produce content: start with 3 videos/day across 2 TikTok accounts (e.g., 2 on one, 1 on the other); after 1-2 weeks scale to 10, then 20 per day. Use TikTok, not Instagram, to find viral formats because new accounts go viral more easily on TikTok. (3) Scale: reproduce the winning format across as many creators as possible; adjacent niches are fine (health app -> cooking creators).
- Use creators who hit your audience, not your topic: the streamers made Fortnite / "people breaking into McDonald's" / reaction content.
- Reframe benefit to a visceral outcome: for Clean Eats, "don't eat this food product cuz it's going to mess up my skin" converts better than "healthy alternatives."
- Stack: React Native (with ChatGPT help), NestJS backend deployed anywhere (Render/Heroku), Firebase DB, Mixpanel analytics. "Always optimize for a stack that would get your product out there as soon as possible."
- Idea validation: the first signal was high-school friends actually caring when he handed them the script-based prototype at homecoming.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Social Wizard: AI texting/social-skills coach for guys aged 16-24; reply suggestions for DMs; story-screenshot feature; launched early 2024.
- Clean Eats: scan a food barcode, get told how it affects skin or weight; core market women 18-28; hit ~$10K revenue in 2 weeks; exited ~4 months later to a UK-based company.
- Casp (Caspi): his earlier failed group photo-sharing social app (few thousand users, 20,000+ pictures) where the Social Wizard idea originated.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Contrary to the "TikTok organic" playbook, he says paid micro-streamer collabs beat both paid ads and UGC agencies: "You can't get this on paid ad. You can't get this on UGC."
- He explicitly tells founders NOT to outsource content/distribution early even if they have capital.
- Recommends going to creators outside your niche (adjacent audiences), unlike typical influencer-matching advice.
- Says education is an overlooked niche while "everyone is building apps in health and fitness and lifestyle" — yet his own second app was health/fitness.
- The host intro says "it's not TikTok," but the strategy is micro-streamer videos distributed largely on TikTok.
- "Haven't spent a dollar on marketing" conflicts mildly with "90% of your budget on marketing" — the former refers to the last year after the app was on autopilot.
