## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Thomas Kim ("Your Average Tech Bro") and his co-founder grew Yorby (spelled "Yorbie" in the notes), a social-media-marketing app for businesses, from 0 to 10,000 users and $0 to ~$4,000 revenue in 30 days, almost entirely via organic short-form video on TikTok and Instagram - no paid ads (self-reported).
- The engine was one repeatable format: "hook and demo" videos fronted by an AI-generated person, later supplemented with human UGC creators and LinkedIn outreach.
- Organic social is "a venture capital play": run many accounts/creators, expect 9 of 10 to fail, and let the one outlier drive most growth.
- The algorithm does not care whether content is AI- or human-made, only whether it is good or spam.
- Capture emails from day one even before you have an email strategy; owned channels compound.
- Founder-led marketing is the moat now that AI makes software cheap; execution speed beats everything ("more is more").

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Video structure: open with an AI-generated face looking excited/surprised to stop the scroll; deliver a curiosity hook framing a problem (example: "POV, you finally understand why creators stopped using ChatGPT"); cut immediately to a product demo that resolves it.
- Hooks were brainstormed with Yorby itself (the product has a viral-content database and an AI "content studio" that remixes proven formats into your niche while keeping the format's "viral soul") - the team ate their own dog food.
- The business co-founder ran all marketing; growth began accidentally while "warming up the account" during holiday travel, then several videos went viral back-to-back.
- Scale with people once revenue arrives: hire human UGC creators to buy back time; going viral created inbound creator interest, solving sourcing.
- Run AI videos and human creators simultaneously; treat each account as a portfolio bet.
- LinkedIn as a second channel (~20% of growth) for team/enterprise buyers with longer sales cycles; TikTok/Instagram (~80%) for volume and solo creators.
- Collect emails at sign-up from day one.
- Infra warning: backend built for ~20 users fell over at 10,000; scaling the database is deferred to a follow-up video.
- Sponsor integration: Brevo (email capture/marketing) with a 50% discount code TECHBRO50.
- (From the X post) 10 accounts -> 9 fail, 1 drives 90% of growth; smaller "die-hard" audiences may convert better than big generic ones; "code every day and market every day."

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)

- 0 -> 10,000 users and $0 -> $4,000+ revenue in the first full month, mainly solo and team plans (self-reported).
- ~80% of growth from TikTok/Instagram, ~20% from LinkedIn (self-reported split).
- 25k+ users in 4 months (self-reported, later X post).

- No retention, conversion or CAC data; the 10k figure is users, not paying users.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Uses AI-generated faces openly and argues the algorithm is indifferent, consistent with An Nayal (pb4JIH8FubE) and contrary to the "authentic UGC only" stance of PlayKit's Julia (9r4kS9zZj9s), who says audiences must not detect an ad.
- Growth was partly accidental ("warming up the account"), so the "30 days" is a post-hoc framing.
- Revenue ($4k) is small relative to users (10k) - a freemium/low-ARPU pattern unlike the subscription-first Cal AI/Payout stories.
- Technical debt bit immediately (database failures at 10k) - a caveat for "ship fast" advice.
- Sponsored segment (Brevo) overlaps with the "capture emails" lesson.
