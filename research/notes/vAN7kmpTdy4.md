## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Onboarding is not the login or account-setup screens; it is "the structured UX flow of screens, interactions, and prompts designed to introduce a user to a product or feature", and it is not only for first-time users.
- Onboarding is "a product's first line of defense against the delete button".
- The golden rule: "You can't onboard someone you do not understand." No illustrations or tooltips compensate for not knowing the user.
- A four-step strategy: define user types (and who the product is NOT for); work backward from what success looks like per type; align onboarding with the business model; test and tweak one change at a time.
- Three mistakes: "Too Many Steps", "Wall of Text", "No Escape Room".
- Winning formula: Personalization ("This is just for me"), Progressive disclosure ("Just-in-Time rule"), Emotional engagement ("Make it feel good").

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Segment users at signup with limited-option dropdowns rather than empty form fields (Notion: team / personal / school, then role and use case: wiki, project management, company goals), then drop into the workspace with walkthrough tooltips.
- Define the non-negotiable first step (Acorns: link bank via Plaid) and reassure about security; reveal subscription tiers only after the success moment; warn upfront if a flow must be completed in one sitting.
- Mark paid features clearly (Fotor's diamond icon) instead of an ambiguous freemium wall (VSCO: unclear what is free vs paid).
- Open with a short classification modal (Fotor: content-creation experience, intended use, editing frequency) to personalize immediately.
- Fix drop-off by changing one variable at a time: reduce clicks, change copy, add a visual cue; then measure.
- Use action-based triggers/conditionals (e.g. Intercom) to deliver guidance just-in-time rather than front-loaded.
- Use micro-rewards, progress bars and subtle animations for a "tiny dopamine hit".
- Always provide a skip option; offer "choose your own journey" for confident users and more hand-holding for others.
- Research method: use Mobbin to browse 200+ real onboarding screen flows instead of installing apps (sponsor).
- Part 2 catalog of 14 patterns with UI components: First Look (carousel/video + close button), Product Tour (modal overlays, progress indicator, Next/Skip), Walkthrough Beacons (pulsing dots + popovers), Empty State (illustration + CTA like "Create your first board"), Personalization (selection chips, progress bar), Checklists (checkboxes, completion %, badge), Action-Oriented (bold CTA "Send your first message now", celebratory micro-animation), Goal Setting (ring/bar progress, motivational notifications), plus six more.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Notion (use-case segmentation), Acorns (bank linking via Plaid, then pricing tiers), VSCO (paywall immediately after signup, unclear free/paid split, "not so great"), Fotor (classification modal, diamond icons on paid features, "much better"), Mobbin (research tool), Intercom (conditional in-app guidance). Part 2 cites Slack, Canva, Figma, Trello, Duolingo, Reddit, QuickBooks, Shopify, Loom, Grammarly and others as pattern examples.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Criticizes immediate post-signup paywalls (VSCO) as "catfishing", whereas paywall-focused videos in this set argue hard/onboarding paywalls convert best; her reconciliation is that paywall placement must match the business model and come after value is understood.
- Says long flows are acceptable for finance products (Acorns) even though "too many steps" is mistake #1, i.e. step count should match user need, not a fixed rule.
