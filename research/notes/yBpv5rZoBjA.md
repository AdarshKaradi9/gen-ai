# How To Scientifically Design Addictive Apps
- URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA | Channel: Tim Gabe | Views: 63326 | Length: 891s (~15 min)
- Sources used: https://sozai.app/transcript/scientifically-design-addictive-apps/ (full caption transcript + summary)
- Confidence: high (full transcript read)

## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Badges, streak counters and leaderboards are "the entry-level version" of gamification; the apps users "genuinely cannot stop using" run on three stacked psychological mechanisms where "the gamification is the product."
- Mechanism 1, the craving machine: variable-ratio reinforcement (Skinner) creates craving, not pleasure; mostly-predictable reward systems with controlled surprise keep users in a chase state.
- Mechanism 2, the infinite game: loss aversion (losses hurt ~2x as much as equivalent gains) plus a refusal to create terminal "done" states; streaks should compound into something tangible you can lose.
- Mechanism 3, the invisible scoreboard: social comparison turns progression into visible status, which makes the first two mechanisms irreversible because quitting becomes "publicly admitting you stopped."
- Human connection plus gamification is a moat AI cannot copy.
- Tim is explicit that these mechanisms are "morally complicated" and can "trap people in loops they didn't really sign up for."

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Craving machine takeaways: (1) keep most of the reward system predictable and transparent, but add an element of surprise (an unexpected bonus reward, a milestone that arrives early); (2) you are adding controlled surprise, not making everything random; (3) track ONE system users obsess over (Finch's six-trait personality profile, League's league points): "one visible measure of total progress creates more engagement than 20 scattered badges."
- Finch's craving loop in detail: pick a destination for your bird from a rotating set (3 options free, 9 paid); the bird adventures there daily "potentially for weeks"; each location has 15-20 unique discoveries it might bring back; some days amazing, some days nothing; the bird's personality (confidence, curiosity, resilience across six traits) evolves partly outside your control; "the app explicitly says your bird is creating their own personality."
- League of Legends: visible ranked ladder plus hidden MMR that calibrates toward a ~50% win rate; "you climb 100 points one week, drop 200 the next, and then climb 150 again. The unpredictability is the craving."
- Infinite game takeaways: (1) audit your product for done states ("if a user can complete your app, you have a ceiling on retention"); (2) build streaks that compound into something, not numbers that reset; (3) if you have levels, consider periodic resets that force re-engagement while preserving earned status (League resets rank but keeps honor levels and cosmetics).
- Sips App "diamond streak" system built for client Freecash: streaks unlock diamonds (first diamond at 7 days, another at 42 days); miss a day and you risk losing accumulated diamonds; the streak freeze must be earned; the diamond path is finite but far longer than a weekly reset. Contrast: a Duolingo streak is "a single thread... one counter, one number."
- Peloton's never-capping metrics: total classes, total miles, total output ("a user at 500 classes isn't stopping when 600, 700, 1,000 are all theoretically reachable"), live leaderboards ranking watt output against thousands of riders in real time, monthly challenges, instructors calling out top performers by name.
- Invisible scoreboard takeaways: (1) make achievements visible to others ("social visibility transforms personal goals into status goals"); (2) build community dynamics, not just features; (3) "design your metrics as a mirror, not just a report": when users see stats they should immediately think about how they compare to others.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Duolingo: XP after each lesson (predictable), single-thread streak with streak freeze; the baseline everyone copies.
- Finch (self-care bird app): 14M+ downloads, 675,000 ratings at 4.9 stars on iOS, Apple Editor's Choice; variable-reward adventures and evolving personality traits.
- League of Legends: hidden MMR calibrating to ~50% win rate, seasonal ladder resets, 130M+ monthly players.
- Peloton: 90% annual subscriber retention attributed to community, leaderboards and parasocial instructors (Cody Rigsby, Ally Love), not the $1,700 bike.
- Strava: 180M+ registered athletes; segment leaderboards so powerful that in 2025 and early 2026 Strava deleted 3.9 million activities because users uploaded e-bike rides as regular rides to climb rankings "for nothing but bragging rights."
- Freecash (Sips App client): diamond streak system.
- Sips App / ZipZap: Tim's agency (free design strategy calls pitched twice).

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Explicitly pushes against the Duolingo-as-gold-standard assumption: Duolingo's XP and single streak are "decoration," not "architecture."
- Pushes against "more badges = more engagement": one obsessive metric beats 20 badges.
- Pushes against pure randomness: most of the system should be transparent; surprise is a seasoning.
- Ethical caveat is stated up front and at the end: "I'm showing you the architecture. What you build with it is your call."
- The video is also an agency pitch (two CTAs for free strategy calls), and retention statistics are presented without sources.

## Memorable framings (quotable one-liners or named frameworks, verbatim if available)
- Named mechanisms: "the craving machine," "the infinite game," "the invisible scoreboard."
- "This is not pleasure. This is craving. Variable ratio reinforcement doesn't make you happy. It keeps your brain in a constant chase for the next hit."
- "The most addictive apps never let you finish... There is no winning in these systems. There's only more."
- "If a user can complete your app, you have a ceiling on retention."
- "Not a vending machine, but a system where the next hit could come on the very next action or 10 actions from now."
- "Quitting stops being about losing progress. It becomes about publicly admitting you stopped. The social layer turns engagement into identity."
- "The scoreboard creates the competition. The human connection makes it matter. You need both."
- "It's not what these apps reward, it's how they structure the reward. The difference between a Duolingo streak and a full progression addiction system is the difference between decoration and architecture."
