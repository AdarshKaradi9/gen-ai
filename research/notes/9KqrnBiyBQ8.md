## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Duolingo reaches ~$500M/yr revenue and a $7.7B valuation while keeping lessons free; only ~9% of users pay and ads are <8% of revenue.
- The paying minority (~8M subscribers) produces ~80% of revenue; a committed minority funds the free majority.
- Growth engine = gamification (lives, XP, streaks, progress bars, leaderboards) + relentless A/B testing + AI personalization.
- Push notifications are a science: Duo the owl's reminders are tuned by a multi-armed "bandit" algorithm; the "passive-aggressive" guilt notification alone lifted retention ~3%.
- Monetization headroom: more downloads than Babbel but only 2-3x the revenue, so new premium tiers (Duolingo Max with AI role-play at ~$30/month) are being tested without compromising free access.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Game loop: hearts/lives (run out = wait or pay), XP per lesson, daily streak counter, progress bars, leaderboards with friends; celebratory animations/fanfare when streaks extend; quests and limited-time events; streak "gambling" to earn currency (Sensor Tower list).
- Monetization timeline: ads introduced 2017; subscription tier (ad-free + unlimited lives) launched at $10/month; Duolingo Max ~$30/month with AI role-play bots.
- Notification tactics: Duo reminders escalate; the famous lapsed-user message after ~a week: "It looks like reminders aren't working. We'll stop sending them for the time being." (Cem Kansu, CPO, calls it "the passive aggressive reminder"). A sleeping/recovering bandit algorithm picks which notification to send each lapsing user, balancing exploration and exploitation.
- Testing cadence: hundreds of A/B tests live at any time; ~13 billion exercises/week feed the "Birdbrain" AI model that adjusts lesson difficulty per learner.
- Streak design (Cohen/WSJ): tell users their consecutive-day count, make loss salient, aim to get users opening the app every day rather than spending long sessions.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- ~34M DAU; $7.7B valuation; ~$500M annual revenue; ~9% pay; <8% revenue from ads; ~8M subscribers = ~80% of revenue (WSJ video, third-party reporting of company figures).
- 3% retention lift from the "reminders aren't working" notification (company-reported via WSJ).
- 13B exercises/week; hundreds of concurrent A/B tests (company-reported).
- WSJ article: >30M DAU, >70% of DAU hold a 7-day+ streak, ~5M have a 365-day+ streak (company figures via WSJ).
- Sensor Tower (third-party): churn fell to 37% (2022) from 47% (2020); power users (15+ days/month) rose from 20% to >30%; >25% of usage between 9-11pm, consistent with streak-saving; 20% of reviews praise fun aspects vs 6% for competitors.
- Prices: $10/month subscription at launch of the tier; Max ~$30/month.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Contrary to "hard paywall / convert early" indie advice, Duolingo keeps 91% of users free and prioritizes DAU growth over near-term subscription conversion.
- Critics (noted in secondary sources) argue streak obsession can displace actual language learning; the video frames this as an open question about effectiveness.
- The "guilt" notification and loss-framed streaks are the same mechanics the Vox/Mrwhosetheboss videos criticize.
