## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Building an app is now easy (AI, "spin one up in 4 hours"); distribution is the bottleneck and "the skill of creating content is the number one skill right now" (Pat Walls' closing line).
- People do not download apps from ads anymore; they download what their feed tells them everyone is already using. Organic UGC on TikTok/Instagram from brand-new accounts can get the first 1,000 downloads with $0 ad spend.
- Five steps: (1) painfully specific user persona, (2) warm up the account 2-3 days, (3) make one of three video formats, (4) a strict posting ritual, (5) track data and recreate winners.
- Optimise the first weeks for comments like "what app is this?" not views; conversion happens in the comment section.
- Most videos flop; that is the data-collection phase. Expect roughly 8 weeks from zero to the first 1M+ view videos.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Persona (15-30 min): name, age, pain points, wants, content they already watch. Not "students" but "female biology students cramming for finals."
- Warm-up (2-3 days before posting): search what the user searches ("how to study for biology"), never your product category ("AI note taker" only surfaces competitor ads). Like, save, comment, share like that person. Keep a table of slang/emojis seen (e.g. "bruh", skull emoji for a high-school note app) and reuse in copy.
- Three formats: (a) Hook + demo - face first with a pain-point story hook ("How come nobody told me this is how everyone's getting straight A's?"), then product demo; do not name the app in the first second. (b) Long text - a ~6-second clip of your face with group-chat-style text overlay; usually does not name the product; goal is "wait, how did you do this?" comments, then reply with the app name - highest-converting format. (c) Talking style - story first, product woven in (Quizlet creator telling a coffee-shop story while flashcards run).
- Posting ritual: scroll the For You page ~2 minutes and engage immediately before posting (signals human, gets past the 100-500 view floor); post 2-3 times/day at the start; space posts at least 2 hours apart; never dump the drafts folder at once; never re-upload the identical file (TikTok suppresses duplicates) - re-record or edit.
- Timeline: weeks 1-2 low thousands of views, optimise for "link?" comments; week 3 first 10K+ breakout; week 4 three or more breakouts; week 5 first viral; week 8 multiple 1M+ videos (Playkit's typical curve, self-reported).
- Cost comparison: Meta ads at this stage cost ~$2-5 per install; the organic loop teaches pain point, language and format for free.
- From Julia's written playbooks (not necessarily in the video): Playkit's average CPM $3.87 vs ~$20 Meta and ~$119 micro-influencer (self-reported); 1% of videos drove ~70% of views in an AI-music-app campaign, and 0.3% of Playkit's 2025 videos drove 64.6% of 755M views; "5-video test": a format is repeatable only when five executions each pass 100K views; split weekly production into thirds (scale winners / variants / new tests); at 25K views reply to top comments, at 50K share with the creator network and film comment-reply videos (one reply video hit 4.7M views); warm new accounts with 1-2 non-branded videos a few days before any product-forward post and ramp 1, 2, 3 videos/day to avoid "shadowbans"; creators paid a guaranteed weekly rate with cumulative performance tiers.
- Playkit engine (Suzanna interview): 10 creators per campaign, each on a brand-new account, 15 videos/week each, 600 videos over a 4-week sprint; week 1 ~1,000 views avg, week 2 first 10K+, week 3 2-3 breakouts, week 4 compounding.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Playkit (Julia Pintar, co-founder): UGC agency for consumer apps; clients named include Substack, Quizlet, Cash App, Flo Health; one video reportedly produced $500K revenue for a client.
- Quizlet (talking-style coffee-shop story), doop.com (hook + demo), an AI note-taker for students (persona/warm-up example), an "AI music app" campaign (1,000 videos -> 647K views until one broke out; 110M+ views across 5,305 videos), an app showing who your partner recently followed on Instagram (curiosity hook).

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Self-reported by Julia/Playkit (figures vary by date): 1B views / 11M downloads (video description); 1B+ views / 12M downloads in 18 months (profitablefounder); 1.5B+ views / 14M+ downloads (Julia's X article); 180M views / 1M+ downloads over seven months (Suzanna, 2025); 1,500 creator applications, ~20-25 accepted.
- Third-party platform stats quoted by Suzanna: TikTok 1.59B MAU (Feb 2025), ~95 min/day vs 62 min Instagram.
- Timelines (4-5 weeks to 1,000 downloads; 8 weeks to 1M-view videos) are Playkit's typical experience, not controlled data.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Julia is explicit that organic UGC works best for Gen Z / platform-native audiences and products with a visual "viral component" (swiping, scanning, big numbers, transformation, drama); for older segments she suggests TikTok ads instead.
- "Marketing can't fix a bad product" - views and downloads without retention.
- The playbook relies on many brand-new accounts and volume posting, which conflicts with "build one authentic brand account" advice and sits close to TikTok's spam/duplicate rules (Playkit's own newsletter documents account-level restrictions since ~March/April 2026).
- Numbers for Playkit's track record are inconsistent across sources (11M vs 12M vs 14M downloads).
