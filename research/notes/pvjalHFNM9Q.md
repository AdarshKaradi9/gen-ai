## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Reddit is "by far the best channel to get users on your products" for early-stage SaaS: Roman (co-founder, Goji Berry AI) went from $0 to ~$30k MRR in 4-6 months with Reddit as the main acquisition channel.
- Reddit is for the first 10-100 customers and product feedback, not for scale: "Reddit is not a platform to get scale. It's not a platform to hit 10,000 MR, 20,000 MR... Get your first 10 customers, your first 100 customers, probably not your first thousand."
- Posts must be stories with proof, never direct product plugs: "never putting your SAS directly in the post. You can trigger curiosity by highlighting it."
- The invisible benefit is brand recall: on demo calls prospects say "Oh, you are the Reddit guy," which lifts close rates.
- Haters are survivable: show proof, block the negative, keep going.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
Roman's 8-step Reddit playbook:
1. Fresh account, one per browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari); never create it with a brand-new email ("new email plus new Reddit account, you will get banned right away").
2. Add profile picture, link the SaaS in bio, enable the setting that hides your post feed so visitors can't see you always talk about one product.
3. Warm up 7-14 days: only comment and upvote, build karma, no marketing.
4. After 7-14 days start posting.
5. Then start marketing, but respect subreddit rules; some (e.g., the YC subreddit) make promotion near-impossible and aren't worth it.
6. Change the angle every post; redirect to a YouTube video, an X post, etc.
7. Get at least 10 upvotes in the first 10 minutes via an internal group (he has 15 marketers who upvote and comment each other's posts); "as many up votes as you can in the first 5 to 10 minutes."
8. Reply to every single comment to drive engagement and ranking.
- Writing process: non-native English speaker, so he voice-dictates the story to ChatGPT and asks it to translate and correct; lets him "dive way deeper into details than if you have to write everything."
- Content sourcing: turn any event in your work life into a post (paid-influencer experiment, YC rejection, product progress, problem you're solving).
- Always include proof (screenshots of the influencer posts, revenue) so "no one can argue with what you are saying."
- Handling negativity: show proof, block insulting commenters, expect ~15 bad comments per post early on.
- Outreach engine behind the product: Goji Berry finds LinkedIn leads by intent signals (engaging with competitors, fundraising, hiring), then automates connection + AI-personalized messages.
- Marketing stack and monthly costs (self-reported): Instantly AI ~$800 (high-volume cold email), Outrank SEO ~$99, Goji Berry ~$99, Calendly ~$20, Framer ~$49, ChatGPT + Nano Banana ~$40 for posts/graphics, Sales Navigator ~$100 on some accounts.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- The coordinated upvote group is explicit vote manipulation under Reddit's rules; he frames it as standard practice, but it is an account-ban risk.
- Multiple accounts per browser and hiding your feed are evasion tactics; contrasts with "be transparent" community-marketing advice.
- Pat Walls caps expectations: Reddit gets you to 100 customers, not 1,000; other Starter Story guests credit Reddit for $17k MRR, so there is internal variation.
- Title number ($34k) vs. shown number ($24k MRR): typical Starter Story title inflation.
