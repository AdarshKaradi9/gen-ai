## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- A repeatable pre-launch checklist is why all four of his productivity apps (Ellie, Mogul, Luna, etc.) are profitable: waitlist -> analytics -> feedback board -> email sequences -> App Store listing -> landing page.
- The waitlist should exist before you write code; it validates demand, seeds beta testers, and gives launch-day users.
- Analytics and a feedback board are the two most-skipped items and the ones he regrets delaying most; without them you are "building on hard mode."
- Apple gives a roughly two-week initial boost at launch that you likely never get again, so the listing (especially screenshots) must be ready.
- Launch when beta analytics show users sticking for several days, not when the feature list feels done.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Step 1 Waitlist page: one app screenshot + email field; built in Framer, email capture via FormSpark; set up before building.
- Step 2 Analytics: PostHog; takes at most 30 minutes; primary purpose is seeing where/why users churn early.
- Step 3 Feedback board: Canny (integrates with web + iOS, users log in with their existing app account); cheaper alternative UserJot. Users post and upvote requests; top-voted items repeatedly surprise him; Ellie's list feature existed only because of board votes. Shipping requested features "drastically" cuts churn.
- Step 4 Email sequence system: Loops (nice UI, good free tier, good support). Sequences: welcome email; "we noticed you haven't used it in 7 days, any feedback?"; Ellie's onboarding sequence = 5 emails over 2 weeks highlighting lesser-known features, which he calls a "huge unlock" for 14-day retention.
- Step 5 App Store listing: spend 3-4 days on screenshots (first thing users judge); research title, description, tags seriously; organic App Store search is "very generous" if done right; don't waste the ~2-week Apple launch boost.
- Step 6 Landing page: separate from the waitlist page; reuse App Store screenshots/copy; spend at least a week but don't let it delay launch; Framer.
- When to launch: run staged beta; if analytics show sign-up-then-leave-after-a-day, don't launch; if users stay a few days, launch; "gut feeling", no fixed number.
- Launch itself: his style is low-key (YouTube video, tweets, LinkedIn, email the waitlist). Bigger alternative: start posting 4 weeks ahead, ask friends to post, Product Hunt, Reddit and forums, funnel everything to the landing page and App Store listing.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Argues against "no users yet, skip analytics" and "I already know what users want" thinking.
- Contrasts with big-bang launch advice: he personally just announces and "hopes for the best", while acknowledging a 4-week hype build-up works better for others.
- The checklist is about readiness and retention plumbing, not acquisition; he explicitly says the video is not about marketing.
- Tool-agnostic caveat: "the specific tool doesn't matter, but implementing it is essential."

## Memorable framings (quotable one-liners or named frameworks, verbatim if available)
- Six-item pre-launch checklist: waitlist page, in-app analytics, feedback board, email sequence system, optimized App Store listing, launch landing page.
- "Building on hard mode" (without analytics and a feedback board).
- "Huge unlock" (the 5-email onboarding sequence).
- Launch rule: launch when you have high confidence users will stick, judged from beta retention.
