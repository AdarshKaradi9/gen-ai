## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Sebastian (HabitKit, FocusKit; four apps) makes $50K+/month with ~98% of users coming from App Store / Google Play search and "literally no marketing at all" (self-reported).
- ASO = SEO for apps. Three foundations: (1) keyword placement in metadata, (2) screenshots for conversion, (3) ratings and reviews.
- App name and subtitle are the single most important ranking factor; "nothing else comes close."
- Do keyword research BEFORE naming the app; indie devs cannot afford a brand-only name like Duolingo can.
- ASO is a long game: launched Nov 2022 invisible; top 10 in UK/Germany after ~6 months; occasional US top 10 after ~1 year; consistently top 5 in the US for "habit tracker" after nearly 3 years.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Keyword research: brainstorm with ChatGPT/Claude, then validate with an affordable ASO tool that tracks unlimited keywords and shows popularity (search volume) and difficulty. Sweet spot = high popularity, manageable difficulty, but for the primary keyword "rather compete for a hard, high-value keyword than dominate an easy, low-volume one."
- App name: put the primary keyword first. HabitKit is listed as "Habit Tracker - Habit Kit" because the keyword is worth more than the brand.
- Subtitle (30 chars): secondary keywords only. HabitKit's subtitle: "streaks and accountability." Never repeat a word between name and subtitle; Apple indexes them as one combined string.
- Keyword field (100 chars in App Store Connect, invisible to users): comma-separated, no spaces, no words already in name/subtitle, no plurals if singular is present, no competitor names (risk of rejection), use all 100 characters.
- Screenshots: you have 3-5 seconds. First screenshot = your most visually impressive, differentiating feature (HabitKit: the grid-based habit view), never a welcome/onboarding screen. Show real UI, not abstract graphics or lifestyle photos. A/B test with Apple's Product Page Optimization: his designer-made "beautiful" screenshots lost to his original self-made ones.
- Reviews: prompt at a "happy moment" (HabitKit asks right after the user completes their first habit), never during onboarding or after an error; if dismissed, do not re-prompt for a while; reply to every review (one-star reviews sometimes get updated to five after a fix); email-signature trick on every support reply: "Hey, if you're enjoying the app, I'd be grateful for a review."; mine reviews for feature prioritization (20 people asking for the same thing = strong signal).

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- HabitKit: 7,400+ iOS ratings at 4.8 stars, 10,000+ Google Play ratings at 4.6; top 3 US for "habit tracker."
- FocusKit: new launch mentioned as going well.
- Duolingo: example of a brand that can afford a non-keyword name.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Self-reported: $50K+/month across four apps; $500K total revenue in 2025; $100K+ in January alone; was ~$15K/month roughly eight months earlier; 98% organic store traffic; ratings counts above; ranking timeline (6 months / 1 year / 3 years).
- Third-party (stork.ai): ~$22K/month average Jan-May 2024; same 98% search figure.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Prettier screenshots lost an A/B test: "don't assume fancy is always better... sometimes authentic beats professional."
- Zero paid/social marketing, versus most creators in this corpus who rely on TikTok/UGC or paid ads.
- Argues for competing on a hard, high-volume keyword rather than the usual "find an easy niche keyword" advice.
- Host frames the result partly as "being in the right place at the right time."
