## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Guest Jay Raavi (ex-Amazon) built Nomadtable, a solo-travel meetup app, to 1M+ downloads and ~$65K/month revenue with no team and no VC, almost entirely on organic short-form UGC (show notes, self-reported).
- The founder must make the content himself first: Jay spent months posting daily and trialing "hundreds" of hooks/formats before hiring a single creator. Only validated formats get handed to creators.
- Virality is predicted by early retention, not views: the benchmark is 75-80% of viewers watching past the 3-second mark. Views alone are a "view trap."
- With a large enough creator network posting daily, viral hits become statistical: roughly one viral video per creator per month, plus a steady baseline of views and downloads between hits. Viral videos keep gaining views for months (evergreen).
- Pay creators on performance (CPM) rather than retainers once you can show proof the formats work.
- Paid ads do not work for low-ARPU social apps; organic UGC plus word of mouth does.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Pre-launch waitlist of 12-15K people yielded ~6,000 day-one downloads spread globally, which was enough density for a travel app ("even two people in a city" makes it work) and avoided the city-by-city cold-start approach dating apps use.
- Jay's cadence while testing solo: "about one video a day" (Starter Story quote). He copied what other apps in the space were doing by following their founders on Twitter.
- First viral video came while the app was still in pre-order, with the hook "I've been solo traveling for two years and now I find out about this."
- The standard "Hook-Demo" format: (1) on-screen text hook aimed at the solo traveler's pain ("Imagine being scared of solo traveling when this literally exists"); (2) a casual visual reaction (facepalm, surprised look, drinking, tying a shoe) for authenticity; (3) quick product demo that does not feel like an ad; (4) a specific personal detail ("...when I had the most unsocial hostel in Thailand").
- Metrics to judge a format: % watching past 3 seconds (target 75-80%), average watch time (6-10 seconds is typical in his niche), and engagement/likes. Discard failed hooks; mass-replicate winners across creators.
- Creator management: 60-70 creators, managed solo, mostly via WhatsApp group chats and individual outreach; Jay keeps posting himself and remains the network's top performer.
- Compensation: $1-$2 CPM (per 1,000 views). Use retainers only at the very start if needed, then switch to CPM once you can show viral examples. Track views on old posts for up to 3 months and pay out on them. One creator earned $8,000 in a month for a video that did 8M views.
- Deliver on the hook: a clickbait hostel hook that reveals an app instead produced 300K views with only 174 likes and poor conversion.
- Do not run paid ads for a social app; 10-15% of new users come from word-of-mouth referrals, and the product itself was designed as a loop (meet someone fast, then post the experience back to social).
