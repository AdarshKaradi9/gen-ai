# Michael Seibel - Building Product

## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Justin.tv/Twitch survived despite breaking every rule because of three things together: an extremely technical founding team, very low burn, and founders whose ego was tied to the startup so they could not conceive of quitting.
- Start from the problem, not the idea: state it in one or two sentences, preferably one you have experienced, define it narrowly, and check it is solvable.
- Know the customer: who, how often they have the problem, how intense it is, whether they will pay, and how easy they are to find. Products on the home screen are the high-frequency ones.
- Charge early; free attracts hobbyists who lead you astray. Find the most desperate customers, not impressive ones, and never use friends or investors as feedback.
- Set up event-based metrics (Mixpanel/Amplitude/Heap, not Google Analytics) with 5-10 stats, and make measurement part of every spec.
- Run a disciplined product cycle: one KPI, open brainstorm, easy/medium/hard, hard-first prioritisation, written spec, two-week (web: one-week) cycles, one meeting only.
- Pivot = change customer or problem (rare; often means start a new company). Iterate = change the solution. Give it ~2 years, not 2 months.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Problem test: "can you state the problem clearly in two sentences? ... it should really only take you one sentence."
- Frequency/intensity grid: car buying is intense but once every ~7 years, so car sites are really built for dealers who have the problem daily. Uber: intense (people buy $20K cars to solve it) and daily.
- Pricing test: "make it a little bit harder for your users to use it and then see if they use it anyway." If the problem is intense, $100 feels like a deal. Starting with a price is "almost always better than starting free." If you will never charge (ads model), free is fine.
- Desperate-customer test: "whose business is gonna go out of business without using you?" If an enterprise prospect takes six months to decide on $1K/month software, move on.
- Fire bad customers: Justin Kan refunded one customer four times then banned them.
- Structured discount (from Parker Conrad, Zenefits): price 15% higher, then offer a time-boxed discount tied to a third-party reason (e.g. "AWS gave us 40% lower bills, so 30-day discount") to create a deadline - instead of giving the product away from fear.
- MVP: build it in ~2 weeks to avoid "MVP drift"; the test is giving it to customers. "Products are not paintings."
- Metrics: pick 5-10 events (Instagram example: open app, create account, take photo, apply effect, share photo); name events for a future with 1,000 stats; spec the stats you expect to move in every product spec.
- Product cycle at Socialcam: top KPI = DAU, contributing levers = new users, retention, new content; each cycle targets one lever. Brainstorm with everyone (4 people), every idea written on the board, laptops open to Mixpanel. Categories: new features/iterations, bug fixes/maintenance, A/B tests. Easy = multiple per day, medium = 1-2 days, hard = one engineer for most of the cycle. Pick hards by KPI impact first, then mediums, then easies. Write the spec (the step everyone skips). Cycle every 2 weeks (App Store review era) or weekly for web. Only one meeting per cycle; the non-coding CEO's job is to "shut up" for two weeks and bring burning ideas to the next meeting.
- Revenue should be the KPI if you ever plan to charge; if revenue is $0, still track it ("it should be depressing") and add 3 leading indicators (conversations, contracts, onboardings).
- Pre-sales for hardware: the #1 mistake is discounting below cost.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Justin.tv/Twitch: problem evolved from "make a TV show" to "let anyone broadcast live"; 3-month argument over background colour ended with five changeable backgrounds; released every 3 months (bad); gamers were ~20% of traffic for years and ignored; Twitch was born by talking to them and shipping mundane requested features (lag fixes); company went from ~$24M value after 5 years to ~$1B three years later. Chat-right/video-left layout decided 2006, unchanged.
- Poppy (YC, "Uber for babysitting", founder Avni): infant babysitting may not be solvable because skilled sitters already have well-paid nanny jobs - supply does not exist.
- Socialcam: Steve Huffman (Reddit CEO) said "thank God now I can delete this app" after the sale - the archetypal friend who is not a customer.
- Zenefits (Parker Conrad) discount structure; Exec (Justin Kan) firing a customer; Mixpanel (Suhail) setup advice; Instagram event list.
- Apple iPhone v1: no 3G, one carrier, bad battery, no App Store - "real Steve Jobs" iterated a shoddy MVP yearly; "fake Steve Jobs" dreams perfection and ignores customers.

## Numbers & evidence (any stats, revenue, conversion rates, retention figures; mark self-reported vs third-party)
- Founders' apartment $2,500/month, $500/month each walking-around money (self-reported).
- Gamers ~20% of Justin.tv traffic for years (self-reported).
- Justin.tv worth ~$24M after 5 years, ~$1B three years later (self-reported).
- Two-week dev cycles; "under two years" is too early to judge progress (Seibel's rule of thumb).
- YC has funded ~50 analytics products (Seibel's aside).

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Directly contradicts "go free to get users" and "go after the hardest/most impressive customers first."
- Says Google Analytics as a primary metrics tool means "you are doing it wrong."
- Seibel argues the genius is in the problem, not the solution ("Facebook wasn't the first social network"); many growth-hack videos treat the product as given.
- Ego tied to the startup is framed as a survival asset, which sits uneasily with founder-wellbeing advice (compare Rootd founder on burnout).

## Memorable framings (quotable one-liners or named frameworks, verbatim if available)
- "If you don't know the problem you can't know whether you solved it."
- "It's a lot easier to make it a little bit harder for your users to use it and then see if they use it anyways."
- "Talking to your customers is good, but talking to the wrong customers is very very very bad."
- "Products are not paintings ... the startup world is very unforgiving to artists."
- "Easy, medium, hard." "Hard first."
- "Pivot = changing the customer or the problem. Iterate = changing the solution."
- "Real Steve Jobs iterates and talks to customers. Fake Steve Jobs just dreams and creates art. Don't be fake Steve Jobs."
- From the 2019 companion lecture: "Keep the mechanic, keep the problem ... fix the screwdriver."
