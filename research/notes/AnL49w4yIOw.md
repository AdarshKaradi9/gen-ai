## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Addictive apps are built in three layers: (1) experience foundation, (2) interface boosting, (3) emotional design integration. Most founders follow a double-diamond process but skip the nuances of layer 3.
- Layer 1: the app must get the user's job done as fast and smoothly as possible before any visual design; 90% of founders under-optimize the core experience and leave "millions" on the table.
- Layer 2: in an age of "AI slop where everything looks the same", the visual interface is your trust badge; people make trust decisions within 50 milliseconds and keep judging.
- Layer 3: emotional design is what separates good (top 10%) apps from addictive ones; most AI-era apps treat users "like robots who just want efficiency", so users finish tasks but never attach and eventually churn.
- You do not need Duolingo's budget: pick the few moments where emotion changes business outcomes and design those.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Agency process for layer 1: strategy workshop first -> written strategy document (also used as AI prompt material for prototyping) -> screen maps of every flow and feature set -> wireframes/prototypes; only then visual design. Applies to new apps, new features and tweaks.
- Layer 2 rules: strong high-contrast color only on elements that drive revenue; primary CTAs "pop off the screen", secondary actions fade; lower contrast on less important content; use directional elements, color coding and visual weight to create a path to conversion; white space to spotlight money-making actions. Test every choice against "Does this help users see what they need to see when they need to see it?"
- Layer 3 examples from his agency: Freecash (then #2 on the App Store) got a tiered diamond/level system designed so power users keep collecting "for years", with progress that can be lost "in a very visually demoralizing way" (loss aversion); an AI note-taking app got its most-used daily surface turned into a branded "living" experience; a Web3 iOS app front-loaded emotional connection in onboarding to win trust from a trust-sensitive audience.
- Pizza test for layer 1: beautiful animation cannot save a 30-minute ordering flow; users would rather say "Hey Google, order my favorite pizza for 6 p.m."

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Duolingo: "masterclass in emotional design" that celebrates progress, makes you feel guilty for missed days, and creates emotional investment with a multi-million-dollar motion budget.

- Double-diamond design process; his agency (Zips/"SIPs app") claims to have tested the framework on App Store top apps and AI apps with hundreds of thousands of MAU.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Says founders should not touch visual design until the flow is mapped, which conflicts with "design-first/vibe-code the UI" AI-era advice.
- Treats visual polish as a conversion lever (trust), unlike Albert Cheng's "it's the core value and retention" framing; both agree emotion/positivity drives retention.
- Uses loss-aversion mechanics (progress that can be lost) that other creators and ethicists flag as dark patterns.
