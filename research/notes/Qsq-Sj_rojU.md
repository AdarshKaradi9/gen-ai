## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- Across 1,460 onboarding flows from 986 apps and websites on Mobbin, the average app has 25 onboarding screens; "keep it short" was never the point.
- The longest flows are in finance, health and fitness, and education; 7 of the 10 longest are finance apps, and several of the longest belong to very successful apps. The shortest flows are mostly AI products.
- Best onboarding follows: sign up → set up account → hit the aha moment (Airbnb: first booking; Netflix: find and watch a show; Mobbin: save a screen you love).
- Winning screens sell the outcome, not a feature list; the best long flows "don't feel like onboarding" because the user is already doing the thing.
- Personalization in onboarding is still a minority (23% of apps; 7% of AI apps) but consistently lifts conversion by measurable amounts.
- Some products (Mobbin itself, AI chat apps) may not need onboarding at all: the first prompt is where value is found.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Welcome screen that shows the product in action on mobile and desktop (Timo); animation that conveys what the app does "without reading a single word" (transcribed as "Front Butts", likely Runkeeper per another summary); let users try the core experience before signup (Alma/Elma) - rare among AI apps.
- Turn the signup screen into a pitch with customer logos as social proof (Superhuman).
- Human touches: founder's note with handwritten signature and hand-drawn flower (One Year); birthday acknowledgement (Tinder); CEO video after your first listing goes live (Airbnb); personal CEO note after account creation (Basecamp).
- Multi-intent goal selection: Headspace let users pick more than one goal → +10% free-trial conversion.
- Short flows for simple products: Tide (transcribed "tight") = download → answer two questions → customize recommendations → prompt to sign up.
- Let users choose their map style during onboarding so it feels like theirs before use (Focus Flight).
- Conversational quiz copy alone: Dollar Shave Club +5% subscriptions.
- Show what the answers unlocked: after six questions Endos/Endless shows a plan; BitePal builds a personal plan and names the exact date you will hit your goal; Brilliant pre-populates the homepage with only personalized courses; Speak says "in two months you'll be able to communicate while traveling in France" with a graph showing speaking beats reading, after steps that already had you speaking.
- Paywalls inside onboarding (22% of apps): Beside pairs the quiz with a one-time offer for urgency; Timo shows a full page of social proof before the paywall; Focus Flight's one-time offer is shaped like a flight ticket that "prints" with haptic vibration; Grammarly recommends a pricing plan from quiz answers → ~+20% plan upgrades.
- Make long flows feel short: Duolingo goes get started → choose language → learns about you → first lesson → satisfaction → create account, ~60 screens before signup; Bump animates even loading and verification states; BitePal has 61 screens with a lovable raccoon you name, then "your personal plan is ready, you'll lose weight by [date]" then the paywall.
- Progressive education instead of front-loading (Cake Equity): reassuring copy and tooltips explaining each step's impact; password field that ticks requirements in real time; progress indicators; microcopy.
- Replace popups and banners with a persistent six-step checklist (Mural): +10% relative one-week retention; checklists survive dismissal. Todoist-style nudge in the empty state instead of guided tours.
- Custom pre-permission screen before the OS notification prompt (Brilliant: "I'll remind you to learn so it becomes a long-term habit"; Center also previews the exact notification you'd get) - "improves accept rates significantly."
- Split a signup form across multiple screens (House): +15% conversion; "friction we add in one place removes friction in another."
- Web onboarding is 21% shorter than iOS because mobile bakes in permission and paywall screens.
- Cultural fit: Eastern-market users are comfortable with information-dense interfaces; don't copy Western minimalism blindly.

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Directly contradicts the conventional "keep onboarding short" rule; the reframe is that perceived length, not screen count, is what matters.
- Also partially contradicts the "long onboarding is always right" camp (Cravotta): says self-evident products should skip onboarding entirely.
- Presenter stresses "there's no one right way" and that effects are context- and culture-dependent, so the lift numbers are not transferable guarantees.
