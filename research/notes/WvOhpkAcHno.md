## Core claims (3-7 bullets, what the video argues)
- The paywall is "the most expensive screen you'll design", yet most designers treat it as an afterthought or copy it.
- There are 6 main paywall types, each with a distinct psychology and a distinct business-model fit: Access, Free Trial, Feature, Usage, Upgrade (performance), Add-on.
- The best paywalls do not hide value; they show just enough to make the user want more, and they appear exactly where the value would appear.
- Good paywalls reframe the decision away from price ("Is this worth paying for?") toward experience, possibility, capacity or mastery.
- Common failure mode across all types: gating before the user understands why the feature matters.

## Specific tactics (concrete, actionable; include numbers, prices, timings, exact UI choices)
- Access paywall: keep the locked feature visible but blurred/obscured inside the UI where its data would normally render; add one line explaining what the insight reveals; list plans that unlock it; single "Upgrade my plan" CTA. Psychology: "earned curiosity" (user already has data, the deeper layer is "right here").
- Free-trial paywall: trial headline + premium feature list + one primary CTA ("Start your free trial") + reassurance line ("Cancel anytime"); visualize one hero feature (e.g. 4K export). Trigger it at the moment of a premium action (export), and let the free path still work with a watermark.
- Feature paywall: grid of premium features with icons/illustrations, then 3 simple price options (Monthly, Annual with discount, Lifetime), one large "Subscribe" CTA. Lead with possibility, not price.
- Usage paywall: show current plan, capacity indicator, a slider/selector for higher tiers, monthly vs annual toggle with discounted annual, clear upgrade CTA; trigger only when usage is actually near the limit ("You're growing. Let's scale with you").
- Upgrade (performance) paywall: side-by-side comparison of current vs upgraded capability (e.g. "Fast" vs "Autonomous (Core)"), explain what gets better, single "Upgrade to Core" CTA. Never hide the advanced feature entirely.
- Add-on paywall: optional checkbox/toggle with price tied to it (e.g. "+$3 per seat per month"), short description, updated order summary; keeps the user on their current plan.
- Process tip: build a Figma swipe file of real screens (she uses Mobbin), link each to its full flow, and use Figma Make to adapt a pattern to your own design system rather than copying.

## Named examples (apps/companies cited and what they did)
- Typeform Insights: basic analytics free; "Question drop-off rate" locked in place with copy "Find out exactly where you're losing people" and "Upgrade my plan" (Access).
- Riverside: exporting HQ video triggers a 14-day Pro trial modal listing 4K export, studio sound, separate tracks, watermark-free exports, AI transcription (Free Trial).
- Moonly meditation app: feature grid (Calendar, Affirmations, Runes, Wisdom, Healing, Meditations) then Monthly / Annual (discount) / Lifetime (Feature).
- "Visitors" analytics dashboard: billing modal with monthly/annual toggle, usage slider for monthly events (Usage).
- Replit: "Unlock Replit's full potential" modal, Fast vs Autonomous (Core) agent modes, "Upgrade to Core" (Upgrade).
- Calendly Teams checkout: seats + billing cycle, optional SAML SSO at +$3/seat/month via checkbox (Add-on).

## Contradictions or caveats (where this disagrees with common advice or other videos)
- Focuses on SaaS/web and lifestyle apps rather than the hard-paywall onboarding funnels (Cal AI style) that dominate mobile-subscription videos; she favors contextual, in-product gates over up-front walls.
- Names the psychological principle behind the Feature paywall as "reactance", which is an unusual label for a "sell possibility" pattern.
- No A/B data, so claims about what "works" are design-judgment, not measured.
