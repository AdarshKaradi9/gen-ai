# Subscription Killer (find and cancel forgotten subscriptions)

## One-line pitch
Screenshot your bank statement or connect your email, and the app instantly totals every recurring charge, flags forgotten trials and price creep, and walks you through cancelling each one.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: privacy-wary US adults aged 20 to 35 with many app and streaming subscriptions who will not link a bank account to Rocket Money. Secondary: freelancers tracking software tools (Subchecks targets them).
Where they gather: r/personalfinance, r/Frugal, money TikTok ("subscription audit" videos). Reddit sizes could not be fetched in this pass, so they are unverified here.

## Demand evidence
- Category leader scale: Rocket Money states "over 10 million members" in its 2026 App Store listing (self-reported); 400K iOS ratings at 4.47 stars (AppGoblin).
- History: Rocket Companies bought Truebill for $1.275B cash in December 2021; Truebill had about 2.5M users and was reportedly on track for $100M ARR (press).
- Regulation keeps the topic in the news: the FTC "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025; the FTC restarted rulemaking with an ANPRM in early 2026 (law firm reports). More than half of US states have auto-renewal laws.

## Money proof
- Rocket Money: Premium about $4 to $12/month (pay-what-you-want range; sources conflict), plus a bill-negotiation success fee of 35% to 60% of first-year savings, plus a new Premium+ tier with "Rowan", an AI assistant over text built "in partnership with Anthropic" (App Store listing, 2026). AppGoblin's estimate of $200K+/month is clearly far too low given Truebill's reported ARR; treat AppGoblin as unreliable here.
- Indie trackers (verified via TrustMRR): Subby (RevenueCat-verified) has 51 active subscriptions at $2.99/week, $29.99/year or $49.99 lifetime, 4 App Store ratings. Subchecks (LemonSqueezy-verified) has no active subscriptions with a $29 lifetime plan. Indie manual trackers earn very little.
- Bobby (manual tracker): no revenue data found in this pass.

## Competitor weaknesses
Rocket Money: BBB average 2.47/5 from 109 reviews and 273 complaints in 3 years; Trustpilot 3.2. Themes: surprise negotiation fees billed as a lump sum on projected savings, a $63.09 fee "for a savings that didn't exist", difficulty cancelling Rocket Money itself, charges after users thought they had left. Some reviewers note users can negotiate bills themselves for free. Manual trackers (Subby-type) make the user type everything in, so there is no aha.

## The wedge
No bank link, no success fees: the camera or share sheet reads statement screenshots (or Gmail receipts), finds recurring charges in seconds, and shows "You pay $1,284/year for 17 subscriptions". Then generates per-service cancel steps and a deadline reminder before each trial converts. Flat, honest pricing aimed directly at Rocket Money's fee complaints. A specific audience (students, couples splitting streaming) could sharpen it.

## The 2-second clip
Phone screenshots a bank app's transactions list; the app shows a counter racing up to "$1,284/yr wasted on 17 subscriptions", with logos stacking. Format: "I audited my subscriptions and found $X" stitch series.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: share 1-3 statement screenshots, see the yearly total and the list in under 30 seconds.
- Daily/weekly action: weak. Realistic cadence is a weekly "charges this week" digest and pre-renewal alerts; new statement screenshots monthly.
- Streak/reward: "money saved" counter that only grows; cancelled-subscription trophies.
- Shareable artifact: "My subscription audit" card with total and saved amount.
- Paywall: show the total free, put the itemised list, cancel guides and reminders behind the paywall; $4.99/month or $24.99/year, or a $14.99 one-time "audit" unlock.

## Risks
- Retention and LTV: the value is front-loaded; once subscriptions are cancelled, users churn. This is the main economic problem, as Subby's 51 subscribers show.
- Saturation and free substitutes: Rocket Money (with an AI agent now), bank apps adding subscription views, Apple's own subscriptions page.
- Data sensitivity: statement screenshots and email access raise privacy and Google restricted-scope verification costs (CASA audit) for Gmail reading.
- Cancellation-on-behalf requires human or agent workflows that are costly and error-prone.
- Regulatory tailwind is uncertain: the federal click-to-cancel rule is gone for now.

## Verdict
Skip as a standalone habit app. The demo is strong and the market is real, but the value is a one-off audit, the incumbent now has an AI agent and 10M members, and verified indie trackers have almost no subscribers. Could work as a cheap one-time "audit" utility or a feature inside a broader money app, not a subscription business.
