# Peptide Stack Tracker

## One-line pitch
A tracker for people running injectable peptide stacks (BPC-157, TB-500, retatrutide, compounded GLP-1s): reconstitution math, vial inventory, site rotation, and a per-compound "is this doing anything?" view against sleep, weight and symptoms.

## Who exactly
- Beachhead: US men and women 25-55 in biohacking, gym and longevity circles self-administering "research" peptides or compounded peptides from med-spas and telehealth.
- Where they gather: r/Peptides (vendor blogs claim 180K-200K+ members and 70K weekly visitors, self-reported/unverified), r/Peptidesource (GummySearch shows 11K in 2024 to 50K in 2026 on one tracker, 93K on another; third-party, inconsistent), dozens of smaller peptide subreddits tracked by GummySearch, plus peptide TikTok and podcasts.

## Demand evidence
- Vendor claims: US peptide-related searches of 10.1M/month in January 2026, five times 2020 (PSPeptides, unsourced vendor marketing); "peptide therapy" TikTok searches up 459% in Q1 2026 (PeptIQ, unsourced vendor marketing). Treat as directional only.
- Strong regulatory news flow keeps the topic mainstream: in February 2026 RFK Jr. announced about 14 of 19 Category 2 peptides would move back to Category 1 (law firms note FDA had not formally done so); an April 2026 FDA action reportedly removed 12 peptides from the safety-concerns list (secondary source); on 23 July 2026 the FDA compounding advisory committee voted 8-6 to recommend BPC-157 for the 503A bulks list against FDA staff advice (RAPS, GMA, Pharmacy Times).
- App supply exploded: a September 2026 comparison counted 24 peptide tracker apps, most launched in 2025-2026 (LynkDose roundup, competitor-authored but with verified store data).

## Money proof
- trends.vc GLP-1 Companion Apps (Aug 2026, third-party estimates): PeptidePal $245K/yr (founded 2026), Pep AI $143K, Peptide Tracker $112K (2026), Glapp $54K. Real but small.
- Pricing from store listings (LynkDose roundup, verified against stores 3 Sept 2026): PeptIQ $19.99-24.99/mo (609 ratings), Peptide Tracker & Calculator $19.99-24.99/yr (509), PepTracker $47.99/yr (437), Regimen $39.99/yr (375), PeptideKit $39.99-49.99/yr (301), lifetime options $34.99-$149.99.
- The single biggest adoption signal is a free app: Peptide Tracker (Log & Reminder) launched May 2026 with no paywall, only tips, and reached about 1,874 ratings at 4.9 in four months. It ships a reconstitution calculator, inventory, plasma curves, site rotation, a course and a forum.
- Shotsy (~31,000 ratings) remains the giant but serves prescription pens, not vials.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Rating counts are tiny outside two apps; many 4.9-5.0 averages rest on fewer than 25 reviews, so no paid leader has locked the category (LynkDose).
- Reported gaps: no Apple Health sync on the free leader, export "on request only"; PeptidePal listed at 3.4 stars (5 ratings) and "not updated since February 2026"; privacy labels on some apps allow tracking; LynkDose removed its own reconstitution calculator. These are feature gaps, not deep pain.
- I found no 1-2 star review clusters indicating a strong unmet need.

## The wedge
The only plausibly open angle is outcome attribution: "which compound in my stack is actually working", scoring each peptide against wearable data (Oura, Whoop, Apple Health) and a daily 10-second journal. But LynkDose, Regimen ("Signals"), PeptIQ and Protocol already claim versions of this, so the wedge is thin. A free, polished incumbent sets the price anchor at zero for the basics.

## The 2-second clip
A vial photo, a slider to "2 ml BAC water", and the syringe graphic filling to "12.5 units" with a check mark. Clear and satisfying, but every competitor can make the same video, and the free leader already gives this away.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: add a vial, get exact syringe units in one screen.
- Daily: log dose, site auto-suggested, 10-second journal (sleep, pain, energy).
- Streak: cycle adherence ring (on/off weeks), vial countdown with reorder prompt.
- Shareable artifact: "My stack" card. Sharing is limited because users avoid publicising grey-market use.
- Paywall: after the first calculated dose; $39.99/yr preselected, $4.99/mo. Must compete with a free app.

## Risks
- Regulatory: most compounds are sold as "research use only, not for human consumption". BPC-157's legal status remains unsettled (advisory vote only, FDA rulemaking pending). An app that gives human dosing guidance for unapproved drugs invites FDA/FTC and liability exposure, and the rules may swing either way in 2027.
- App Store: 24+ apps are live, so Apple currently tolerates tracking; I found no documented rejections. However, supplier directories or affiliate links to "research chemical" vendors (some apps do this) are a clear guideline risk (unlawful or dangerous substances), as are dosing recommendations. Ads on Meta/TikTok for peptide content are likely restricted, which removes paid acquisition.
- Saturation: 24 apps in under two years, a free leader, and GLP-1 trackers expanding into vials.
- Trend fragility: if FDA legalises compounding, telehealth clinics will bundle their own trackers; if FDA cracks down, the user base shrinks.

## Verdict
Skip. The trend is real and loud, but the app layer saturated in 2026 (24 trackers, one excellent free one with about 1,900 ratings in four months), revenue per player is small, and the regulatory and ad-platform risk sits on the founder. Only revisit if FDA formally legalises compounding and a clinic-partner B2B2C model becomes possible.
