# Notes to Flashcards

## One-line pitch
Snap a photo of your class notes or a textbook page and get clean AI flashcards plus a five-minute daily spaced-repetition review that keeps a streak alive until the exam.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
- **Beachhead:** US high school AP students and pre-nursing/nursing students, who memorise large volumes of facts against fixed exam dates. Knowt says at least 700,000 students used it during the May 2025 AP season (self-reported, via a news report).
- **Where they gather:** StudyTok and "study with me" TikTok, AP subject subreddits, r/nursing and r/StudentNurse. I did not retrieve exact subreddit sizes or hashtag counts in this pass.
- **Secondary:** grad and licensing-exam students (state boards, bar). A Gizmo App Store reviewer describes using it for state board exams.

## Demand evidence
- Gizmo reports 13 million users across 120+ countries and raised a $22M Series A led by Shine Capital in April 2026 (press, self-reported users).
- Turbo AI (notes, flashcards, quizzes from lectures and PDFs) reported 5 million users and was adding about 20,000 users a day (TechCrunch, Business Insider via aggregators; self-reported).
- Knowt claims 5 million+ students and teachers (self-reported).
- TrustMRR lists many small AI flashcard apps (Study Cards AI, FlashKing, Studr, Cram, Cramberry, GakkoDeck), a sign of a crowded indie field.

## Money proof
- **Turbo AI:** founders claim "eight-figure ARR"; other outlets say $10M+ or $13M; about $20/month (self-reported, unverified).
- **Gizmo:** AppGoblin estimates the iOS app at about 89K MAU, 57K ratings at 4.67 stars, $10K+/month with 42% IAP and 58% ads (third-party estimate). Dealroom lists revenue under $1M; another site claims $15.3M ARR with no source. Figures conflict badly. One review site quotes $13.99/week ($6.99 student).
- **Quizlet Plus:** about $7.99/month or $35.99/year; revenue estimates range from about $96M to $277M with weak sourcing (third-party estimates only).
- **Knowt:** core features free; no revenue found.
- **Indie apps on TrustMRR:** Study Cards AI shows 15 active subscriptions (verified via Stripe); FlashKing snapshots conflict ($0 vs $1K 30-day revenue). No small player found with meaningful verified MRR.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Gizmo reviews: spaced repetition is poorly tuned ("keeps repeating the same ones" and waits 30 days after three correct answers, compared unfavourably with Anki); a hearts system locks students out for hours after wrong answers; generated questions drift into material not yet studied; slow photo upload.
- Quizlet: review coverage in 2026 headlines "free tier cuts", pushing users to Knowt and Gizmo (third-party review sites).
- Anki: powerful but ugly and manual; the gap every AI entrant targets.

## The wedge
Exam-date-aware scheduling that never locks you out. Instead of a hearts paywall and a generic algorithm, the user enters "AP Bio exam on May 11" and the app plans a daily review count that finishes the syllabus on time, using an Anki-grade scheduler (FSRS). Narrowing to one exam family (AP or NCLEX) at launch gives a content and TikTok angle the generalists do not have.

## The 2-second clip
A phone hovers over a messy handwritten biology page; shutter sound; the page dissolves into a fanned stack of 24 tidy flashcards with "AP Bio exam in 41 days: 12 cards today" on top.

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** pick exam and date, scan one page, see cards within seconds before any signup.
- **Daily action:** review today's due cards (5 minutes), scan new notes after class.
- **Streak/reward:** streak with two freezes; a "syllabus covered" progress bar toward exam day.
- **Shareable artifact:** an exam-countdown card ("82% of AP Bio mastered, 41 days left") and shareable decks that pull classmates in.
- **Paywall:** after the first deck is generated; free tier for limited scans per week; $5.99/month or $29.99/year, annual preselected. Students are price sensitive, so pricing sits under Quizlet.

## Risks
- **Saturation:** Gizmo, Turbo, Knowt, Quizlet, Revisely, plus ChatGPT itself does this for free. High risk.
- **Retention:** seasonal; usage spikes before exams and collapses in summer.
- **Unit economics:** student willingness to pay is low; Gizmo leans on ads (58% by estimate). Paid CPI for teens is unlikely to be recovered.
- **Platform:** under-13 users trigger COPPA; OCR and LLM cost per scan.

## Verdict
Maybe. Demand and money are real, but this is one of the most crowded AI consumer categories and free chatbots do the core job. Only worth building as a narrow exam-specific product (AP or NCLEX) with a visibly better scheduler and no lockouts.
