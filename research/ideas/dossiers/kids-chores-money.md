# Kids Chores and Pocket Money (game-first, no bank)

## One-line pitch
A chore and pocket-money app where the kid raises a character by doing real-world tasks, earns "coins" the parent pays out in cash or however they like, and saves toward a visible goal, with no debit card or bank account needed.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: parents of kids aged 6 to 11 who want chores and money habits but do not want a kid debit card yet (too young, fees, or not available in their country). Strong sub-niche: ADHD and neurodivergent families, who already pay for Joon. Possible geographic wedge: India and South Asian diaspora families, where pocket money is cash-based and US kid cards do not operate, but I found no demand data to confirm it.
Where they gather: r/Parenting, r/ADHD and ADHD parent Facebook groups, parent TikTok and Instagram. I could not fetch Reddit subscriber counts in this pass, so sizes are unverified.

## Demand evidence
- Kid finance is a proven paid category: Greenlight reports 6M+ users by late 2025 (company-reported).
- Gamified chores have paying demand: Joon claims 200,000+ families and 20M+ chores completed (self-reported); 8.3K App Store ratings at 4.63 stars (AppGoblin).
- Many small entrants (KidTask, Homey, TasksForKids, Kids Chores & Earnings Tracker, Finsl from Kerala, Track Chores by an Indian studio), which shows the idea is obvious and crowded at the low end.
- I found no search-trend or community data specific to India/diaspora chore demand.

## Money proof
- Greenlight: about $180M ARR in 2024, up 45% from $124M in 2023 (Sacra, third-party estimate). Pricing $4.99 to $14.98/month, Core about $5.99/month for up to 5 kids (comparison sites, conflicting).
- GoHenry: folded into Acorns Early in the US in late 2025; about $5 to $10/month (comparison sites, conflicting). No revenue found.
- BusyKid: about $48/year (comparison sites). No revenue found.
- Joon (ADHD chores game): under $10K/month and about 19K MAU (AppGoblin, third-party estimate); about $11K/month (Screensdesign, estimate); 415K to 620K installs (trackers). Price now about $12.99/month or $89.99/year.
- S'moresUp and RoosterMoney: not researched in this pass.
- Read: the money sits with the bank-card players (interchange plus subscription, regulated). The game-first, no-bank segment that a solo founder can build has a best-known player earning around $10K/month by estimates.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Joon: price "sticker shock" in 1-star reviews; "paid almost 100$ for an app that my child is already losing interest in after only a week"; one guide says enthusiasm fades after 4 to 8 weeks (single source, unverified).
- Greenlight and peers: fees across tiers draw "hidden cost" articles; require a bank card and are US-only; overkill for a 7-year-old.
- Generic chore charts: no character, no reason for the kid to open the app.

## The wedge
Game-first and bank-free: a character that grows from chores and from saving (the savings jar is part of the creature's home, so not spending is a visible reward), and payouts the parent settles any way they like (cash, UPI, gift card). Fix Joon's two complaints directly: lower price (family plan around $39.99/year) and fresh content cadence (seasonal events, weekly new outfits) to fight the week-4 fade. Localised currencies and festivals (Diwali savings goal) are a cheap differentiator for diaspora parents, unproven.

## The 2-second clip
Kid taps "Made my bed" on a tablet, the creature does a happy dance and a coin flies into a jar labelled "LEGO set 62%". Parent-facing format: "How I got my 8-year-old to do chores without nagging" with the before/after chore chart.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha (parent): pick kid age and 3 chores from a list, see the week's chart and the kid's character instantly; the paywall comes after this plan reveal.
- Daily action (kid): check off morning and evening chores; parent approves with one tap.
- Streak/reward: chore streak with freezes; coins; character levels and room items; savings goal progress bar.
- Shareable artifact: weekly "Chore Champion" card and "Goal reached!" certificate parents post.
- Paywall: hard paywall after the plan reveal with a 7-day trial, $5.99/month or $39.99/year per family.

## Risks
- Two-sided product: parents pay, kids must engage; retention depends on the kid, and the 4 to 8 week fade is the known killer.
- Child privacy: COPPA and Apple Kids Category rules (no third-party analytics/ads in kid-facing surfaces, parental gates) limit tracking and attribution tools.
- Paid acquisition is harder with restricted tracking; parent CPIs are not cheap.
- Saturation at the low end; bank-card players own the brand "kids money app".
- India/diaspora wedge is a hypothesis with no evidence found.

## Verdict
Maybe, leaning skip. The habit loop is the best of the three ideas, but the closest proof (Joon, YC-backed, 6 years, 400K+ installs) earns an estimated $10K/month, and the big money needs a bank card a solo founder cannot offer. Worth it only with a sharp niche (ADHD at lower price, or a validated diaspora audience).
