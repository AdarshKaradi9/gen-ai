# Couples Health Habits (Flamme model for eating well and moving together)

## One-line pitch
A two-player habit app where partners set shared daily goals (a walk, a vegetable-first dinner, no late snacking), see each other's check-ins, keep a joint streak that only grows when both show up, and get nudged with "your partner is waiting on you".

## Who exactly
Beachhead: cohabiting or engaged couples 25 to 38 who want to get fit or lose weight together, often before a wedding, after a baby, or one partner starting GLP-1. Long-distance couples are Flamme's and Paired's core but less relevant here. Where they gather: TikTok couple-content (Flame reported 58M organic views from couple content, self-reported), wedding communities, fitness subreddits. I did not find sizes for a "couples fitness" community or hashtag.

## Demand evidence
- Paired: 10.7M installs, about 461K MAU, 214.9K ratings (AppGoblin, third-party estimate), showing big demand for daily couple rituals.
- Flamme Android: 238K+ installs, about 19K MAU, steady growth of about 1.7K installs per 4 weeks (AppGoblin, third-party estimate). Acquired by AI relationship startup Arya (Global Dating Insights, press).
- A new couples app (daily activities, questions, streaks, countdowns) launched Feb 2026 and reached 34K+ users and 713 paying subscribers in three months via organic TikTok (Microns sale listing, self-reported).
- Couples fitness shelf is new and thin: Couple Glow, Sweatmates, FitWithLove (about 14.1K installs, 12 ratings), Fitlove (released Feb 2026; despite the name its description is about tracking "intimate workouts") (store listings, AppGoblin). No evidence any has traction.
- Not found: search volume or hashtag counts for couples healthy eating or fitness accountability.

## Money proof
- Paired: $10K+/month bucket (floor, not estimate), $59.99 to $69.99/year per reviews, 7-day trial (AppGoblin third-party; prices from App Store reviews). Raised $2.5M in 2021 with about 5K subscribers then (UKTN, press). One aggregator claims $300M revenue, which is implausible and ignored.
- Flame/Flamme: grew MRR from $3K to $10K in under eight months using a TikTok phone farm (4 to 5 phones, 15 to 20 accounts, up to 150 content variants a day), 250K downloads (Stork.ai write-up of founder interview, self-reported). AppGoblin puts Flamme iOS and Android each under $10K/month (third-party estimate).
- Feb 2026 couples app: $19,500 ARR, 713 subs, listed at $220,000 and marked sold (Microns, self-reported).
- Date Night couples card game on TrustMRR: $168 all-time (verified), a reminder that many couples apps earn nothing.
- Screensdesign claims the relationship-app category averages $155K/month, which looks skewed by leaders and is unverified (third-party).
- No revenue evidence found for Lasting, Between or any couples fitness app.
Conclusion: couples apps monetise at modest levels ($10K MRR is a good outcome); couples health is unproven.

## Competitor weaknesses
Paired reviews: "very costly", annual-only pricing pushed one couple away, generic content that does not fit some couples' values, questions "out in left field". Flamme reviews: onboarding assumes the relationship needs fixing ("communication, money, or family") when happy couples want "fun challenges for couples", which Flamme says it is now building. Couples fitness apps: Sweatmates requires a subscription just to log a workout; most have almost no ratings. Pattern: relationship apps are talk-only, fitness apps are solo, and no one owns "do healthy things together".

## The wedge
Health as a couple's game, not therapy and not a workout library. One shared daily card with 2 or 3 tiny goals both partners choose ("10K steps each", "cook at home", "no dessert weekdays"), a joint flame that only grows when both check in, and a weekly couple challenge with a date-night reward ("hit 5 of 7, she picks the restaurant"). Light photo proof ("show me your dinner") adds fun accountability. One subscription unlocks both phones, removing the pay-twice objection.

## The 2-second clip
Split screen of two phones side by side: one partner taps "walked 30 min", the other phone buzzes "Sam finished, your turn to keep the 23-day flame alive", and a shared flame grows. Caption: "my boyfriend won't skip the gym anymore because of this app". Couple POV content is the format Flame scaled with.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: the first partner picks a shared goal and gets an invite link; the aha arrives only when the partner joins and the flame lights (this is the activation risk).
- Daily action: each partner checks in on 2 to 3 shared goals; optional photo.
- Streak and reward: joint streak with two freezes; "partner is waiting" reminder about 23.5 hours after the last check-in; weekly couple challenge with a chosen reward.
- Shareable artifact: "Day 50 together" card with both avatars, steps walked together, home-cooked dinners.
- Paywall: after the partner joins (or on the inviter's last onboarding screen with a "covers both of you" frame), $49.99/year preselected, $7.99/month alternative, 7-day trial.

## Risks
- Two-sided activation: the inviter must convince the partner to install; drop-off at invite is the main killer, and solo users get no aha.
- Monetisation ceiling: one subscription per two installs halves revenue per install against paid CPI; the best couples apps sit around the $10K/month bucket.
- Relationship strain: "your partner will miss out" guilt can create real friction around weight and food; diet shaming between partners is a wellbeing and review risk.
- Category confusion: App Store "couples workout" searches surface sexual content (Fitlove), which can hurt ASO and ad approval.
- Platform: growth depends on TikTok volume tactics (phone farms) that violate TikTok rules.

## Scores
| Criterion | 1-5 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Two-second aha | 3 | Shared flame is clear, but needs two people to feel it |
| Visual short-video hook | 4 | Couple POV content worked for Flame (58M views, self-reported) |
| Money already swims | 3 | Paired $10K+/month bucket, Flame $10K MRR, new app 713 subs (estimates and self-reported); no proof for couple health |
| Daily reason to return | 4 | Daily joint check-in with partner pressure |
| Shareable moment | 4 | "Day N together" cards suit couple content |
| Unit economics | 2 | One sub per two installs, invite drop-off, modest category ceilings |
| **Total** | **20/30** | |

## Verdict
Maybe, leaning skip. Couples apps get organic reach and the built-in invite is a real growth loop, but proven revenue tops out near $10K MRR and nothing shows couples will pay for health habits specifically. Better as a "do it with your partner" mode inside a stronger single-player habit app (for example the quit-sugar or GLP-1 ideas) than as its own product.
