# No-Spend Streak

## One-line pitch
Quittr for shopping: a "days without an impulse buy" streak with a running "money kept" counter, an SOS screen for the moment you're about to check out, and a blocker for shopping apps (Shein, Temu, Amazon, TikTok Shop).

## Who exactly
Beachhead: women aged 20 to 35 doing a "no buy year" or "low buy" challenge, especially those trying to quit TikTok Shop, Shein or Temu hauls. They gather on r/nobuy (about 67K to 69K members per press snapshots, so modest), under TikTok's #nobuy and #nobuy2025 tags, and in "underconsumption core" content.

## Demand evidence
- ConsumerAffairs reports 94.4 million TikTok posts tagged with "No Buy" content and r/nobuy at 69,000 members (press, figures not independently checked).
- NSS G-Club reports over 3,000 #nobuy TikToks in one month and 270% growth between Christmas and the end of 2024 (press, short window).
- "No Buy 2025" was covered as a mainstream trend by Vice, The Week, Success and others in early 2025. "Underconsumption core" was covered by the Straits Times and Starling Bank.
- A Gerald Wallet guide notes there is "no single dominant app" for these challenges. People combine spreadsheets, Gumroad and Goodnotes printable trackers, and Notion templates. That supports demand while showing no category winner yet.
- Caveat: the trend peaks every January as a New Year resolution. I found no 2026 trend data in this pass.

## Money proof
This is the weakest point. I found no revenue figures for any no-spend app.
- NoBuy: No Spend Challenge Game (iOS): $4.99/mo or $29.99/yr with a 7-day trial (App Store listing). No revenue data.
- Stop Impulse Buying | Budget, Cartless (No-Buy Tracker, impulse score), Willow (Better Spending Habits, yearly subscription), and Unspent (free, Android). All small; no revenue found.
- Printable and digital no-buy trackers sell on Gumroad and the Goodnotes marketplace, which shows small willingness to pay.
- Analogy: the Quittr model (hard paywall, reportedly $250K MRR four months after launch; self-reported/third-party, from FINDINGS_SO_FAR) shows that the "quit X streak" format monetises when the pain is intense. Overspending is painful, but less shameful and urgent than porn, and the audience is short on money by definition. Budgeting apps (YNAB and others) do prove people pay to control spending, but I did not re-verify their figures here.

## Competitor weaknesses
I found no meaningful volume of 1 to 2 star reviews, because the competitors are too small to have them. From the listings:
- Most are generic budget trackers with a no-spend calendar bolted on. None has a real blocker or an emotional character.
- None ties into the moment of purchase (the shopping app opening), which is where an impulse happens.
- Several require manual logging of every "didn't buy" decision, which adds friction (Stop Impulse Buying listing).

## The wedge
1. **Intercept at the point of temptation.** Use the Screen Time API (the one sec mechanic) to put a pause screen before Shein, Temu, Amazon or TikTok Shop opens: "Day 23. $412 kept. What are you about to buy?" Then a 24-hour "cart cooling" timer. No current no-buy app does this.
2. **Money kept is the hero metric.** Users set a goal (a trip, an emergency fund), and each resisted urge moves a visual jar or progress bar toward it.
3. **Built for the January moment.** Run a "No Buy 2027" campaign from December 2026, then retain through the year with monthly "low buy" rule sets.

## The 2-second clip
A thumb taps the Shein icon. The screen turns into a pause card: "Day 47 of your no-buy year. $1,284 kept. That's your Lisbon flight." The user taps "Close the app" and a jar visibly fills.

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** the user estimates monthly impulse spend and picks the apps that tempt them. The app shows "That's $3,600 a year, a trip to Japan," then the user names a goal and sets the blocker.
- **Daily action:** an evening check-in ("Did you buy anything off-list today?"). Intercept screens fire through the day.
- **Streak/reward:** days without impulse buys, with two freezes. The money-kept jar grows, with milestones at $100, $500 and $1,000.
- **Shareable artifact:** a "No Buy Year: Day 100, $2,140 kept" card in underconsumption-core style, plus a monthly "things I didn't buy" list (a common TikTok format already).
- **Paywall:** after the goal reveal, $29.99/yr preselected plus $4.99/mo. A hard paywall may meet resistance from an audience that is cutting spending. A soft paywall that gates the blocker and SOS features is safer.

## Risks
- **Irony of monetisation:** asking people on a no-buy year to buy a subscription is an obvious objection. Pricing must be framed against money saved, and a lifetime price may suit this audience better.
- **Seasonality:** demand spikes in January and fades, so retention after the first quarter is uncertain.
- **Platform:** the same Screen Time API dependence as blocker apps. On Android, access to shopping apps is easier.
- **Low ceiling:** r/nobuy is small, and no competitor shows revenue. The market may be a hobbyist niche.
- **Regulatory:** low. It is not financial advice if it stays a habit tool.

## Verdict
**Maybe, as a seasonal bet.** The trend is real and the point-of-purchase blocker is a wedge no one has built. But no app in the niche shows meaningful revenue, and the audience wants to spend less by definition. It is worth a cheap test launched in December 2026 for "No Buy 2027", but not as a main bet.
