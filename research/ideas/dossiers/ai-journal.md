# AI Journal: a journal that notices your patterns and asks the next question

## One-line pitch
Talk or type for two minutes. The AI asks one sharp follow-up question, and every Sunday it shows you a pattern you did not see ("You mention feeling drained after every call with your manager").

## Who exactly
Beachhead: therapy-curious adults aged 22 to 40 who have tried journaling and stopped. Communities include r/Journaling and productivity/self-improvement TikTok; I did not get verified sizes within budget. Screensdesign lists the journal category at 78.5K average installs a month per app (third-party).

## Demand evidence
- Rosebud raised a $6M seed round (TechCrunch, June 2025). Users have logged 500M words and 30M+ minutes (company, via StartupHub). This is the clearest venture signal for AI journaling.
- Screensdesign journal category: average $186.8K/month revenue, but its top app is a general AI chatbot, so the average is inflated (third-party).
- Many indie AI journals appear on TrustMRR (Loopself, Chataq AI, Dayflix, Stoxia), which shows that building one is easy and selling one is hard.

## Money proof
- Rosebud: about $100K/month (Screensdesign estimate). Prospeo models about $3.6M/yr (estimate). A teardown blog claims $800K MRR (unverified, and wrong on funding, so discard it). Priced at $12.99/month or $69/yr.
- Day One: about $350K/month (Screensdesign estimate). The established leader, with a private-diary positioning.
- Daylio: about $100K/month; Gratitude Plus about $250K/month; Liven (AI life coach) about $350K/month (Screensdesign estimates).
- Reflectly: under $5K/month; Stoic: about $15K/month, or under $10K on AppGoblin with about 203K MAU (third-party). These were early leaders in prompted journaling and have faded.
- Indie verified (TrustMRR): Chataq AI $2.8K over 30 days from 6 subscriptions; Stoxia about $152/month; Loopself has no active subscriptions.

## Competitor weaknesses
I did not pull 1 to 2 star reviews. The structural weaknesses are visible in the numbers above. Reflectly and Stoic fell from category leaders to under $15K/month, which suggests prompted journaling churns once the novelty wears off. Rosebud charges $12.99/month, which is close to therapy-app pricing. Day One is a beautiful archive with little intelligence. Generic chatbots (ChatGPT itself) now do "reflect on this" for free, and that is the real competitor.

## The wedge
Pattern evidence, not chat. Most AI journals respond to today's entry. The wedge is the weekly "pattern report", which links entries across weeks with quoted evidence ("3 of your 4 worst days followed less than 6 hours of sleep") and connects to Apple Health sleep and steps data. Voice-first, two minutes, one follow-up question, then done.

## The 2-second clip
A voice memo waveform turns into a card: "Pattern found: you feel anxious every Sunday night, 7 weeks in a row", with three highlighted quotes from past entries. Weak as a clip, because it needs weeks of data to be real.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: answer three questions about a recent hard week. The AI reflects back one non-obvious link within 60 seconds.
- Daily action: a 2-minute voice entry plus one follow-up question.
- Streak: bend-not-break, with "entries this week" as the central measure.
- Shareable: little. Content is private by nature, so at most an anonymised "my year in moods" card.
- Paywall: after the first reflection, $59.99/yr with a 7-day trial. The pattern report is premium.

## Risks
Mental health adjacency: an AI giving advice on distress needs crisis routing and careful App Store wording. Privacy is the product, so data-handling failures are fatal. LLM cost per active user is real against $5/month revenue. General chatbots with memory (ChatGPT, Claude) can copy the core feature for free. Retention history in the category is poor.

## Scores

## Verdict
Skip for a solo founder. Demand exists and Rosebud has raised money, but the category churns (Reflectly and Stoic faded) and the output is private, so there is no viral artifact. General-purpose chatbots with memory erode the core feature. Revisit only as a feature inside a narrower product (for example, a sobriety or ADHD app).
