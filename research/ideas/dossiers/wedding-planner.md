# AI Wedding Planner

## One-line pitch
Tell the app your date, city, guest count and budget, and it builds a full plan in seconds: budget split by category, a week-by-week checklist, a guest list with RSVPs, vendor tracker and a countdown widget, with an AI planner you can ask "can we afford a photo booth?".

## Who exactly
Beachhead: newly engaged couples, usually the bride, 25 to 35, planning a 6 to 18 month engagement on a mid budget, or micro weddings that feel ignored by vendor marketplaces. Where they gather: TikTok and Pinterest wedding content, r/weddingplanning, bride Facebook groups. I did not get verified community sizes this round. Optional second market: India (WedMeGood reports average platform budgets around Rs 39.5 lakh in 2025, up 8%, self-reported), where multi-event weddings make planning harder.

## Demand evidence
- Steady spend: US average about $36,000 (Zola 2026 First Look report, self-reported survey), $30,119 (The Wedding Report via Joy), about $34,000-$35,000 (The Knot via Upstart/MoneyLion). UK GBP 20,604 (Bridebook, ~7,000 couples) and GBP 21,990 (Hitched, 2,020 newlyweds); 56% overspent their budget (Hitched).
- Big app usage: The Knot app about 462K MAU, 231K ratings; Zola app about 210K MAU, 98K ratings (AppGoblin, third-party).
- India: market sizing ranges from about $33B to $149B depending on definition (Ken Research, IMARC, others; analyst estimates of low reliability). 66.8% of services still offline (IMARC).
- The Knot launched an app inside ChatGPT (press release), signalling incumbents moving into AI planning.

## Money proof
- Incumbents earn from registries, invitations and vendor ads, not subscriptions: Zola's app is under $10K/month in IAP (AppGoblin) while company revenue estimates range $44M to $397M (aggregators, unreliable). Zola reportedly takes about 20% on experience gifts (third-party analysis).
- Indie subscription planners show almost nothing: "Wedding Planner: Your Big Day" 1 rating, "Wedding Planner & Invites" 15 ratings, about 40 MAU (AppGoblin). No TrustMRR listing found.
- So couples spend tens of thousands on the wedding, but the planning tools are free, which anchors willingness to pay near zero.

## Competitor weaknesses
Zola reviews: registry gifts vanishing from the tracker, 74 of 111 save-the-date texts failing during unannounced maintenance with slow support ("I used Google Gemini to help me resolve the issue"), guest list search that only finds primary guests, calendar invites with no time zone, and a micro-wedding couple ($6,000, 20 guests) saying it "pretty much only offer[s] you options to book or pay for" vendors. Pattern: incumbents are vendor marketplaces wearing a planner costume.

## The wedge
Budget-first and vendor-neutral: an AI planner that fights for your budget (flags overspend, suggests cheaper swaps, tracks deposits and due dates), handles messy guest lists (households, plus-ones, multiple events for Indian or multicultural weddings), and never sells you vendors. Micro and budget weddings plus multi-event South Asian weddings are underserved niches.

## The 2-second clip
Type "June 2027, Austin, 120 guests, $30K". The screen fills with a budget donut ($12K venue, $4.2K photo) and "47 tasks, 214 days to go" countdown. Caption: "AI planned my whole wedding budget in 3 seconds".

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: the instant budget breakdown and checklist from four inputs.
- Daily/weekly action: this week's 3 tasks, log a payment, update RSVPs.
- Streak/reward: countdown widget and progress ring ("62% planned"); weekly rhythm, not daily.
- Shareable artifact: countdown and "our wedding in numbers" cards, engagement announcement template.
- Paywall: onboarding wall after the plan is generated; one-time "until the wedding" pass at $39.99 (fits a finite 12-month need better than a subscription), or $9.99/month.

## Risks
- Finite lifetime: every user churns after the wedding; no lasting habit, LTV capped at one purchase.
- Free incumbents: Zola, The Knot, Joy give planning away to sell registry and invitations; The Knot is already in ChatGPT.
- Generic AI: ChatGPT itself does a credible budget plan for free.
- Low risk on regulation and App Store; India adds language and payment complexity.

| **Total** | **18/30** | |

## Verdict
Maybe, leaning skip. Wedding spend and planning stress are real, and Zola's reviews show a vendor-neutral, budget-first planner has a story, but free incumbents have trained couples not to pay for planning and every customer churns on the wedding day. Worth a small test only as a one-time-purchase "budget planner" aimed at micro weddings or South Asian multi-event weddings.
