# Photo to Quote

## One-line pitch
A tradesperson snaps two or three photos on site, says a sentence ("replace 40 feet of fence, cedar"), and sends the client a branded, itemised quote before getting back in the van.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: solo and two-person residential trades who quote many small jobs a week and lose evenings to paperwork: painters, fencing, handymen, landscapers, pressure washers. Jobber claims 200,000 home service pros on its own pages and Housecall Pro claims 200K+ (both marketing claims), which shows the size of the paying market. Trades content ("how I price a job", "day in the life of a painter") is a large TikTok genre, and owners gather in trade Facebook groups and subreddits such as r/Contractor and r/HandymanBusiness (sizes not checked this round).

## Demand evidence
- Handoff (1build) says 40K contractors use its AI estimating and it reached 10,000+ monthly active users in 14 months; it raised a $5.8M strategic round in June 2025 with Nemetschek and Masco (press release, self-reported usage).
- A wave of 2025-2026 iOS entrants: Cost Estimator - Construction+ (Brite), SnapScope AI Bid Estimator, EZ Estimates AI, SimplyWise Cost Estimator, Markate's Kate AI ("customer photos into job estimates in under 60 seconds"), Clear Estimates' photo feature.
- Jobber's quarterly Home Service Economic Reports describe "stabilization" in 2025 and a steady 2026, so the base market is healthy.

## Money proof
- Jobber: about $167.5M revenue in 2024, up from $143.2M (Latka / geo.sig.ai, third-party estimate); raised a $100M Series D (BetaKit).
- Handoff: $5.8M round 2025; PitchBook lists $22.8M raised (third-party).
- Cost Estimator - Construction+: a reviewer reports being charged $79 (App Store review), so trades accept high-priced weekly or monthly plans.
- An iOS photo-to-estimate app listed for sale on Approck showed $20K MRR in a search snippet (unverified listing data).
- TrustMRR: BuildCost AI shows $589 30-day revenue (verified, tiny), so being in the category does not guarantee revenue.

## Competitor weaknesses
App Store reviews of Cost Estimator - Construction+:
- "Very, very inaccurate... gave me the same price on each one" for jobs needing 12 versus 30 yards of concrete; "if you rely on this, this app will 100% make you lose work."
- "Application stop working... they already charged my account for $79."
- A positive review still says "you still need to know how to estimate". Jobber and Housecall Pro are full field-service suites with monthly prices aimed at teams, and quoting is one module among many.

## The wedge
Do not guess prices from the internet. Learn the contractor's own price book from three past quotes, then use photos and voice only to fill in scope and quantities, with every line editable. Start with one trade where photos map cleanly to quantities (interior painting: rooms, walls, trim) and make the client-facing PDF beautiful, with e-sign and deposit link. Add a review request after the job (absorbing the review-responder idea).

## The 2-second clip
Phone points at a living room wall, shutter sound, and a crisp itemised quote ("2 coats, 420 sq ft walls, trim, $1,840") lands on the client's phone with an Accept button. Caption: "I quoted this job before leaving the driveway."

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: pick trade, enter hourly rate and markup, photograph anything nearby, see a real quote with your logo in under a minute.
- Weekly job: every site visit is a quote; dashboard of sent, viewed and accepted quotes.
- Reward: "won this week: $X" and win-rate tracking; follow-up nudges on unviewed quotes.
- Shareable artifact: every quote goes to a homeowner with a small "Made with" footer, a built-in product-led loop into other trades the homeowner hires.
- Paywall: after the first quote is sent (aha delivered); $29/month or $199/year, framed as "one won job pays for the year".

## Risks
Accuracy liability: a bad quote costs the trade real money, so AI must assist, not decide. Crowded with funded players (Handoff, Jobber adding AI) and many fresh clones. Retention depends on becoming the system of record; competing with full suites means integrating with QuickBooks and Jobber rather than replacing them. Low regulatory risk.

## Verdict
Build, cautiously. It is the strongest of this batch: real B2B spend, a quote that travels to new customers on its own, and clear incumbent weaknesses on accuracy. Win by starting with one trade and the contractor's own prices rather than "AI local pricing".
