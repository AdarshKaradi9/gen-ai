# Strength Skill Tree ("Level Up Your Body")

## One-line pitch
Your calisthenics or gym progress as an RPG skill tree: every push-up variation, pull-up and lift is a node you unlock by passing a short test, with a character level that rises as you get stronger.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
Men aged 16-30 who train at home or in a park with bodyweight, follow calisthenics creators, and grew up on games and anime. A sub-niche is "Solo Leveling" fans who want a real-life "System" quest log.
- Calisthenics subreddits and Discords are large, but I did not find current member counts in my searches.
- The "Solo Leveling workout" trend has spawned many apps (Arise Solo, Solo X Player, Solo Hunter, Workout Gods, Solo System), a sign of audience pull.

## Demand evidence (searches, communities, trend signals, with sources)
- CaliQuest (launched Jan 2026): about 39K Android installs, 3.1K installs a week and "strong growth" (AppGoblin, Oct 2026, third-party estimate).
- At least six skill-tree calisthenics apps exist (Calistree, CaliStack, CaliFlow, CaliQuest, Caliverse, Sport Is My Game), plus a Product Hunt "Solo Leveling System" launch (Jan 2026).
- Hevy, the leading gym logger, claims 4M+ users on its listing; older RevenueCat interview said 2M and profitable (self-reported).

## Money proof (competitors with revenue/downloads, pricing, labelled by evidence type)
- Hevy: revenue under $5M (ZoomInfo, third-party estimate); about $4M raised; Pro $2.99/month, $23.99/year, $74.99 lifetime (App Store snapshot, Apr 2026).
- Calxthenics: 10K downloads and $50K+/month in early months (Passion.io case study, self-reported, vendor marketing).
- CaliStack: 32 active subscriptions (TrustMRR, RevenueCat-verified, Nov 2026), so tiny.
- CaliQuest, Fitloop, Solo X Player: each under $10K/month (AppGoblin, third-party estimates).
- A gamified RPG fitness web platform sold for $19K at about $18.1K ARR and 350 customers (Microns listing, self-reported).
- Net: the category is crowded with apps earning under $10K/month; the big winner (Hevy) wins on logging, social feed and low price, not RPG mechanics.

## Competitor weaknesses (what 1-2 star reviews and complaints say)
- I did not run review searches for this idea, so I have no sourced complaint themes. From listings: RPG/"System" apps are mostly checklists with XP stickers rather than real progression, and skill-tree calisthenics apps lack a tracker as good as Hevy's. Treat this as my reading of listings, not review evidence.
- The playbook warns that points and badges alone do not retain; an emotional stake and small groups do.

## The wedge (the specific angle that beats incumbents)
Make unlocks earned and verified: each node has a camera-checked test (for example, phone counts reps and checks a 10-second L-sit hold with pose estimation), so a level means something and can be shown off. Combine a real Hevy-grade logger with the tree, and give a character whose look changes with real strength. Small friend "parties" race to the same unlock.

## The 2-second clip
A dark skill-tree screen; a node labelled "Muscle-up" glows, cracks open with a game sound, and the level counter jumps from 23 to 24 while the clip cuts to the real person's first muscle-up.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: 3 quick tests (max push-ups, dead hang, squat), then your tree lights up with your starting level and the next 3 unlocks.
- Daily action: today's quest (a 20-30 minute session chosen to push the nearest node).
- Streak/reward: weekly training streak with freezes, XP, level-ups, rank badges; party challenges.
- Shareable artifact: skill-tree card showing unlocked nodes and level, plus unlock clips.
- Paywall: after the tree reveal; $6.99/month or $39.99/year. Hevy's $23.99/year anchors the price low.

## Risks (regulatory, platform, saturation, retention)
- Saturation: many RPG and skill-tree clones, mostly small.
- Injury liability for advanced skills; pose-checked tests must stay conservative.
- Progression is slow (weeks per node), so level-ups are rare after the beginner phase.
- Low price anchors from Hevy, Strong and free YouTube programs.

## Scores
| Criterion | 1-5 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Two-second aha | 3 | Tree reveal is visual, but the real value (getting stronger) takes weeks |
| Visual short-video hook | 3 | Unlock clips work, but rare; I found no hashtag data |

| Daily reason to return | 4 | Training 3-5 times a week is a natural unit |
| Shareable moment | 3 | Skill tree card is shareable inside the niche |
| Unit economics | 2 | Young male audience, $24/yr price anchor, crowded paid channels |
| **Total** | **18/30** | |

## Verdict
Skip as a general product. The gamified framing is well liked but already copied by many apps with little revenue, and the strongest incumbent wins on logging and price. Only worth revisiting if verified, camera-checked unlocks prove to be a strong social hook in a quick prototype.
