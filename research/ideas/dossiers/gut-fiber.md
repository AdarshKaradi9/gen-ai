# Gut Fiber (30 Plants a Week)

## One-line pitch
A fibermaxxing tracker built around the 30-plants-a-week goal: log each plant once, watch a weekly garden fill toward 30, see an estimated fiber total, with an optional private stool log.

## Who exactly
Gen Z and millennial women following the 2026 fibermaxxing trend, people who heard the "30 plants a week" idea from Zoe and Tim Spector, and the constipation/bloating crowd. Communities: TikTok fibermaxxing content (about 150M TikTok views per one outlet, 160M+ across TikTok and Instagram per another, press estimates), r/ibs at about 150K-172K members, r/plantbased and r/vegan (~1.9M) (GummySearch cached snapshots, third-party). GLP-1 users are a second audience, since constipation is common on those drugs and one fiber app already targets them ("Fiber Tracker: Gut & GLP-1").

## Demand evidence
- Google says searches for dietary fiber hit an all-time high in 2026 and "fibermaxxing" searches rose 115% in the prior 90 days (Google Summergeist post, Google Trends data).
- Wide press coverage calling fibermaxxing the 2026 trend (The Week, Bustle, Green Queen, ACSH), mostly positive with dietitian caveats.
- Supply is chasing it: at least seven fiber trackers on the App Store, most launched in 2026 (Fiber Tracker: AI Calculator, Fiberly, FiberUp, Fiber Ace, FiberPal, Fiber Tracker: Gut Health, Fiber Tracker: Gut & GLP-1).

## Money proof
- Plant Points (30 plants a week): 374 ratings, 4.66 stars, ~1.1K MAU, under $10K/month (AppGoblin, third-party). The developer removed ads because they "did not result in enough revenue".
- Fiber Tracker | Plant Power: 128 ratings, ~287 MAU, under $10K/month (AppGoblin).
- 2026 fiber apps: Fiber Tracker: Gut Health ~12 MAU, Fiber Tracker: AI Calculator ~28 MAU (AppGoblin), the latter priced at $49.99/yr or $6.99/week. Pricing exists; buyers so far do not.
- Zoe: £29M revenue in the year to August 2023 (company accounts via press), membership cut from £24.99 to £9.99/month in Dec 2024, layoffs in 2024. People pay for gut health, but Zoe sells test kits and science branding, not a counter.
- Cara Care for IBS: German prescription app (DiGA) reimbursed at €248 per course (press), owned via HiDoc. Money for gut apps flows through clinical channels, not consumer counters.
- "Grove" could not be found as a fiber or gut app in searches.

## Competitor weaknesses
Few reviews exist because the apps are tiny, which is itself the finding. From listings: Plant Points is a plain list with points and streaks; Eating30 and Thirty Plants are free one-person projects; many 2026 apps are interchangeable gram rings. Experts criticise stool-tracking content (main agent findings). Zoe is expensive and kit-led. Nobody has made the weekly 30-plant goal visually rewarding.

## The wedge
Make the week the product: a "plant garden" or rainbow grid where each new plant adds a tile, resetting each Monday, with photo logging of a plate counting every plant in one shot. Fiber grams are shown as an estimate, not the goal. Optional, private stool and bloating log with no diagnoses. Honestly, this wedge is thin against free apps, and it works better as a feature row inside the nutrient body map or as a GLP-1 constipation angle than as a standalone business.

## The 2-second clip
A phone photographs a grain bowl; seven plant icons pop out of the photo and drop into a garden grid; the counter jumps from 14 to 21 of 30.

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** "How many plants did you eat yesterday?" then photo or tap-log, garden starts at that number (progress above zero).
- **Daily action:** log plants per meal; repeats do not count, so novelty is the game.
- **Streak/reward:** weekly unit (hit 30 by Sunday), with week streaks and one freeze; a "new plant this week" nudge.
- **Shareable artifact:** the weekly 30-plant collage, a colourful grid that fits fibermaxxing "what I ate" content.
- **Paywall:** soft wall after the first photo log; photo logging and history paid at $29.99-$39.99/yr.

## Risks
- **Trend fade:** "maxxing" trends are seasonal; Google itself framed it as the "summer of maxxing".
- **Saturation:** seven-plus clones in 2026 and free alternatives, with no incumbent proving revenue.
- **Regulatory:** stool logging and bloating drift toward IBS symptom management, where clinical DiGA apps live; rapid fiber increases cause GI distress, so copy must advise gradual increases.
- **Retention:** weekly units give fewer daily triggers; counting plants gets tedious once novelty passes.

## Verdict
Maybe, leaning skip as a standalone. Demand and content fit are real and rising, but no fiber or plant-count app has shown meaningful revenue and 2026 clones are already piling in. Best use: fold the 30-plants garden into the nutrient body map, or aim it at GLP-1 users where constipation creates a paying need.
