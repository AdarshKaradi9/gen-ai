# Review Responder

## One-line pitch
An AI that drafts on-brand replies to every Google review within minutes and texts happy customers a review link after each job, for local businesses at a tenth of Birdeye or Podium's price.

## Who exactly
Owner-operated local businesses with one to three locations: restaurants, dental and med-spa clinics, salons, plumbers and other trades. They gather in Facebook groups by trade, r/smallbusiness and r/restaurantowners (sizes not checked this round), and are mostly reached through cold outreach and Google Maps listings rather than TikTok.

## Demand evidence
- Google itself is testing "Reply to reviews with AI" inside Business Profile, seen in the US, Brazil and India (Search Engine Land, March 2026). Google building it confirms the need and also threatens the category.
- Product Hunt shows a steady stream of small launches (Replify, ReplyBrew, Review Reply AI, TopTierClass, ReplyPro, ReviewReply AI), and Zapier has a community template for AI review replies with human approval.
- Founder post on dev.to: replies are indexed content and can carry business name, service and city for local SEO, a selling angle owners respond to.

## Money proof
- Birdeye: CFO announced more than $100M ARR (company statement); Latka estimates $146.8M 2024 revenue, 80K customers, 12% churn (third-party estimate). Over 2x growth in $100K+ customers reported January 2026 (press release).
- Costbench "market intelligence": Podium median annual cost $500 from 176 deals; Birdeye $4,788 from 4 deals (third-party, small sample for Birdeye).
- Small AI tools: Replify charges $15/month flat for 4,000 replies (listing). No TrustMRR revenue found for any pure review-reply tool, which is a warning sign.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Birdeye: BBB lists 116 complaints in three years, themes are 12-month lock-in after a sales promise of easy cancellation and auto-renewal without notice; Trustpilot 1.7/5 from 25 reviews versus Capterra 4.7 from 703 (mixed).
- Podium: BBB rating reported D- with 200+ complaints, mostly billing and cancellation; Capterra reviewers cite cost and fee increases.
- Generic AI replies hurt trust on negative reviews (Search Engine Land warning).

## The wedge
Month-to-month, self-serve, under $30 per location, set up in two minutes by connecting the Google Business Profile. Auto-post 4-5 star replies, hold 1-3 stars for a one-tap approval with a "make it right" draft. The second half, review requests via SMS or QR after a job, is where Podium earns its price; doing it cheaply is the real differentiator.

## The 2-second clip
A savage one-star review appears; one tap; a calm, specific owner-voice reply slides in underneath. Caption: "I let AI answer my worst Google review."

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: connect Google profile, instantly see drafts for the last 20 unanswered reviews.
- Weekly job: Monday digest of new reviews, one-tap approve; after each job, send a review request.
- Reward: rating trend and "reviews this month" counter versus local competitors.
- Shareable artifact: weak; maybe a "new 5-star review" social card for Instagram.
- Paywall: 14-day trial, then $19-29/month per location.

## Risks
Google's native AI reply could commoditise the core feature for free. Google Business Profile API access needs approval. Review gating (asking only happy customers) is against Google's review policies and the FTC's 2024 fake-review rule restricts review suppression, so "ask happy customers" must be designed as "ask everyone". Low ARPU plus sales-led acquisition is hard for a solo founder.

## Scores
| Criterion | 1-5 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Two-second aha | 4 | Bad review in, good reply out, clear but text-only |
| Visual short-video hook | 2 | Owners are not a TikTok audience; distribution is outbound |
| Money already swims | 5 | Birdeye over $100M ARR, Podium large, owners already pay |
| Recurring weekly job | 4 | New reviews and new jobs each week |
| Shareable moment | 2 | Nothing users would screenshot |
| Unit economics | 3 | Near-zero COGS, but $15-30 price, B2B CAC and churn |
| **Total** | **20/30** | |

## Verdict
Skip as a standalone product. The money is real but sits with sales-led incumbents, the cheap AI end is already crowded with no visible revenue, and Google is shipping the core feature itself. It works better as a feature inside a vertical tool (for example the photo-to-quote app sending a review request after the job).
