# Desi Plate

## One-line pitch
Snap your thali and get calories, protein, iron and B12 for dal, roti and sabzi in katori and roti units, with a one-tap oil/ghee prompt that fixes the biggest error source, built first for the South Asian diaspora.

## Who exactly
- **Beachhead: diaspora vegetarians and "ghar ka khana" eaters in the US, UK and Canada** who want to lose weight or hit protein targets without giving up home food. Asian Indian-alone population in the US was about 5.41M in the 2025 ACS, up from 5.09M in 2024 (press on ACS data, third-party reporting of a government survey). About 3.4M were born in India. Wider "South Asian alone" estimate about 6.4M (secondary summary, treat cautiously).
- **Secondary: urban India**, larger but low willingness to pay.
- **Where they gather:** desi fitness creators on Instagram/Reels, r/ABCDesis, r/IndianFood, r/indianfitness, WhatsApp family groups. I did not pull current subreddit member counts in this pass.
- **Nutrition angle:** asymptomatic Indian lacto-vegetarians have distinctly low B12 versus non-vegetarians (Naik et al., Nutrition Journal 2013); a Cambridge-hosted paper cites ~70% deficiency among vegetarian Indians (Yajnik et al., cited secondhand). This makes a protein/iron/B12 lens credible, not just calories.

## Demand evidence
- Many apps now market "Indian food calorie tracking" (Nutrimate, Clearcals/Hint, NutriScan, Caliq, RotiCal, VoCal). Their blogs all repeat the same pain: MyFitnessPal entries are crowdsourced, portions are in grams/cups not katori or roti, and "a generic dal entry has no idea about the extra ghee" (vendor content, self-interested but consistent).
- A September 2024 MyFitnessPal App Store review complains about inaccurate Indian food data (via search summary).
- A 2026 Reddit post tests the same idea ("most calorie tracking apps... struggle with indian meals"), a sign of builder interest, not proven user demand.
- Healthify raised $45M in Oct 2024 ($20M fresh) specifically for US expansion, tuning its AI to local food and dialects (LeapFrog press release, Inc42). That is a funded incumbent betting the diaspora/US matters.

## Money proof
- **Healthify (HealthifyMe):** FY24 operating revenue about Rs 206.8 Cr (~$25M), down about 10% year on year; FY23 Rs 228.8 Cr (Inc42 from filings, verified-ish). 35M+ users claimed (self-reported). AI plan reported at ~$20/month in the US (Dec 2025 press). The Ken framed the US push as a "hail mary" after India growth stalled.
- **Cal AI:** acquired by MyFitnessPal in March 2026, revenue reported $30M-$40M/yr (TechCrunch, press). Proves photo logging monetizes in the West.
- **Indian niche apps:** Hint (Clearcals) about 269K Android installs, 25K MAU, revenue under $10K/mo; Cal Plus 15K installs, under $10K/mo; Poshan AI 1.6K installs (all AppGoblin, third-party estimates). RotiCal (US, desi-focused, voice/photo, katori units) prices at $2.99/mo or $19.99/yr and has too few ratings to show (App Store listing).
- I could not find install or revenue data for MetaFit, NutriScan or CalFix.
- **Read:** money clearly swims in photo calorie tracking overall, but desi-specific apps have not yet shown meaningful revenue.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Healthify: Trustpilot ~3/5 from ~526 reviews; themes are aggressive upsells and paywalls, auto-renew and refund disputes, and Snap estimates that "show unrelated amounts" with weak portion calibration (Trustpilot, aggregator summaries; individual opinions). App store rating is still ~4.3 from 574K reviews.
- MyFitnessPal/Cal AI: Indian dishes misidentified, gram/cup portions, no oil adjustment (reviews and vendor blogs).
- Small desi apps: thin polish, few ratings, mostly calorie-only.

## The wedge
Three things incumbents do not do together: (1) katori/roti/ladle units with a visual size picker; (2) an explicit "how much oil/ghee/tadka?" slider after every photo, shown as a range, because no camera sees oil; (3) a vegetarian micronutrient score (protein, iron, B12) with swaps from your own cuisine (add paneer, curd, soya chunks, fortified foods). Position as "made by desis, for mom's cooking," priced in USD/GBP for the diaspora.

## The 2-second clip
Phone hovers over a steel thali (dal, two rotis, bhindi, raita). Snap. Labels pop onto each katori: "Dal 1 katori 180 kcal, 9g protein", then a ghee slider jumps from "1 tsp" to "1 tbsp" and the total visibly rises by ~80 kcal. Caption: "MyFitnessPal thinks this is 400 calories."

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** pick cuisine region (Punjabi, Gujarati, South Indian, Bengali, Pakistani), diet (veg, eggetarian, non-veg), goal. First action is snapping a real meal before any account.
- **Daily action:** log 2-3 home meals; the oil prompt doubles as a learning moment.
- **Streak/reward:** daily protein target ring; bend-not-break streak with two freezes; weekly "B12 and iron check."
- **Shareable artifact:** "My thali, decoded" card and a weekly "protein from dal vs paneer" card, watermarked, for Instagram/WhatsApp family groups.
- **Paywall:** after the first decoded meal and plan reveal; diaspora price $39.99/yr or $7.99/mo with 3-day trial; India pricing much lower, closer to Rs 999/yr.

## Risks
- **Saturation:** Cal AI/MyFitnessPal and Healthify can add better Indian recognition; at least six small desi trackers already exist (RotiCal launched with the exact katori/ghee pitch).
- **Accuracy:** mixed curries and hidden oil keep error high; credibility rests on honest ranges.
- **Unit economics:** India CPIs are cheap but ARPU is very low (Healthify revenue shrinking); diaspora is a few million people, so paid US/UK CPIs must be beaten by organic creator content.
- **Medical:** micronutrient claims (B12) must stay informational.

## Verdict
Maybe, leaning build only as a diaspora-first, protein-for-vegetarians product. The demand and the wedge (katori units plus oil prompt) are real, but the space is filling with small clones, and a well-funded incumbent (Healthify) is entering the US. The diaspora is the better beachhead than India: higher willingness to pay, an underserved Western app stack, and family WhatsApp distribution back to India later.
