# Vedic Kundli: English-first Vedic astrology for the Indian diaspora

## One-line pitch
An AI Vedic astrologer for second-generation Indians abroad: plain-English kundli, honest marriage-match reports their parents will accept, and daily rashifal, with no fear-based remedy upsells and priced in dollars.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: US-based Indians aged 25 to 35, mainly women, facing marriage decisions, and their parents. Astrotalk's own customer profile matches: core age 25 to 35, 60% women, and about 65% of revenue from marriage questions (self-reported by the CEO to Inc42, 2026). I did not find verified diaspora community sizes within budget. Channels to test are Instagram desi creators, WhatsApp family groups, and matrimonial-site users.

## Demand evidence
- Astrotalk's international business, launched in late 2024, made up about 20% of operating revenue in FY25 and 37% in FY26, and the US is more than 60% of that international revenue (CEO, self-reported to Inc42). This is the strongest diaspora willingness-to-pay signal found.
- AstroSage claims 80M+ downloads and 1.5M DAU (company press release run as a sponsored ANI piece, Feb 2026). AppGoblin shows 58.4M Android installs and about 154K iOS MAU (third-party).
- Screensdesign's Vedic astrology category averages $160.7K/month revenue (third-party; note that its list mixes in Western apps like Nebula).

## Money proof
- Astrotalk: operating revenue Rs 283 Cr (FY23), Rs 651 Cr (FY24) and Rs 1,176 Cr (FY25), with FY26 expected at about Rs 1,850 Cr. FY25 PAT about Rs 33 Cr after exceptional costs. Unicorn valuation via an ESOP buyback, IPO planned (FY25 from filings via Inc42; FY26 self-reported). Average consultation order about Rs 210. Its store sells rudraksha and crystals: Rs 140 Cr in 2025 (self-reported).
- AstroSage: Rs 60 Cr revenue in FY24, up 59.5% (filings via press). It claims 90% margins and 20% month-on-month growth on its AI product (sponsored release, unverified).
- Kundli GPT: a paid Pro tier exists, but I found no revenue or user numbers.
- Small indie: Tatva (AI Vedic astrology plus wellness) has no active subscriptions, and Sripatro (Nepali panchang) has one (TrustMRR, verified). So a thin AI wrapper alone does not sell.

## Competitor weaknesses
I did not pull 1 to 2 star reviews. From press: the Indian model is per-minute chat with astrologers at about Rs 20 to 200 per minute (vendor blog, unverified), and Storyboard18 reports rising regulatory concern over the category. Remedy and gemstone upsells are now Astrotalk's growth engine, which feeds the fear-selling image. AstroSage's interface is dense and Hindi/jargon-first (its feature list runs from Lal Kitab to KP cusps), which suits experts, not a 28-year-old in New Jersey.

## The wedge
Translate, don't upsell. Diaspora users want something their parents trust (proper Vedic maths, Lahiri ayanamsa, 36-guna matching) explained in English they understand, at a flat dollar price instead of per-minute chat. Sell a "Match report for your parents" PDF that can go straight into the family WhatsApp group. AI handles follow-up questions at near-zero marginal cost. Astrotalk is also now an AI-disruption target, per Inc42.

## The 2-second clip
Two birth details go in, a 36-point guna meter fills to "28/36 Great match", and a plain-English line appears: "Your Mangal dosha cancels out, tell your mom."

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: birth details, then your rashi, nakshatra and current dasha explained in one screen of plain English.
- Daily action: rashifal plus a "today's muhurat" line for planning.
- Streak: weekly rather than daily. The natural rhythm is festivals, transits (Saturn's sade sati) and dasha changes.
- Shareable: the guna-match card and the parent-ready PDF.
- Paywall: $29.99/yr, or a one-time $14.99 match report. Unlimited AI questions are paid.

## Risks
Retention: demand spikes around marriage decisions and then fades, and Astrotalk's marriage-heavy mix suggests episodic usage. US CPI against Indian-style price expectations. Regulation in India (advertising and consumer-protection scrutiny) if the app ever targets India. AI errors in chart calculation would destroy credibility. Astrotalk is already pushing into the US with large budgets.

## Verdict
Maybe. The money is clearly there, and Astrotalk's US revenue is the best evidence of diaspora spending in this batch. But the money comes from human consultations and remedies, not app subscriptions, and usage is episodic. Worth a test as a one-time paid match report with a subscription attached, not as a daily-habit app.
