# Gig Driver Tax

## One-line pitch
Automatic mileage, expenses and a live "set aside this week" tax number for Uber, DoorDash and Instacart drivers, so April never brings a surprise bill.

## Who exactly
US multi-app rideshare and delivery drivers, especially full-timers who earn above the 1099-K reporting threshold. Headcounts are fuzzy: blog sources put DoorDash Dashers at roughly 7-8 million in 2024 and 2 million monthly active in 2023 (third-party, conflicting; no primary 2025 figure found). Drivers gather in r/doordash_drivers, r/uberdrivers, r/InstacartShoppers and Facebook groups (sizes not checked), and post earnings screenshots on TikTok constantly.

## Demand evidence
- The tax rules keep moving: the 2026 standard mileage rate is reported at 72.5 cents per mile, with several blogs reporting a mid-year rise to 76 cents from 1 July 2026 (not confirmed on IRS.gov). The One Big Beautiful Bill Act added a qualified-tips deduction for 2025-2028, and whether app-based contractors qualify is described as an open question. Confusion drives searches and content.
- Every major player publishes 2026 tax and mileage-rate explainers (Gridwise, Everlance, Solo, QuickBooks), a sign of search demand they compete for.

## Money proof
- Everlance: about 2.7M iOS installs (54K ratings) and 2.4M Android installs (AppGoblin, third-party estimate); "over 1 million users" claim (vendor copy).
- Gridwise: about 1.4M iOS and 1.2M Android installs (AppGoblin, third-party).
- Solo: claims 1M+ gig workers, $8B+ earnings data, $25M+ in guaranteed pay delivered, 4.7 stars (company site, self-reported).
- Stride: free mileage and expense tracker, monetised via health-insurance marketplace (Gridwise comparison).
No revenue figures found for any of them. The anchor competitors are free or freemium, which caps willingness to pay.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Stride needs manual start/stop, so deadhead miles get missed (Gridwise's own comparison, biased source).
- Gridwise has no in-app tax filing or automatic expense tracking per Solo's comparison (biased source).
- Everlance is built for general self-employed users, not gig specifics.
1-2 star review themes could not be pulled this round (App Store page did not render); battery drain and missed trips are typical for background GPS apps but are not verified here.

## The wedge
One number, not a ledger: after each shift, "You made $186, keep $151, move $35 to taxes", with quarterly estimated payment reminders and a pre-filled 1040-ES figure. Bilingual (Spanish) by default, since many delivery drivers are underserved by English-only tax tools. That said, Solo already claims "projects your taxes year-round".

## The 2-second clip
End-of-shift screen: miles counter ticking up to 142, then a big green "Tax saved: $103" from the mileage deduction. Caption: "DoorDash doesn't tell you this."

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: enter last week's earnings and typical miles, see "you are on track to owe $4,900 in April".
- Weekly job: auto-tracked shifts, a Sunday "weekly payout split" with set-aside amount.
- Reward: running "deductions found this year" counter, streak of weeks set aside.
- Shareable artifact: weekly earnings card with real hourly after expenses (drivers already share earnings screenshots).
- Paywall: after the first projection; $7.99/month or $49/year, timed to quarterly deadlines and January tax season.

## Risks
Low ARPU and strong free incumbents with millions of installs. Background location must earn its permission and drains battery. Tax-estimate errors invite complaints; it must stay an estimate, not advice. Earnings sync depends on unofficial gig-platform connections that platforms can break. Strong seasonality (January to April) and churn after filing.

## Verdict
Skip. The habit is perfect but the market is held by well-installed free or freemium apps (Everlance, Gridwise, Stride, Solo) and drivers are price-sensitive. Only a Spanish-first or tax-filing-included angle might carve a niche, and Solo is already moving there.
