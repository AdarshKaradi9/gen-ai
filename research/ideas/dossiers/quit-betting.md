# Quit Betting

## One-line pitch
A Quittr-style app for young US men quitting sports betting: a "days bet-free" streak, a running "money not lost" counter, an SOS screen for game-day urges, and a one-tap blocker for sportsbook apps (DraftKings, FanDuel, prediction markets), timed to the NFL and NBA calendar.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: US men aged 21 to 34 who bet on mobile sportsbooks. Pennsylvania reports that men, mostly aged 25 to 34, make more than two-thirds of its hotline calls every year (Axios, Dec 2025). A Pew figure, cited by an aggregator, says 36% of men under 30 placed a sports bet in the past year. I could not confirm the size of online communities (r/problemgambling, r/GamblingAddiction) in this pass.

## Demand evidence
- The National Problem Gambling Helpline's 2025 annual report says more than 31,000 people reached out each month in 2025, with callers getting younger (NCPG, primary source).
- Pennsylvania logged a record number of hotline calls in 2025 (Axios). North Carolina's helpline answered over 8,100 calls in the last fiscal year, up more than 11% (NC Newsline). The New York State Comptroller (Sep 2026) reported a surge in mobile betting and a jump in problem-gambling calls.
- One aggregator puts 2025 legal sports betting handle at $166.9B, up 11%, attributed to the AGA. I could not find the AGA release, so this is unverified.
- Supply signal: at least ten new quit-gambling apps launched in 2025 and 2026 (Gamblr, Anchor, Spinless, Bettr, QuitRisk, Bet Free, Lessen, CashOut launched June 2026, "i'm done", Cashed Out, Betless). Founders clearly see the opportunity.

## Money proof
Weak for app subscriptions.
- Gamban (blocker): subscription with a 7-day trial. One low-quality guide lists about $99/yr (unverified). In the UK it is often given free through GamCare.
- BetBlocker: free and widely recommended, which caps what a pure blocker can charge.
- "i'm done. Quit Sports Betting": about $6.99/week (listing), with 1 rating.
- Quit Gambling by Cashed Out: about 234 MAU and 15 ratings (AppGoblin, third-party estimate).
- Betless (quit gambling) is listed for sale on Flippa. I could not read the listing's revenue figures.
- I found no revenue for any quit-betting app and no Reframe gambling data. Willingness to pay is plausible by analogy with Quittr's $12.99/mo hard paywall and with users who have lost thousands, but it is unproven here.

## Competitor weaknesses
None of the new apps has enough reviews to mine complaint themes. Most have 0 to 15 ratings. From listings and comparisons:
- Blockers (Gamban, BetBlocker, Bet Free) block but do nothing emotional or habit-forming: no streak, no money counter, no urge support.
- Many new streak apps are generic Quittr clones with nothing specific to sports, such as game-day timing, parlay urges or "sweat" rituals.
- Lessen (Android) uses the user's actual betting history, which is the most sophisticated idea seen, but it targets cutting back rather than quitting.
- Free nonprofit resources (1-800-GAMBLER, GamCare) are trusted but not in the form of an app.

## The wedge
1. **Sports-calendar urge support.** The app knows the user's teams and sends an SOS check-in before kickoff and during prime time ("Sunday 12:55pm is your riskiest hour"), the moments generic apps miss.
2. **Money not lost, made concrete.** Users import or estimate past losses, so the counter compounds into "you've kept $2,300 since September".
3. **Blocker plus an honest exit.** One-tap blocking of sportsbook and prediction-market apps, plus self-exclusion checklists for each state.
4. **Masculine, sports-native design.** It looks like a sports app, not a wellness app, so users don't feel embarrassed using it in front of friends.

## The 2-second clip
A Sunday NFL scoreboard on TV in the background. The phone shows the DraftKings icon tapped and replaced by a dark card: "Day 61 bet-free. $3,140 kept. Watch the game, not the line." A "Call a friend" button pulses.

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** the user enters average weekly bets and losses since they started, and the app shows "You've lost about $11,400. At this pace: $9,000 more by the Super Bowl." They pick a quit date, teams and blocker apps.
- **Daily action:** a morning check-in, plus game-day SOS prompts and urge logging.
- **Streak/reward:** bet-free days, a money-kept counter, and season milestones ("Bet-free through Week 10").
- **Shareable artifact:** a private-by-default milestone card ("100 days bet-free"). Shame limits sharing, so expect anonymous Reddit and TikTok posts rather than personal shares.
- **Paywall:** a hard paywall after the loss reveal, $39.99/yr preselected plus $9.99/mo. Avoid weekly pricing aimed at people in financial trouble.

## Risks
- **Ad platforms:** Meta treats addiction treatment as a restricted category that can require LegitScript certification and prior written permission for US ads (third-party policy record; current wording unconfirmed). A recovery app may be flagged even if it does not offer treatment. Paid acquisition may be slow or blocked, so organic content would carry the load.
- **App Store and ethics:** charging people in debt from gambling, especially through weekly subscriptions, invites review backlash and press criticism. Any clinical claim pushes toward health regulation. The app must route users to 1-800-GAMBLER and stay in the habit-tool lane.
- **Saturation:** many 2025 and 2026 clones, plus free blockers (BetBlocker) and free helplines.
- **Shareability:** stigma limits word of mouth.
- **Upside:** the problem is growing and in the news. State lottery and gaming regulators fund responsible-gambling tools, a possible B2B2C channel.

## Verdict
**Skip as a lead consumer bet; maybe as a mission-driven side project or a B2B2C play.** The need is growing and well documented by helpline data, but the monetisation proof is missing, paid acquisition is restricted, sharing is limited by shame, and a crowd of 2026 clones already exists. A sports-calendar-aware SOS is the one wedge worth stealing if someone builds it anyway.
