# Glow-Up Food (skin glow-up through food and habits, with a selfie timeline)

## One-line pitch
Log water, sugar, sleep and skin-friendly foods, take one consistent daily selfie, and watch a timeline that shows what your skin did after your best and worst weeks.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
Women 16 to 30 with mild acne or dull skin who already follow "eat your skincare" and "I quit sugar for 30 days" content, plus young men from the looksmaxxing scene who want a non-toxic plan. They gather on TikTok (skincare and glow-up content), Lemon8, r/SkincareAddiction and r/acne (sizes not checked; Reddit blocked our fetcher). Scale signal: Umax reports 7M+ downloads (founder via Fortune, self-reported) and the skin cycling hashtag reportedly passed 3.5B views (press, third-party).

## Demand evidence (searches, communities, trend signals, with sources)
- 2026 TikTok trends include "eating your skincare" (salmon skin, carrot "Botox salads") and the sardine diet for clearer skin, with dermatologist pushback (2026 press and blogs).
- Looksmaxxing app supply exploded: one 2026 comparison lists 25 apps, 16 of 19 App Store ones with weekly plans; another counts 16 Umax lookalikes.
- Earlier findings: "I quit sugar for a month" TikTok content is popular while quit-sugar apps are small.
- No hashtag view counts for "clear skin diet" or "glow up diet" were found.

## Money proof (competitors with revenue/downloads, pricing, labelled by evidence type)
- **Umax:** about $500K/month subscription revenue and 7M+ downloads (founder Blake Anderson via Fortune, self-reported); a modelled estimate says about $150K/month (third-party estimate). 4.6 stars on about 51,600 ratings. Weekly $3.99 to $9.99.
- **DailyGlowUp (closest comparable: daily glow-up tracking with streaks):** MRR $13,537, last-28-day revenue $19,492, 1,163 active subscriptions, 7.3K downloads in 28 days, revenue per download $2.29, LTV $116; launched January 2026; listed for sale at $450K (RevenueCat-verified on whatsthe.app, Aug 2026). A solo-scale app reaching about $160K ARR in seven months is the strongest signal here.
- **AbMaxx:** about $17.5K MRR (RevenueCat-verified, whatsthe.app) shows the "maxxing" framing monetizes beyond faces.
- **TroveSkin** (selfie skin tracker): about 2.3M Android downloads (AppBrain, third-party), seed stage; no revenue found. **SkinSort:** 50K+ Play downloads, about 250K total (aggregator); no revenue found.

## Competitor weaknesses (what 1-2 star reviews and complaints say)
From 1,559 one- and two-star reviews of 12 face-rating and glow-up apps (Escape the Matrix analysis, Oct 2026; the publisher sells a competing app):
- 67.6% mention paying or subscriptions; 31.1% say results were locked until payment, after uploading photos.
- 30.1% use "scam", "fake" or "misleading"; 14.4% say the scan never finished, often after paying; 11.4% report unexpected charges.
- Inconsistent scores are a smaller theme (4.4%), plus a few say the app "will just make you insecure".
- Skin trackers like SkinSort focus on product routines, not food or habits. None of the face-rating apps link diet to skin over time.

## The wedge (the specific angle that beats incumbents)
Score-free and cause-and-effect. Instead of rating your face, the app shows your own correlations: "your three clearest days followed 2L+ water and under 25g added sugar" with matched selfies. Use a guided capture (same distance, lighting check, face outline) so the timeline is honest, which incumbents ignore. Show the first insight before paying and say exactly when the charge happens, directly answering the top complaint. Target women first, where looksmaxxing apps are weakest.

## The 2-second clip
A 30-day selfie timelapse morphing from red, bumpy cheeks to calm skin, with a small strip under the face ticking through days: water drops filling, a sugar bar shrinking, sleep hours rising. End frame: "Day 30. No new products. Just food."

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** five questions (skin goal, sugar habit, water, sleep, dairy), a guided first selfie, and a personalised 30-day plan that visibly uses the answers.
- **Daily action:** one guided selfie plus four taps (water, sugar, sleep, one skin-friendly food). Photo food logging is optional, later.
- **Streak/reward:** selfie streak with two freezes; a weekly "skin report" that pairs your best and worst days with what you ate.
- **Shareable artifact:** auto-built 7, 14 and 30-day timelapse with a habit strip, watermarked.
- **Paywall:** last onboarding screen after the plan reveal, with one free insight first. $39.99/yr preselected and $6.99/week, 3-day trial. DailyGlowUp's $2.29 revenue per download suggests this can work.

## Risks (regulatory, platform, saturation, retention)
- **App Store:** no face-rating-specific guideline was found; the 16 Oct 2026 revisions target non-consensual intimate imagery and apps that behave differently after review (new 2.5.19). Face photos still trigger privacy rules and disclosure. Avoiding scores lowers rejection and reputational risk.
- **Minors and mental health:** the audience skews teen; insecurity complaints exist. Set 17+ or strong safeguards, no attractiveness ratings.
- **Medical claims:** diet's effect on acne is modest and contested; "no miracle foods" per dermatologists. Frame as tracking, not treatment.
- **Data noise:** lighting and makeup make timelines unreliable without guided capture.
- **Saturation:** 25+ glow-up apps, though nearly all are face raters.
- **Retention:** visible skin change takes weeks; days 1 to 7 must be carried by streaks and habit insights.

## Verdict
Build. Money and content format are proven by face-rating apps, and a verified small player (DailyGlowUp) shows a solo-scale path, while the top complaints (locked results, scam feel) point straight at an honest, score-free, diet-driven wedge. Keep claims modest and protect younger users.
