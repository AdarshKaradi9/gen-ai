# Salary Negotiation Coach

## One-line pitch
Tell the app about your offer or raise meeting, get a market-rate check, an exact script and anchor number, then rehearse out loud against an AI recruiter that pushes back until you stop caving.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: tech and professional workers holding a new offer (or a review in the next two weeks), especially first-time negotiators and women who report lower negotiation rates. They gather on Blind, r/cscareerquestions, r/careerguidance and r/jobs (fresh sizes not found in this pass), and on Levels.fyi.

## Demand evidence
- Resume Genius survey of 1,000 US full-time workers (2025): 55% did not negotiate their first salary; 27% of non-negotiators regret it; only 22% of those who negotiated got no better offer (survey, via GOBankingRates/Nasdaq syndication).
- Glassdoor (2023): 54% did not negotiate their most recent salary. PayScale: 57% have never negotiated (date unclear).
- Supply-side signal: at least six AI negotiation-practice apps launched in 2026 alone (PayBoost, Haggle, Negotiate: AI Practice Coach, iGrow, NegotiateIt, Salary Sage), plus a Udemy course on negotiating with ChatGPT role-play.

## Money proof
- Levels.fyi negotiation service: a 2026 roundup lists $1,250 to $5,000 per package; a Blind user reported about $2,000 for a 30-minute call, up from about $600 (third-party roundup and anecdote). Revenue not found.
- interviewing.io reportedly runs a negotiation package collected only if it wins a $10K+ increase (Blind thread, anecdotal). Moonchaser is another named service.
- AI apps: Haggle has about 21 MAU and "<$10K" monthly revenue (AppGoblin estimate, effectively near zero); PayBoost has 0 ratings six months after launch, priced at ₹699/week or ₹4,999/year (AppGoblin, App Store listing). NegotiateIt from $19 (PeerPush).
- Candor and Pave revenue: not found. Pave is an employer-side comp tool, so not a consumer comparison.
Conclusion: people pay four figures for human coaching around high-value offers, but no AI consumer app shows traction yet.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Human services: expensive and rising; Blind threads call some "a scam" (anecdotal) and exclude new-grad and intern offers.
- AI apps: unknown, few or no ratings, chat-only practice, generic "AI-estimated" market rates that the apps themselves tell users to verify elsewhere (PayBoost listing).
- ChatGPT Voice does a decent free role-play, and roundups recommend it.

## The wedge
Voice-first rehearsal with a scorecard ("you conceded in 40 seconds; you said 'sorry' 3 times") plus a defensible market-rate check built from public pay-transparency postings (salary ranges now required in job ads in several US states). Sell it as a one-time "offer pack" timed to the moment of need rather than a subscription.

## The 2-second clip
A phone on a desk, the AI recruiter's voice says "That's the top of our band," and the screen cuts to a counter-offer card: "$118,000 to $131,500 (+$13,500)."

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: enter role, city, offer number; see "you're 11% below market" and a three-line script.
- Daily action: during the 3 to 10 days before the conversation, one 2-minute drill per day (Haggle already uses this format).
- Streak/reward: a "negotiation readiness" score rising toward 100 before the meeting date.
- Shareable artifact: "+$X negotiated" card, the single most shareable money moment in a career.
- Paywall: after the market check and before the full script and voice practice; $29 to $49 one-time per negotiation, or a short weekly plan.

## Risks
- Usage is episodic (a few times per career), so there is no habit and LTV is one purchase.
- Market-rate accuracy: wrong numbers can cost users offers; liability and trust risk.
- ChatGPT and Gemini are good free substitutes for scripts and role-play.
- Crowded with 2026 launches, none proven, which suggests weak demand for standalone apps.

## Scores
| Criterion | 1-5 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Two-second aha | 2 | "You're underpaid by X" works, but voice rehearsal needs time to show |
| Visual short-video hook | 3 | "How I got $20K more" storytime is strong; app demo is talking heads |
| Money already swims | 3 | Human coaching at $1,250 to $5,000 (third-party); AI apps show ~$0 |
| Daily reason to return | 1 | Episodic, a few times per career |
| Shareable moment | 3 | "+$13,500" card is great but rare and private for many |
| Unit economics | 3 | One-time $29 to $49 is plausible against a $10K+ gain; no repeat revenue |
| **Total** | **15/30** | |

## Verdict
Skip as a standalone app. Willingness to pay is real at the moment of an offer, but there is no daily loop, the 2026 field of AI clones shows no traction, and free chatbots cover most of it. Better as a paid feature inside a job-hunt or career app.
