# Jet Lag Plan: a personalised light, sleep and caffeine schedule for each flight

## One-line pitch
Enter your flight and usual sleep times and get a minute-by-minute plan for when to seek or avoid light, sleep, nap and drink coffee, delivered as timed notifications before, during and after the trip.

## Who exactly
Frequent long-haul travellers: business flyers, cabin crew, athletes and points enthusiasts. They gather around frequent-flyer communities (FlyerTalk, r/awardtravel, r/churning at about 664K-676K members per third-party trackers) and travel-hack creators. Timeshifter's investors include The Points Guy founder (press), which shows where the audience lives. The audience is small and affluent, not mass market.

## Demand evidence
- Timeshifter passed 1M user accounts in October 2024 (company press) and added nearly 500K jet lag members in 2025 (company, via Athletech News, April 2026).
- Airline integrations with United, Lufthansa, Swiss, Austrian and IHG (press) suggest airlines see jet lag as a premium perk rather than something customers pay for directly.
- Timeshifter reports 96.4% of 130K+ post-flight survey respondents avoided severe jet lag (self-reported, not a trial).
- Demand is tied to long-haul flights, typically a few per year per user.

## Money proof
- Timeshifter: first plan free, then $9.99 per plan or $24.99/year unlimited; discounted $4.99 plan (Athletech News; Adapty paywall library).
- Revenue estimates disagree: Adapty about $40K/month and 30K monthly downloads; Screensdesign "$45K"; AppGoblin iOS bracket "<$10K/month" with about 7.5K MAU (all third-party estimates). Total funding $5.3M (press).
- Timeshifter is pivoting toward shift work ($6.99/month or $69.99/year), a signal that consumer jet lag alone was not enough to scale.
- Arcashift: I found no revenue or review data this round.

## Competitor weaknesses
- The top complaint in a review summary is that a subscription creates friction for people who travel infrequently (marlvel.ai summary, third-party AI-generated).
- Notification reliability complaints on Google Play ("still not getting notifications", per android-stats review data). Plans fail if a nudge arrives late.
- A Fodor's writer reported the plan did not work for them; AppleInsider says it is "only as effective as you make it". The plan demands discipline on the worst travel days.
- Google Play average 4.33 from about 1.2K ratings versus a 4.7 iOS rating (third-party).

## The wedge
Make it free-feeling and visual: a single-screen timeline card ("glasses on now, coffee until 14:00, sleep 22:30") that lives in a Live Activity / lock screen widget, auto-imported from flight confirmation emails or Wallet boarding passes, priced per trip at $2.99 for the infrequent flyer Timeshifter's annual plan ignores.

## The 2-second clip
Lock screen on a plane: a Live Activity shows a sun icon crossed out, "Avoid light for 2h 10m", then flips to a coffee icon "Coffee OK until 14:00 Tokyo time".

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: forward a booking or scan a boarding pass, see the plan instantly.
- Daily action: follow 4-6 nudges a day for 3-5 days around a trip; nothing between trips.
- Streak/reward: "% of plan followed" and a post-trip "you adapted 2 days faster" score.
- Shareable artifact: a weak "beat jet lag in Tokyo" card; low share appeal.
- Paywall: after the plan reveal, $2.99 per trip or $19.99/year.

## Risks
- Medical and regulatory: melatonin dosing advice is regulated differently by country; health claims need care under App Store guideline 1.4.
- Retention: usage is episodic, so subscriptions churn after the trip.
- Platform: depends on reliable background notifications, the exact failure users already complain about.
- Incumbent: Timeshifter has science credibility (Dr Steven Lockley), airline deals and NASA/F1 endorsements that a solo founder cannot match.
- Commoditisation: general AI assistants can produce a decent jet lag schedule for free.

## Verdict
Skip. The category leader has 1M+ accounts, airline partnerships and science credibility, yet its own revenue estimates are modest and it is pivoting to shift work. The use is episodic, unshareable and easily replicated by a chatbot.
