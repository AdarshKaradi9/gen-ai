# Closet Outfits ("Wear What You Own")

## One-line pitch
Photograph your clothes once and every morning get three outfits built only from what is in your wardrobe, matched to the weather and your plans.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
Women aged 20-40 who own lots of clothes but "have nothing to wear", plus capsule-wardrobe and no-buy followers who want to shop less.
- r/capsulewardrobe and no-buy communities exist and discuss closet indexing; I did not find current member counts in my searches.
- Indyx data: the average catalogued closet holds 166 items (Indyx, self-reported), which defines the setup burden.

## Demand evidence (searches, communities, trend signals, with sources)
- Syndicated consumer press (Stacker, carried by several US TV station sites, 2026) ran "Can you really organize your closet with an app?", a sign of mainstream curiosity.
- Whering reportedly has 10M users and a $7M seed in July 2026 (aifunding.me, unverified; I found no press confirmation).
- A steady stream of new AI stylist apps on the App Store and TrustMRR in 2025-2026 (Dressed, Sty AI, Vestiary AI, StyleGenAI, schrnk, OOTD).

## Money proof (competitors with revenue/downloads, pricing, labelled by evidence type)
- Indyx: about 8.1K MAU, under $10K/month, 3K ratings at 4.76 (AppGoblin, third-party estimate). Monetises human styling services as well as the app.
- Acloset: free up to 100 items, paid tiers $3.99-24.99/month (company pricing via press).
- Stylebook: one-time $4.99 purchase (aggregator review page), which anchors prices low.
- TrustMRR wardrobe apps are tiny: OOTD about $22 MRR with 2 subscriptions; schrnk $7 lifetime; LiveStylist $0 (TrustMRR, RevenueCat-verified).
- Cladwell moved to subscription and users complain about it (reviews).
- Net: large user counts in the category, but I found no verified evidence of meaningful subscription revenue.

## Competitor weaknesses (what 1-2 star reviews and complaints say)
- Setup is the killer: a Stylist writer "lost track of how many hours" photographing 183 items in Whering; about 2 minutes per item means roughly 5.5 hours for an average closet (press and blog estimates).
- Stylebook: "adding items is tedious... you have to remove the background manually"; background remover "doesn't work hardly at all".
- Cladwell: switch to a "mandatory and expensive" subscription; "too many limitations".
- Whering's own blog admits uploading items one by one "can feel overwhelming".

## The wedge (the specific angle that beats incumbents)
Kill setup. Let the user film a 30-second video sweeping the open wardrobe rail, and have vision AI segment and tag each visible item, then fill gaps from receipts and the "outfit I am wearing today" mirror selfie. Deliver a usable outfit on day one with as few as 15 items, rather than demanding a full catalogue first. Pair this with the weather and a "not worn in 60 days" nudge.

## The 2-second clip
A phone pans across a stuffed wardrobe; tiles of individual garments pop out of the video one by one, then snap into a styled outfit card titled "Today, 14 degrees, office".

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: film the rail, see 20 items appear, get the first outfit within a minute.
- Daily action: morning push with three outfits; tap "wore it" (one tap logs a wear).
- Streak/reward: "no-repeat" or "shop my closet" streak, cost-per-wear dropping on a favourite item, monthly money-saved estimate.
- Shareable artifact: weekly outfit grid and "wore 41 of my 120 items this month" stat card.
- Paywall: after the first generated outfits, $4.99/month or $29.99/year. A hard paywall is risky because value only shows after days of use; a soft paywall with item cap is more realistic.

## Risks (regulatory, platform, saturation, retention)
- Low willingness to pay: Stylebook anchors at $4.99 once; most free tiers cover casual use.
- Video segmentation of crowded rails is technically hard; errors recreate the tedium.
- Retention depends on users logging wears, which historically decays.
- Crowded with funded players (Whering, Indyx) and many AI clones.

## Scores
| Criterion | 1-5 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Two-second aha | 3 | Outfit from your own clothes needs setup first; the rail-scan shortens it but is unproven |

| Money already swims | 2 | Indyx <$10K/mo estimate; TrustMRR wardrobe apps near $0 (verified) |

| **Total** | **20/30** | |

## Verdict
Skip unless the video rail-scan works convincingly in a prototype. The daily need is real and the setup pain is well documented, but the category has many users and almost no verified subscription money, so even a better product may fail to monetise.
