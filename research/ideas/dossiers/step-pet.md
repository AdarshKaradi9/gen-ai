# Step Pet (walking companion that grows with your steps)

## One-line pitch
A pet that only grows when you walk, so your daily step count becomes something you care for instead of a number you ignore.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
Women 18 to 35 who already like cozy self-care apps (Finch, Pikmin Bloom) and want to walk more, plus people with a step goal from a doctor or a weight-loss plan. They gather on TikTok cozy-gaming and "hot girl walk" content and in the Finch and Pikmin Bloom communities. Size signals: Finch has about 766,600 US App Store ratings and 10M+ Google Play installs (store listings checked Oct 2026, via Review Rounds); AppGoblin estimates about 2.6M monthly active iOS users (third-party estimate). Subreddit sizes could not be checked (Reddit blocked our fetcher).

## Demand evidence (searches, communities, trend signals, with sources)
- Pikmin Bloom, a walk-to-grow game, made $36.1M net in its fifth year (to Oct 2026), its best year, up 53% on year four; $97.1M lifetime; spending peaked at about $4.7M in May 2026 (AppMagic via PocketGamer.biz, third-party estimate). Walking-as-play demand is growing, not fading.
- A new wave of indie step-pet apps in 2025 and 2026 (Steppy, Trot, StepPet, StepPup, plus GrowMe, MochiStep, STEPcare, TameWatch from earlier findings) shows builders see the demand, but most have under 10 ratings.
- No Google Trends or TikTok hashtag numbers were found for "step pet"; treat search demand as unproven.

## Money proof (competitors with revenue/downloads, pricing, labelled by evidence type)
- **Finch (benchmark):** about $380K/month in June 2025 and about $1M/month in June 2026, about $12M lifetime (Sensor Tower via Review Rounds, third-party estimate). AppGoblin says "$100K+ monthly" (third-party estimate). One newsletter claimed $30M to $40M ARR (unconfirmed, likely overstated). Bootstrapped, grown mainly by paid social at high creative volume. Free core loop; Finch Plus about $39.99/yr or $9.99 (store listing).
- **Pikmin Bloom:** $36.1M year-five net (third-party estimate), but backed by Nintendo IP and Scopely.
- **Steps & Beasts (closest indie comparable):** MRR $2,891, 1,794 active subscriptions, ARPPU $1.61, LTV $16, 695 ratings, launched Oct 2024 (RevenueCat-verified on whatsthe.app, Oct 2026). Prices run from $0.79/week to $49.99 lifetime.
- **StepDog:** about 4.3 stars on 1,268 ratings; no revenue found. Stepets 262 ratings, no revenue found.
- Conclusion: no small step-pet player was found earning more than a few thousand dollars a month. The money is in Finch (self-care, not steps) and Pikmin Bloom (IP plus game spending).

## Competitor weaknesses (what 1-2 star reviews and complaints say)
- StepDog reviewers want more animation, say in-app competitions are hard to opt out of, and ask for a one-time fee instead of a subscription.
- Stepets reviewers want pets that interact with objects and each other (the pet feels static).
- Pikmin Bloom uses GPS and is described as battery-heavy (MistyWay comparison, which is a competitor's own blog).
- Most new step-pet apps look alike: a pedometer with a skin. Low pricing (ARPPU $1.61 at Steps & Beasts) shows users will not pay much for that.
- Finch 1-2 star themes were not collected in this pass.

## The wedge (the specific angle that beats incumbents)
A co-raised pet: two people (couple, long-distance friends, parent and adult child) share one pet that only thrives when both walk. This uses the playbook's "small winnable groups" and "a character worth missing" patterns, gives a built-in invite, and makes a missed day social rather than private guilt. Ship it widget-first (lock screen and home screen pet that visibly droops by evening) so the reminder is the pet itself. Pikmin Bloom and Finch are single-player at their core; the indie apps are solo pedometers.

## The 2-second clip
Split screen: two phones side by side, both lock screens showing the same sleepy pet. One person walks, the step count ticks past 8,000, and the pet on both screens jumps up and sprouts a new accessory. Caption: "our pet only grows if we BOTH walk."

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** hatch the egg using today's steps already in Apple Health (progress never starts at zero), then name it and send the invite link.
- **Daily action:** walk; the widget updates; evening "pet report" shows each person's contribution.
- **Streak/reward:** shared streak with two freezes; earn-back by walking extra the next day; unlockable outfits and habitats by distance milestones.
- **Shareable artifact:** weekly "our walk map" card with the pet and combined miles.
- **Paywall:** soft paywall after the pet hatches. Free tier keeps the shared pet alive; Plus at about $29.99/yr or $4.99/month unlocks accessories, more pets, history. Hard paywall is a poor fit: the genre expects a free core (Finch) and the indie price ceiling is low.

## Risks (regulatory, platform, saturation, retention)
- **Saturation:** at least ten indie step-pet apps; differentiation by art alone fails.
- **Unit economics:** verified indie LTV of $16 is far below typical US fitness CPI; Finch only works at scale with a free core and huge ad volume.
- **Retention:** step novelty fades after week two; shared pets can die when one partner quits, which may cause churn of both.
- **Platform:** depends on HealthKit and Health Connect step data and widget refresh limits.
- Low regulatory risk.

## Verdict
Maybe, leaning skip. The habit loop is excellent and Finch proves pets retain, but every small step-pet app we could measure earns pocket money, and the price ceiling is low. Build only if the co-raised pet wedge can be tested cheaply for invite rate first; otherwise it is a crowded field with poor unit economics.
