# ADHD Focus: shame-free tiny tasks with a body double

## One-line pitch
An ADHD-friendly focus app that turns one dreaded task into three tiny steps, starts a 10-minute session with an always-available body double (a live stranger room or an AI companion character), and rewards finishing with a pet that grows, never a red "you failed" streak.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: adults 20 to 40 with diagnosed or self-identified ADHD, mostly women diagnosed late, who already tried planners and quit. They gather on Reddit (r/ADHD, r/adhdwomen), ADHD TikTok and Instagram creators. I could not confirm subreddit sizes or TikTok hashtag views inside this search budget; verify before committing. Tiimo alone reported over 500,000 users in 2024 (press via EU-Startups, cited on Wikipedia), which sets a floor on reachable demand.

## Demand evidence
- Tiimo: 500K+ users and 50K paid users by August 2024 (press); Apple iPhone App of the Year, December 2025 (TechCrunch). Apple featuring an ADHD planner as its top iPhone app signals mainstream demand.
- Tiimo Android: about 3.6K installs a week, 24K over four weeks, "above average growth" (AppGoblin estimate, late Oct 2026).
- Focusmate: body doubling learned "on TikTok" is a recurring user story; a review notes the search term has "climbed sharply" (anecdotal, no figures found).
- Finch: 14M+ downloads, 675K ratings at 4.9 stars (App Store figures quoted in our playbook), much of it from neurodivergent users.

## Money proof
- Finch (self-care pet, adjacent): about $380K a month in June 2025 and about $1M a month by June 2026 (Sensor Tower via aggregator write-ups, third-party estimate); ARR Club lists $40M ARR (unverified); bootstrapped. Strongest evidence that a gentle, character-led habit app can earn eight figures.
- Tiimo: $10 a month premium with AI chatbot (press); 50K paid of 500K users, about 10% (press, 2024). Raised about €4.3M (press).
- Inflow (CBT-based ADHD program): $8.99 a month or $59.99 a year per its own blog; users report $95 to $199 annual charges (Trustpilot, self-reported by reviewers).
- Focusmate: high hundreds of thousands a year run rate (Fast Company estimate); a newsletter claims about $1M a year (unverified). Plus plan $8 a month annual, $12 monthly; free tier three sessions a week.
- Structured: about $19.99 a year, positioned as the budget option (comparison blog).
- Goblin Tools: no revenue or download data found.
- Tiimo Android alone shows under $10K a month (AppGoblin), so iOS carries most revenue; treat AppGoblin buckets as lower bounds.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Inflow: billing is the main complaint. Reviewers report being charged $199 after a forgotten trial, being locked into a year, and "too expensive, approximately 350 DKK per month" (Trustpilot, App Store). For ADHD users, who forget trials more than most, an aggressive trial is a reputational landmine.
- Tiimo: "with the existing bugs, I don't find it worth the subscription price" (review aggregator); described as pricier than Structured.
- Focusmate: desktop-first, scheduled 25 to 75 minute video sessions with strangers, which is a big ask on a low-energy day; free tier capped at three sessions.
- Planners in general (Tiimo, Structured) require planning a day, which is exactly the executive-function step many users cannot start.

## The wedge
Start, not plan. Competitors sell a plan for the day; the wedge sells the first 10 minutes. One button: "I'm stuck on..." then the app breaks it into three micro-steps (Goblin Tools style), starts a 10-minute timer, and a body double appears: a live room of others working silently on phones, with an AI companion fallback so there is never an empty room. Rewards are pet growth and "you showed up" counts that never reset. Plus honest billing: trial reminder push two days before charge, one-tap cancel, explicitly marketed as "ADHD-proof billing", directly attacking Inflow's top complaint.

## The 2-second clip
Split screen: "clean the kitchen" typed in, it explodes into three cards ("put 5 dishes in sink", "wipe one counter", "take out trash"), the timer starts, and a cozy character sits down beside the user's avatar with a "doing it with you" label.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: pick one task you have been avoiding; within 15 seconds it is broken into tiny steps and a session starts. Questions limited to energy level and preferred session length.
- Daily action: one "unstuck session" of 10 minutes; anything more is bonus.
- Streak and reward: weekly "showed up" count (target 4 of 7) instead of a daily streak, two freezes, the pet grows each session and goes on small surprise trips (Finch pattern).
- Shareable: end-of-session card "I did the thing: 3 tiny steps, 10 minutes" plus a weekly pet postcard.
- Paywall: after the first completed session, $39.99/yr preselected, $6.99/mo, 7-day trial with an in-app reminder before charge. Free tier: one session a day; paid: unlimited, live rooms, task history.

## Risks
Regulatory: avoid claims to treat or diagnose ADHD (FDA digital therapeutic line; EndeavorRx exists as a prescription product). Market as a focus tool. Saturation: Tiimo has Apple's App of the Year halo, Finch owns the pet mechanic, Goblin Tools owns task breakdown for cheap. Live rooms need liquidity and moderation; AI companion fallback needs inference cost control. Retention: ADHD users notoriously churn from productivity apps ("app hopping"), so novelty must be refreshed. Trust: harsh reviews follow any billing surprise.

## Verdict
Maybe. Willingness to pay is proven and incumbents have clear holes (aggressive billing, planning-first design, desktop body doubling), but the space is crowded with beloved brands and churn is structural. Build only if the founder can make the "start in 10 seconds with a body double" moment feel magical and owns an ADHD creator audience.
