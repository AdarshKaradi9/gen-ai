# Picky Eater Kids

## One-line pitch
A family game where kids climb a "food exposure ladder" (look, touch, smell, lick, taste) for one new food a day, earning stickers and growing a food-explorer passport, while parents get a no-pressure plan from feeding research.

## Who exactly
- **Beachhead:** parents of 2-6 year olds who describe their child as a picky eater, in the US/UK. Prevalence estimates range 13% to 50% depending on definition, peaking between ages 2 and 6 (2025 narrative review, PMC12736178); a longitudinal study found 13-22% picky at any given age, with ~40% lasting over 2 years (PubMed 20850060).
- **Secondary:** parents of neurodivergent kids and ARFID families, who pay for feeding therapy; this segment is higher-need and more medical.
- **Where they gather:** parenting Instagram (Kids Eat in Color, Feeding Littles, Solid Starts), Facebook groups, r/Parenting, r/toddlers. I did not retrieve follower or member counts in this pass.

## Demand evidence
- A steady stream of apps targets the problem: Teach Your Monster Eating, Food Explorer Club, Yummi, Playful Eats, Tiny Tastes, Choose My Food, ARFID app (App Store listings).
- Teach Your Monster Eating shows the strongest pull: 119K+ Android installs, ~3.9K MAU, 782 installs in the latest week (AppGoblin, third-party), and claims "over a million young food explorers" (self-reported). It was built with Dr Lucy Cooke and uses the SAPERE sensory method.
- Kids Eat in Color, a large parenting brand, launched a paid app (BetterBites) for picky eating and ARFID, positioned as home-based support "alongside or instead of feeding therapy." It sits outside the top 30 in US Medical (third-party tracker).

## Money proof
- **Teach Your Monster Eating:** under $10K/mo (AppGoblin); free with an occasional $4.99 unlock; backed by a charity-linked studio, so not a revenue benchmark.
- **Playful Eats:** subscription $11.99/mo or $59.99/yr, built by feeding specialists; only 9 iOS ratings and ~17 MAU on iOS by one estimate, under $10K/mo (AppGoblin).
- **Yummi:** subscription model (reward goals, multi-child profiles), no revenue data found.
- **Kids Eat in Color:** sells ebooks, bundles, meal plans, a WIC partnership and a Home Chef deal; no revenue or member counts found.
- I could not find any picky-eating app with verified or estimated revenue above $10K/month. Parents clearly pay for courses, ebooks and feeding therapy, but I did not find figures for those either.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Teach Your Monster Eating rates 3.58 on Android (AppGoblin); review themes were not gathered in detail.
- Food Explorer Club got a 3/5 from a dietitian review (Food & Nutrition magazine).
- Pattern across apps: either a kid-only game with no link to the real dinner table (Teach Your Monster), or a parent-only course/tracker with nothing for the child to enjoy (Playful Eats, Yummi). Few connect the real plate to the game.

## The wedge
Bridge the real plate and the game: the parent photographs tonight's new food, it becomes a character in the child's sticker book, and the child moves one rung up the exposure ladder (touch, smell, lick, taste) rather than being rewarded for eating. That respects feeding guidance on pressure (exposure, not bribes) while keeping a game loop. Yummi's "reward = Robux" approach is the opposite and is a credibility opening.

## The 2-second clip
Split screen: a toddler gingerly licks a broccoli floret at the table; on the phone, a cartoon broccoli sticker slams into the "Food Explorer Passport," confetti fires, and a ladder bar jumps from "Smelled it" to "Licked it!" Parent voice: "Day 12. She LICKED it."

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** pick child age and 3 "safe foods" and 3 "target foods"; instantly see a personalized 2-week ladder with a cute food character for each.
- **Daily action:** at dinner, parent taps the rung reached (30 seconds), child slaps a sticker.
- **Streak/reward:** family streak with freezes (dinners get missed); character evolves; monthly "foods tried" certificate.
- **Shareable artifact:** "Food Explorer Passport" page and "My kid tried 20 new foods" card; these suit parent Instagram.
- **Paywall:** after the ladder reveal, $39.99/yr or $6.99/mo, family plan for siblings, 7-day trial.

## Risks
- **Weak proof of payment:** no app in the niche shows meaningful revenue; parents may treat it as a one-time phase purchase, so churn after 2-3 months is likely.
- **Feeding science:** many feeding specialists caution against rewards and pressure; a sticker loop must be framed as exposure (Teach Your Monster and Playful Eats already follow sensory-ladder methods).
- **Medical:** ARFID, autism-related feeding issues and growth concerns need referral language; avoid diagnosis claims.
- **App Store/privacy:** Kids Category rules and COPPA restrict analytics and ads and require parental gates; keeping it a parent-facing app with a kid mode avoids some constraints.
- **Retention:** the problem fades as kids age; LTV ceiling is short.

## Verdict
Maybe, leaning skip as a standalone subscription. The content hook and shareability are strong and the incumbents are weak, but there is no evidence that any picky-eating app makes real money, and the need is a phase that ends. Best tested cheaply as a parent-facing content-led app, or partnered with a feeding-expert creator who already has the audience.
