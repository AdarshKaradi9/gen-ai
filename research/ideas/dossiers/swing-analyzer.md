# Swing Analyzer ("Pickleball Pro Eye")

## One-line pitch
Prop your phone on the fence, play, and get an auto-cut highlight reel plus a skeleton overlay of your strokes compared with a pro, with one fix to work on next time.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
Recreational pickleball players aged 35-70 with disposable income who play several times a week, then golfers as the second market.
- US pickleball: about 24.3M players in 2025, up 22.8% on 2024, about 4.5M new players, and 7.48M core players (8+ times a year), up 20.4% (SFIA 2026 report, industry association data via press).
- DUPR (pickleball rating system) publishes growth by city and country, showing a rated, competitive segment.
- Golf: I did not search for current golfer counts.

## Demand evidence (searches, communities, trend signals, with sources)
- Pickleball has been the fastest-growing US sport for multiple consecutive years (SFIA via pickleball.com).
- SwingVision expanded from tennis to pickleball and padel and raised money specifically to reach them (Profluence, press).
- Sportsbox AI was acquired by an investor group led by golfer Bryson DeChambeau in 2026, described as an eight-figure deal, and launched a Gemini-based AI coach (GeekWire, press).

## Money proof (competitors with revenue/downloads, pricing, labelled by evidence type)
- SwingVision: about $100K/month revenue and 20K monthly downloads, 3.2K ratings at 4.7; pricing Semi-Pro $89.99/yr, Pro $179.99/yr, Max $359.99/yr, Pro monthly $29.99 (Adapty paywall library, third-party estimate). Other estimates range from about $1M to $4.8M a year (low-quality sites, unverified).
- Sportsbox 3D Golf: 100K+ golfers and thousands of paying coaches; $15.99/month or $110/year (GeekWire, press, 2024). Revenue estimates conflict: under $10K/month (AppGoblin) vs $40K/month (Adapty), both third-party.
- V1 Sports: moving from freemium to subscription-only raised revenue about 90% in 12 months (RevenueCat case study, company-reported).
- Net: this audience pays $90-360 a year, far above typical consumer app prices.

## Competitor weaknesses (what 1-2 star reviews and complaints say)
- SwingVision: "technical instability and recording failures during matches erode trust in the subscription" (Marlvel aggregator); "after a recent update the app was very unstable... couldn't export" (review); scoring accuracy complaints (App Store review via aggregator).
- SwingVision is iPhone-first and tennis-first; pickleball was bolted on in 2026.
- Sportsbox is golf-only and coach-oriented; price is high for casual players.

## The wedge (the specific angle that beats incumbents)
Pickleball-first and casual-first: no calibration, works from any fence angle, and the output is a shareable highlight reel (best rallies, fastest drive, a "dink duel" stat) rather than a dense analytics dashboard. Offer one clear coaching tip per session with a side-by-side pro overlay, and price below SwingVision Pro. Reliability of recording, the top complaint, becomes the core promise (local recording first, analysis after).

## The 2-second clip
Split screen: the player's backhand with a glowing pose skeleton next to a pro's backhand, the elbow angle highlighted red then green, with the caption "My paddle was 20 degrees too open".

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: upload or record one rally and get an instant skeleton overlay and highlight clip.
- Weekly action: record each session (2-4 a week for core players); get a "session report".
- Streak/reward: weekly play streak, stroke-score improvements, DUPR-style progress line.
- Shareable artifact: 15-second highlight reel with stats overlay, ready for Instagram and club WhatsApp groups.
- Paywall: after the first full session analysis; $9.99/month or $69.99/year, undercutting SwingVision Pro ($179.99) with a 7-day trial.

## Risks (regulatory, platform, saturation, retention)
- Technical: reliable pose and ball tracking from a fence-mounted phone is hard; compute costs per session are high.
- Competition from funded players (SwingVision, Sportsbox with DeChambeau backing) and court camera systems.
- Pickleball growth could slow (SFIA mid-year data showed slower growth before the full-year rebound).
- Usage tied to sessions, not daily, so habit loops are weekly.

## Verdict
Maybe. The money and audience growth are the strongest of my four (24.3M US pickleball players, apps charging $90-360 a year), but it is a hard computer-vision build against funded incumbents. Build only if a prototype can record reliably from a fence angle; otherwise skip.
