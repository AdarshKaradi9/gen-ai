# Screen Time Companion

## One-line pitch
A screen-time blocker for one specific audience (college students in exam season, or parents who want to model less phone use for their kids) where a character's health and a "years of your life back" counter make the screen-time cost easy to feel, plus a small accountability group so the blocker is harder to quietly switch off.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: US college students aged 18 to 24 during finals and study seasons. They already post "lock in" and "study with me" content and complain about doomscrolling. A second option is parents of children under 12. I did not find community sizes for either group in this pass (not searched), so treat the audience size as unverified. The demand for the category as a whole is well established (see below).

## Demand evidence
- Opal says its users have saved over 500 million hours as of June 2026 (self-reported usage metric, Opal blog).
- one sec has 104K iOS ratings at 4.81 stars and an estimated 257K MAU (AppGoblin, third-party estimate).
- Brick (a $59 physical NFC blocker plus app) reports over 500,000 active users in 180 countries (Entrepreneur, May 2026; company-sourced). Business Insider framed "bricking your phone" as "the new Dry January" in January 2026.
- Brainrot (a decaying cartoon-brain pet) has 23K App Store ratings (App Store listing).

## Money proof
- Opal: about $400K/month and 200K monthly downloads (Adapty paywall library, third-party estimate). Lazyweb lists 2024 revenue at $4.8M, 500K active users and about $6.9M raised (third-party database). Pricing runs weekly $4.99 to $9.99, monthly $8.99 to $19.99, yearly $59.99 to $99.99.
- Brainrot: reportedly $400K/month within about a year of a mid-2025 launch, starting from $10K on launch day off one short video and a 200K founder audience on TikTok and Instagram (Stork.ai blog, self-reported via founder, unverified). Mostly annual subscriptions.
- Brick: "multiple eight figures in revenue" (Entrepreneur, single company-sourced claim; Brick declined to share sales figures with SSENSE).
- one sec: estimates conflict, from under $10K/month (AppGoblin) to $80K to $150K/month (screensdesign). Third-party estimates, low confidence. Priced at about $19.99/yr.
- Jomo $34.99/yr and Freedom $39.99/yr (Unstar review roundup). ScreenZen is free and supported by tips.
Conclusion: money clearly flows here, and the character-pet framing (Brainrot) is the one already proven to reach six figures a month.

## Competitor weaknesses
Unstar analysed 1 to 3 star reviews of Opal, Forest, Freedom, one sec and Jomo. It is a vendor blog, so its percentages and quotes are indicative only.
- Subscription surprise at the trial-to-charge step (24%): "Opal charged me $99.99 the day my trial ended." Opal's community forum has a similar complaint about losing over $100 to an annual renewal.
- Too easy to bypass (19%): users remove the app from Screen Time, change the date, or edit the schedule.
- Screen Time API fragility (16%): permissions reset after iOS updates and blocking stops silently.
- Guilt mechanics backfire (12%): a dead Forest tree or a broken streak causes anxiety.
- Works for a week, then stops (10%): users tap through one sec's pause on autopilot. A May 2026 detox.so comparison says the same about Brainrot.

## The wedge
1. **Accountability instead of willpower.** Bypassing the blocker or changing its schedule sends a gentle note to one or two chosen friends ("Sam unlocked TikTok at 1:14am"). That makes the 19% bypass problem social rather than technical, and it gives users a reason to invite friends.
2. **A character that heals as well as rots.** Brainrot only punishes. A companion that recovers, and a lost streak that can be won back, address the guilt complaint and follow the playbook's "streaks that bend" rule.
3. **Honest billing.** A reminder before the trial charge and an annual price under $40 address the biggest complaint category directly.
4. **One audience's language.** A finals-week "lock in" mode with a study-hours counter that can be shared.

## The 2-second clip
A split screen: on the left, a phone opens TikTok and a cartoon character visibly wilts while a counter reads "-4.2 years of your life on your phone at this rate". On the right, after a week of use, the same character is glowing and the card reads "You got back 11h 40m this week".

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** the user enters their age and daily screen time (or grants Screen Time access), and the app shows "At this pace: 17 years of your remaining life on your phone." They pick a goal and a character, and a first block starts immediately.
- **Daily action:** a morning check-in, and character health updates throughout the day. The "unlock" flow includes a 5-second intention prompt.
- **Streak/reward:** days under goal, with two streak freezes and a way to earn a lost day back. The character grows outfits and stages.
- **Shareable artifact:** a weekly "hours reclaimed" card with the character, and an end-of-finals "I locked in for 42 hours" card.
- **Paywall:** a hard paywall after the plan reveal: $34.99/yr preselected, plus $6.99/mo, with a 3-day trial and a stated charge date.

## Risks
- **Saturation:** this is the most crowded category of the three, with Opal, one sec, Brick, ScreenZen, Jomo, Forest, Brainrot and many clones. Ranking and paid acquisition will be competitive.
- **Platform:** every iOS blocker depends on Apple's Family Controls / Screen Time API. It is fragile, Apple can change it, and Apple's own Screen Time is free.
- **Retention:** the "worked for a week" pattern is common across the category. Annual-first pricing hides the problem for revenue but not for reviews.
- **Regulatory:** low. If the app targets minors or parents, child privacy rules apply.

## Verdict
**Maybe, leaning build.** The demand and money are the strongest of the three, and the character format is already proven. The category is crowded, so a new entrant only wins with a sharp audience and the accountability-group wedge, plus a founder able to produce daily short-form content. Copying Brainrot without a new angle would be a late, me-too entry.
