# Dating Profile Coach

## One-line pitch
Upload your dating photos and bio, get a score per photo, the best order to show them, and a rewritten bio, so you get more matches this week.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
Men 20-35 on Hinge, Tinder and Bumble who get few matches. They gather on dating-advice subreddits (r/Tinder, r/hingeapp, r/OnlineDating) and in looksmaxxing and "rizz" TikTok. I did not pull member counts for these subreddits in this pass, so community sizes are unverified here.

## Demand evidence (searches, communities, trend signals, with sources)
- Rizz (AI dating assistant): co-founder told AP it passed 7.5 million downloads by September 2024 (press, self-reported); Rizz showed 4.8 stars from 23,000+ App Store reviews and 4.4 from 83,000+ Play reviews (Vidaselect, third-party, may be dated).
- AppGoblin lists a long tail of copycats (RizzGPT x3, Rizz Up, RizzWiz, Plug AI, Rizz God), many with only hundreds of installs.
- Reuters (2025) covered AI "digital wingmen" as a trend, citing YourMove pricing at $12.99/month.
- Adjacent: looksmaxxing face-rating apps (Umax and many clones) are a large lane.

## Money proof (competitors with revenue/downloads, pricing, labelled by evidence type)
- Rizz: about $300K/month revenue and 200K monthly downloads (Adapty paywall library, third-party estimate); Screensdesign lists about $150K (third-party). Pricing: weekly $3.99-$9.99, monthly $19.99, annual $69.99 (Adapty).
- YourMove AI: peaked around $30K/month, about 80% margin, founder quoted at $28K/month when he stopped working on it (Starter Story compiling founder interviews, self-reported). Growth came from SEO.
- RizzGPT (one of several by that name): 15 active subscriptions, RevenueCat-verified (TrustMRR). A Flippa listing for RizzGPT says 67,000 downloads and about $500/month (seller-reported). A search snippet claiming $70K MRR for this listing did not match the page, so I discard it.
- Looksmaxxing app on Flippa: $664K trailing 12-month gross, asking $915K (seller-reported, unaudited).
- Photofeeler: credit-based human voting, roughly $9-$20 entry pricing per third-party sites (conflicting).
Money clearly swims here, but it concentrates in a few winners while copies earn little.

## Competitor weaknesses (what 1-2 star reviews and complaints say)
- Price: Rizz reviews repeatedly complain about about EUR 7.99/week and little free functionality (Kimola review analysis, third-party).
- Hard walls: Rizz God reviewed as blocking meaningful use without a weekly subscription; Umax reviews complain about paying weekly just to see a result (Marlvel, App Store reviews).
- Quality: reports of "corny, low-sophistication content" after updates (AI-generated audit, weak source).
- Photofeeler is slow (you vote to earn karma or pay credits) and gives scores without fixes.

## The wedge (the specific angle that beats incumbents)
Focus on the profile, not the chat: instant per-photo scores with a concrete fix ("crop, you are too small in frame; swap photo 3 to first") and a Hinge-prompt rewriter, then a 7-day "match sprint" that asks users to report matches so the app can prove improvement. Most rivals sell reply generators; profile outcomes are measurable and more defensible.

## The 2-second clip
Split screen: six dating photos tile in, each gets a red, yellow or green score, the grid reshuffles itself into a new order and the overall profile score jumps from 41 to 78.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: pick app (Hinge/Tinder/Bumble), upload 6 photos, instant scored grid.
- Daily action: during a 7-day sprint, log matches and get one daily tweak (new prompt answer, photo swap).
- Streak/reward: profile score trend and match count chart.
- Shareable artifact: "My profile went from 41 to 78" card; roast-style mode for friends' profiles.
- Paywall: after the scored grid, before the fixes; weekly about $6.99 or yearly about $49.99.

## Risks (regulatory, platform, saturation, retention)
- App Store: guidelines have flagged "objectification of real people (e.g., polls about their attractiveness)" (third-party copy of guidelines, verify current text). Scoring only your own photos with improvement advice is safer than rating faces; looksmaxxing apps do ship, so it is not fatal.
- Retention is episodic: users churn once they have matches or a partner.
- Saturation and price complaints across the category; weekly plans attract refund requests.
- Body-image and wellbeing concerns, especially with teen users (17+ rating needed).
- Dating apps themselves (Tinder, Hinge) are adding AI photo pickers, which can commoditize the core.

## Verdict
Skip for a habit-app founder; maybe only as a fast cash-flow utility. Money is proven, but it is a crowded, episodic, paywall-complaint category with App Store sensitivity, and it fails the daily-return test that our playbook says decides retention.
