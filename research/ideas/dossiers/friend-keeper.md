# Friend Keeper (personal CRM for friendships)

## One-line pitch
A warm, non-corporate "keep in touch" app: pick the 15 to 30 people who matter, set how often you want to reach them, and get one small nudge a day ("Text Maya, it's been 6 weeks, she started a new job") plus birthday and life-event memory.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: adults 25 to 40 who moved cities or graduated and feel friendships slipping, especially young men (Gallup's pooled 2023-24 data: 25% of US males 15 to 34 felt lonely "a lot" the previous day vs 18% nationally, press summary). Broader pool: Gallup's October 2024 survey put about 1 in 5 US adults (about 52M) as lonely daily (press). Where they gather: loneliness and "how to make friends as an adult" content on TikTok, r/socialskills, r/MakeFriends, productivity creators. I did not find reliable subreddit sizes or hashtag counts in this round.

## Demand evidence
- Loneliness is a mainstream topic (Gallup 2024 and 2025 releases widely covered; Fortune "Bowling Alone 25 years later", Dec 2025).
- A visible but small personal CRM category: Dex, Mesh (formerly Clay, bought by Automattic in 2025 per comparison articles), Monica (open source), Garden, CatchUp, Stay In Touch, Smart Contact Reminder, TouchBase, Nat. Many comparison listicles exist, mostly written by Dex itself.
- Weak consumer pull signal: Garden, the closest "friends not networking" app, has only 122 ratings, about 432 MAU, and has not been updated since June 2020 (AppGoblin, third-party estimate).
- Not found: search volume, a viral TikTok format, or a breakout consumer app in this niche.

## Money proof
- Dex: about $12/month billed annually per its own guide; another table lists $24/member/month (vendor pages, self-reported pricing). No revenue figure found.
- Mesh/Clay: free tier up to 1,000 contacts, paid about $10/month (comparison article). Acquisition by Automattic suggests some value, terms not found.
- Stay In Touch (Android) gates beyond 2 reminders behind premium (store listing).
- No TrustMRR or AppGoblin revenue evidence for any consumer keep-in-touch app. The money that does exist sits with professional networking (salespeople, founders, investors), not friendships.

## Competitor weaknesses
Dex, Mesh and Monica are built for networking: LinkedIn sync, email enrichment, "contacts" language. Comparison pieces call some of them robotic, and a forum user complained about paying $50 for a personal CRM (BusyContacts). Garden and CatchUp are abandoned or basic: reminders without context, no conversation starters, no shared moments. The pattern is that the tools are either a spreadsheet for professionals or a dumb timer.

## The wedge
"Friendship, not networking": one daily card with one person and one suggested message (AI drafts a natural opener from your notes: "How did the half-marathon go?"), a "friendship garden" visual where each friend's plant wilts if neglected, and voice-note capture after a call ("she's moving to Denver in March") that becomes a dated reminder. Contact selection via the iOS picker only, no full contact sync.

## The 2-second clip
Home screen widget shows a wilting plant labelled "Jake, 47 days". User taps "Text him", a pre-written message pops into iMessage, sends, and the plant blooms. Caption: "this app guilt-trips me into being a good friend".

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: pick 5 people, the app instantly shows "you haven't talked to 3 of them in over a month" with the wilting garden. First nudge and drafted message on screen 1.
- Daily action: one nudge, one tap to message, one-line note afterwards.
- Streak/reward: "reached out" weekly streak, garden grows; birthdays never missed.
- Shareable artifact: yearly "friendship wrapped" (people you kept in touch with, birthdays hit). Weak, since sharing a CRM about friends feels awkward.
- Paywall: soft paywall after onboarding, free for 5 friends, $29.99/year for unlimited, AI drafts and events. Hard paywall unlikely to convert given weak category spend.

## Risks
- Retention: nudges become ignorable, and the act happens in iMessage, not the app, so value is invisible.
- Platform: Apple could ship this inside Contacts or Journal suggestions; iOS partial contact access reduces data.
- Weak monetisation: no proof consumers pay for friend reminders; incumbents monetise professionals.
- Creepiness: "I track my friends in an app" is a social stigma, which suppresses sharing.

## Scores
| Criterion | 1-5 | Why |

| Shareable moment | 2 | People rarely share that they track friends |

| **Total** | **16/30** | |

## Verdict
Skip as a standalone app. The loneliness problem is real and widely covered, but every money signal points to professional networking, and the one consumer-focused app (Garden) stalled at a few hundred users. Could work as a feature inside a couples or habit app, or pivoted to a professional "network nudges" niche where Dex already proves willingness to pay.
