# Screenshot Brain

## One-line pitch
An AI inbox for your screenshots and voice notes that turns each one into a reminder, task, calendar event or searchable note, so your camera roll stops being a junk drawer.

## Who exactly
- **Beachhead:** heavy screenshotters aged 18-35 with ADHD-style capture habits: people who screenshot event posters, restaurant recs, outfits, tickets and DMs "for later".
- **Where they gather:** productivity TikTok, r/ADHD, r/productivity, r/iphone (subscriber counts not retrieved). The "my camera roll is a to-do list" framing already appears in creator newsletters (TechTiff Substack, January 2026).

## Demand evidence
- Supply is booming: a single App Store search surfaces Captr, Shotted, ScreenshotAI: Calendar Events, Screenshot Swipe, kip!, ShotMemory, SnapMind, CaptainShot, ScreenVault, RecallBox, VizKeeper and Clutch, most launched 2025-26 (App Store listings, dev.to post). This shows builders see the pain, but I found no evidence that any of them has broken out.
- A paid newsletter guide to an iPhone Shortcut that routes screenshots to Reminders via ChatGPT suggests people want it enough to hack it themselves.
- Press commentary argues Apple should extend Visual Intelligence to create reminders from screenshots (pulse.bot), a sign the platform owner is the natural owner of this feature.

## Money proof
- **mymind:** about 3.1K iOS MAU and under $10K/month (AppGoblin, third-party estimate), 1.1K ratings, launched 2020.
- **Fabric:** under $10K/month, about 958 iOS MAU (AppGoblin).
- **Recall (consumer save-anything):** Pro at $7.99/month or $49.99/year (App Store); no revenue found. Note: the widely quoted "$31M ARR" belongs to Recall.ai, an unrelated meeting-bot API (Sacra), and must not be used as proof.
- **SnapZyn** (screenshot to AI note) is listed on TrustMRR with no active subscriptions. I found no verified MRR for any screenshot organizer.
- Granola-style capture tools earn money in meetings and work contexts, not in consumer screenshot sorting; I did not find consumer revenue evidence to bridge the two.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Most small apps cap the free tier hard (Clutch: five screenshots a week) or sell lifetime deals, which hints at weak subscription demand.
- Organizers focus on search and tags rather than action: the screenshot still has to be opened again to act on it.
- Privacy is a recurring selling point (ScreenVault and CaptainShot push on-device processing), so cloud AI on private DMs and tickets is a trust barrier.
- I did not retrieve 1-2 star review themes for these apps in this pass.

## The wedge
Action, not storage: every screenshot or voice note becomes exactly one proposed action (reminder with date, calendar event, shopping item, "watch later"), confirmed with a swipe, plus an automatic daily cleanup that deletes the source screenshot once the action exists. Show the "camera roll shrinking" number as the reward. On-device OCR first, cloud only when needed.

## The 2-second clip
A camera roll scrolls through 3,000 screenshots; cut to the app where a concert poster screenshot slides into a card "Phoebe Bridgers, Fri 14 Nov 8pm, add to calendar?" and a swipe right adds it, then a counter ticks "Camera roll: 2,999".

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** grant photo access, app processes the last 50 screenshots and shows "You have 7 events, 4 things to buy and 12 places to try hiding in here."
- **Daily action:** an evening triage of today's captures in under a minute, Tinder-style.
- **Streak/reward:** inbox-zero streak and a running "screenshots cleared" counter.
- **Shareable artifact:** "I cleared 2,400 screenshots" before/after card.
- **Paywall:** after the first triage, $4.99/month or $29.99/year; free tier of 20 actions a month.

## Risks
- **Platform:** Apple's Visual Intelligence and Google's Pixel Screenshots app (launched with Pixel 9; not re-checked in this pass) already do parts of this at the OS level, and iOS can add reminder creation in any release.
- **Permissions:** full photo library access is a big ask; Apple's limited-access picker weakens the magic.
- **Saturation:** a dozen near-identical apps launched in 2025-26 with no breakout winner, which suggests weak pull rather than an open lane.
- **Retention:** triage is a chore; users who "just screenshot" may not change habits.

## Verdict
Skip as a standalone app. The demo is great but no one in the category shows real revenue, a dozen clones are already out, and Apple and Google are building it into the OS. The "screenshot to action" mechanic could be a feature inside a stronger product (for example the recipe saver or an ADHD planner).
