# Heritage Language

## One-line pitch
Learn the language your family actually speaks: real phrases recorded by your own parents and grandparents become daily lessons, and a weekly call with Nani or Lola is the practice test.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
- **Beachhead:** US and Canadian second-generation Indian and Filipino families, starting with parents of kids aged 5 to 12 who feel guilty their child cannot talk to grandparents. The 2019 census count put Indian Americans at about 4.6M and Filipino Americans at about 4.2M (via Manila Mail). Pew finds second-generation Asian Americans number about 7.9M, 66% under age 30 (Pew, 2022 CPS).
- **Language loss is the norm:** studies of second-generation Filipino Americans describe "zero or limited fluency" in Filipino languages; Hindi is the second most common non-English language among Asian Americans and Tagalog third (census-based reporting).
- **Where they gather:** desi and Filipino-American TikTok, r/ABCDesis, r/FilipinoAmericans, gurdwara and temple Sunday schools. Subreddit sizes not retrieved.

## Demand evidence
- A Change.org petition asks Duolingo to add Punjabi, noting few online options. Duolingo's Indian-language work teaches English from Hindi, Punjabi, Tamil, Bengali and Telugu, not those languages to English speakers (Indian campaign press). Ling's blog states "no Punjabi on Duolingo" (competitor source).
- New entrants in 2026 target exactly this: BOLI (UK, pre-seed, "AI Nana", Urdu/Punjabi/Hindi/Bengali/Tamil), AncestraLingua (Tamil AI elder, near-zero Product Hunt traction), DesiBolo (Hindi/Punjabi for kids), Ellinopoula (Greek diaspora kids), Zabbot (Yoruba, NSF-backed). A 2026 CHI paper documents immigrant-language preservation needs.

## Money proof
- **Bluebird Hindi:** 38.4K installs, 858 MAU, under $10K/month (AppGoblin estimate).
- **"Learn Punjabi" (gswebcrunch):** 520K installs, under $10K/month (AppGoblin).
- **DesiBolo, BOLI, AncestraLingua:** no revenue found. BOLI's "£10B+ market" and "15M+ children at risk" are self-reported pitch claims.
- **Adjacent spend:** families pay for weekend heritage-language schools and online tutors (italki-style), but I did not retrieve figures in this pass.
- Conclusion: I found no heritage-language app with evidence of revenue above $10K/month.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Duolingo: missing or thin courses for Punjabi, Tamil, Gujarati and similar; where they exist, they teach textbook register, not "how my family talks."
- Generic apps (Bluebird, Ling, Learn Punjabi) are aimed at travellers, with no family or grandparent integration (BOLI's own comparison table makes the same point).
- I did not gather 1-2 star review themes for these apps in this pass.

## The wedge
Family-sourced content. Grandparents record 10 phrases a week by voice note (they already use WhatsApp), the app turns them into flashcards with transcription and translation, and the learner's practice is a scheduled 10-minute call. This covers any language or dialect without building a curriculum, and the emotional outcome (talking to Nani) is the product.

## The 2-second clip
A grandmother on a video call in Punjab bursts into tears and laughter as her 7-year-old grandson in Toronto says "Nani ji, main tuhanu pyaar karda haan."

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** pick language and family members; hear Nani's first recorded phrase turned into a lesson card.
- **Daily action:** 5 minutes on that week's family phrases with speech practice.
- **Streak/reward:** a weekly "call goal" plus daily streak; a family tree that lights up as relatives contribute.
- **Shareable artifact:** the call reaction clip and a "first sentence to Nani" card, a natural diaspora TikTok format.
- **Paywall:** after the first family phrase pack; $9.99/month or $59.99/year per family, gift-able by the grandparent generation or aunties.

## Risks
- **Market fragmentation:** each language is a small market; dialect variety (Punjabi Shahmukhi/Gurmukhi, Tagalog vs Bisaya) multiplies work. Speech recognition for low-resource languages is weaker.
- **Retention:** guilt-driven motivation fades; depends on a grandparent staying engaged.
- **Money:** no evidence of paying apps in this niche; parents may prefer free YouTube or weekend school.
- **Competition:** Duolingo could add courses; BOLI is pursuing the same "AI Nana" positioning.

## Scores
| Criterion | 1-5 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Two-second aha | 3 | The emotional payoff (talking to grandma) takes weeks, not seconds |
| Visual short-video hook | 4 | Grandparent reaction videos are a proven tearjerker format |
| Money already swims | 2 | Heritage apps estimate under $10K/month (AppGoblin); no verified revenue |
| Daily reason to return | 3 | Daily phrases work, but motivation is weekly and guilt-based |
| Shareable moment | 4 | Family call clips are highly shareable within diaspora communities |
| Unit economics | 2 | Small, fragmented language markets; unproven willingness to pay |
| **Total** | **18/30** | |

## Verdict
Skip as a venture, maybe as a niche. The emotional story and diaspora sizes are real and the content hook is strong, but there is no money proof in the category and each language is a small market. If pursued, start with one language (Punjabi or Tagalog) and test paid conversion with a family-pack price before scaling.
