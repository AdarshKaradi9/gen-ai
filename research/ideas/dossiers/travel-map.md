# Travel Map: every place you've been, built for you

## One-line pitch
Grant photo-library access once and the app scans your photo locations to paint every country, state and city you have ever visited onto a shareable world map, then hands you a "Year in Travel" recap each December.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: 20-35 year old travellers who already own years of geotagged phone photos but never logged their trips. They gather on TikTok and Instagram travel content and r/travel (several million members per search snippets, not verified this round). Polarsteps alone says it has 23M+ travellers (App Store description via AppGoblin, self-reported) and 20M tracked trips (company press, Oct 2025), so the habit of mapping trips is mainstream in Europe and growing fastest in Germany, France and the US (self-reported).

## Demand evidence
- Polarsteps reports 15M+ users by 2025 and 23M+ by late 2026 (self-reported), with the travel book as the growth engine.
- Polarsteps launched "Unpacked", a Spotify-Wrapped-style year recap, on 15 December 2025, plus a Year in Travel report built on 5M+ country visits (company press). The incumbent is already betting on the recap format.
- Dozens of "Been"-style apps exist. Countries Been (Android) has about 1.7M installs and 26K ratings at 4.74 stars (AppGoblin, third-party estimate). Places Been has about 872K installs; Visited has about 409K (AppGoblin).
- I found no source showing a travel-map format going viral on TikTok specifically, so the hook is plausible but unproven.

## Money proof
- Polarsteps: AppGoblin bracket "$50K+ monthly" IAP on iOS, 716K MAU (third-party estimate). Latka estimates 2025 revenue at $13.9M; Owler says $100K-5M; a FAZ report cited elsewhere says over EUR 10M and profitable (all third-party, conflicting). Most money comes from printed travel books, not subscriptions. Polarsteps Plus launched 2025-26 at EUR 8.99/month, App Store IAPs of $9.99 and $34.99, no free trial (company support pages, App Store tracker).
- Funding: EUR 500K and EUR 900K seed rounds and a reported EUR 3M round (company press; aggregators disagree).
- Country-map apps: Countries Been, Places Been and Visited each sit in AppGoblin's "<$10K/month" bracket despite hundreds of thousands to millions of installs, mostly ad-funded (third-party estimate). AppGoblin snapshots disagree (one search snippet showed ">$50K" for Countries Been, the live page shows "<$10K").

## Competitor weaknesses
- Been/Visited-style apps are manual: you tap each country. Nobody wants to back-fill fifteen years by hand.
- Polarsteps only knows trips you tracked live with the app; your pre-Polarsteps life is blank. It positions itself as private sharing with a small group, so its outputs are not designed to be public flex cards.
- Polarsteps Plus has no free trial and gates stats and 3D maps, which signals that its subscription is a thin layer.

## The wedge
Zero-effort backfill. Read photo EXIF locations on device (no upload), reconstruct every trip since your first smartphone, and produce a lifetime map plus "you have seen 23% of the world" in about ten seconds. Then keep it current passively from new photos. Polarsteps needs live tracking; Been apps need manual taps.

## The 2-second clip
A dark globe; the user taps "Scan my photos"; countries light up one by one in a fast sweep while a counter climbs "1, 4, 11, 27 countries", ending on a big "27 countries, 112 cities, 14% of the world" card.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: photo permission, instant lifetime map (on-device processing is also the privacy story).
- Repeat action: weak. Travel is episodic. The best you get is "new place detected" notifications after trips, a bucket-list "next country" and friend comparisons.
- Streak/reward: badges per continent, % of world, "states visited" for US users. No natural daily unit.
- Shareable artifact: lifetime map card, "travel age" and annual recap in December.
- Paywall: after the map reveal, $4.99-9.99 one-time or $19.99/year for high-res posters, city-level detail, recap video and printed poster (physical upsell, like Polarsteps books).

## Risks
- Saturation: dozens of map apps plus a free, well-loved incumbent with 23M users that already ships a yearly recap.
- Retention: users return a few times a year, so subscription LTV is low; this is a one-off purchase product.
- Platform: iOS limited photo access reduces the magic for privacy-minded users. Location data is sensitive; on-device processing is mandatory.
- Seasonality: share spikes in December and summer only.

## Scores
| Criterion | 1-5 | Why |
|---|---|---|

| Visual short-video hook | 4 | Globe-fill reveal and "how many countries" comparisons; viral proof not found |
| Money already swims | 2 | Map apps sit at <$10K/month (AppGoblin); Polarsteps money is mostly print books |

| **Total** | **19/30** | |

## Verdict
Maybe, as a low-cost viral side product, not a subscription business. The photo-backfill aha is genuinely strong and shareable, but money in this category is thin and Polarsteps already owns the recap moment for free. Build only if paired with a physical product (posters, books) and launched for the December recap season.
