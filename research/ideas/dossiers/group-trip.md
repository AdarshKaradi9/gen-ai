# Group Trip: polls, shared itinerary and expense split in one link

## One-line pitch
Drop one link in the group chat and friends vote on dates, destination and stays, see a shared itinerary, and split costs, without anyone installing three apps.

## Who exactly
Friend groups of 4-12 aged 21-35 planning bachelor/bachelorette parties, festivals and annual friend trips. They plan in WhatsApp/iMessage groups. Splitwise and Wanderlog are the default tools; JetBlue's Troupe was built because group trips "fragment across three apps" (Product Hunt post). Community sizes for group-travel subreddits were not found this round.

## Demand evidence
- Wanderlog: 71K iOS ratings at 4.89 stars and about 139K iOS MAU (AppGoblin, third-party estimate); "over 1 million users" (directory, unverified).
- Splitwise free-tier daily expense limits (reports of 3-4, later 10 per day) and a 10-second cooldown triggered a wave of 1-star reviews and Trustpilot complaints in 2023-24 (user reviews via Kimola and Trustpilot). This proves heavy usage and that people care enough to be angry.
- JetBlue launched Troupe in 2022 for polls, RSVPs and Vrbo booking (press), showing travel companies see group planning as a funnel.
- Lambus: no data found this round.

## Money proof
- Wanderlog: $1.5M seed (TechCrunch 2021); Pro about $40-50/year with offline access, Google Maps export and Gmail sync; plus booking commissions. AppGoblin bracket "<$10K/month" iOS IAP (third-party estimate, likely low). No verified revenue.
- Splitwise Pro: reported $5/month or $40/year (user reports). No revenue figure found.
- Troupe is free, subsidised by JetBlue.
- Overall: high usage, weak direct willingness to pay. Money is in bookings (stays, flights), not subscriptions.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Splitwise: "free version useless" with daily caps, "zero communication about new daily limit", cooldown seen as deliberate crippling (Trustpilot, Kimola review analyses). Users moved to Settle Up, Splid and Tricount.
- Wanderlog: paywall complaints (attachments, offline, even dark mode behind Pro; only a yearly plan), "white screen" and loading bugs right after subscribing, cluttered UI with constant pop-ups (Trustpilot 1.8/5 on about 45 reviews; AI review summaries, lower reliability). Its App Store rating is still 4.89.
- Wanderlog is planner-first; voting and money are bolted on. Troupe is polls-first but lacks a strong expense split and is tied to JetBlue/Vrbo.

## The wedge
Web-first, no-install participation: the organiser uses the app, friends vote and add expenses from a link in the chat. Unlimited free expense logging (the explicit anti-Splitwise promise), with a one-time "trip pass" ($9.99 per trip, paid by the organiser or split) instead of a subscription. Revenue add-on: stay and activity booking commissions.

## The 2-second clip
A group chat screenshot shows "Lisbon or Mexico City?"; tap the link, eight avatars stack onto "Lisbon" in a live poll, and it snaps into a dated itinerary card with "You owe Sam $42".

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: create trip, paste link into chat, first votes arrive within minutes.
- Daily action: during planning weeks and the trip itself, vote, add expenses, check itinerary. Dormant between trips.
- Streak/reward: weak; "trips together" count and settle-up completion.
- Shareable artifact: the invite link is the growth engine; post-trip recap card ("6 friends, 4 days, 1 Lisbon").
- Paywall: per-trip pass at $9.99 when a trip passes 4 members or adds receipt scanning; avoid daily caps.

## Risks
- Monetisation: users punished Splitwise for paywalling a core action; free alternatives are plentiful (Tricount, Splid, Settle Up, Troupe).
- Retention: episodic; most users plan 1-3 group trips a year.
- Saturation: Wanderlog is well loved, and airlines and booking sites can fund free tools.
- Platform: low; web-first reduces App Store dependence but also reduces in-app purchase conversion.

## Verdict
Skip as a standalone subscription app. Virality is built in, but the category trains users to expect free tools and punishes paywalls, and the real money sits with booking platforms. Revisit only as a booking-commission play or a per-trip pass for high-spend trips like bachelor parties.
