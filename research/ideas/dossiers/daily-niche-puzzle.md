# Daily Niche Puzzle

## One-line pitch
A Wordle-style daily puzzle for one passionate fandom (start with cricket), one puzzle a day, with an emoji result grid made for WhatsApp groups and X.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
- **Beachhead:** cricket fans in India, Pakistan, the UK and Australia who argue in WhatsApp groups and follow live scores daily. I did not retrieve fan-base or subreddit sizes in this pass.
- **Where they gather:** cricket X/Twitter, WhatsApp family and office groups, r/Cricket, Cricinfo comments.
- **Adjacent niches:** Bollywood, football, geography (Worldle), each with similar daily-guess formats.

## Demand evidence
- **Wordle scale:** NYT acquired Wordle in January 2022 for "low seven figures" (press); one report puts Wordle at about 10.7M average monthly players in 2025 (third-party).
- **NYT Games:** over 10M daily players and over 1M premium subscribers as of 2024 (Wikipedia citing NYT); launched Connections: Sports Edition in February 2025, showing sports-trivia appetite.
- **Niche proof:** Immaculate Grid (baseball) launched April 2023, reached 100K+ daily plays by mid-June and about 200K users most weekdays, and was acquired by Sports Reference in July 2023 for an undisclosed price (Front Office Sports, Sports Reference).
- **Cricket supply exists but no breakout:** Crickingo (6+ daily cricket games, Product Hunt), Nurdle, Crickdle, Crickle, Stumple, "Guess the Cricketer." A cricket blog reviewed four cricket Wordles together. No player counts found for any.
- **Steven Cravotta's "Wordle!" app:** the brief asks about its sale; I could not verify a sale or price in this pass.

## Money proof
- **Ad and traffic model:** Sports Reference said Immaculate Grid would stay free with ad units and possibly a subscription, and that it "is already seen as a money maker" through traffic it drives to its sites (press, self-reported).
- **Exits:** Wordle (low seven figures to NYT), Immaculate Grid (undisclosed to Sports Reference), Puzzmo (acquired by Hearst Newspapers, per PocketGamer). Buyers are publishers wanting daily engagement.
- **Subscriptions:** NYT Games sells archive access (Connections archive is subscriber-only) inside a bundle; no indie daily-puzzle app with verified subscription revenue was found.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Cricket Wordles reviewed by a cricket blog stalled on narrow guess validation (e.g. Crickdle cannot accept certain names).
- Most cricket puzzles are hobby web pages with no streaks, accounts, or leagues; share text is generic.
- I did not gather structured 1-2 star review themes.

## The wedge
Tie the daily puzzle to the live cricket calendar: on match days the puzzle is about today's players, and private WhatsApp "leagues" rank friends and family weekly. Better data (every international and IPL player) and fair guess validation fix the main complaint about existing cricket Wordles.

## The 2-second clip
A WhatsApp group chat filling with green and yellow emoji grids under "Cricketle #214 🏏 3/6", then a friend replies "how did you get Bumrah in 3??"

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** play today's puzzle instantly with no signup; result grid appears in under a minute.
- **Daily action:** one puzzle a day, plus a match-day bonus.
- **Streak/reward:** streak with freezes; weekly friends league.
- **Shareable artifact:** the emoji grid with puzzle number, the entire growth engine.
- **Monetization:** free daily puzzle with light ads; optional $2.99/month or $19.99/year for archive, extra modes and leagues. A hard paywall would kill the share loop.

## Risks
- **Unit economics:** ad revenue in India is low per user; subscription conversion for puzzles outside a big bundle is unproven. Paid acquisition will not pay back.
- **Copycats:** trivial to clone; cricket already has five-plus clones.
- **IP/platform:** player names are facts, but logos, team marks, and IPL branding are licensed; avoid them.
- **Retention:** strong daily habit if the puzzle is good, but tied to the cricket season.

## Scores
| Criterion | 1-5 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Two-second aha | 4 | Guess rows turning green read instantly (Wordle format) |
| Visual short-video hook | 3 | Grids spread by text more than video; reactions are possible but not native |
| Money already swims | 2 | Exits exist (Wordle, Immaculate Grid) but revenue is ads/bundle, not app subscriptions |
| Daily reason to return | 5 | One puzzle per day is the purest daily unit |
| Shareable moment | 5 | The emoji grid is the canonical share artifact |
| Unit economics | 1 | Ad-funded, low-ARPU geographies, no paywall-friendly moment |
| **Total** | **20/30** | |

## Verdict
Skip as a subscription business, build only as a cheap distribution asset. It is the strongest viral loop in this set and costs little to make, but the money proof is acquisitions by publishers and ad traffic, not consumer subscriptions. Worth a weekend build if the aim is an audience to sell to or an exit to a cricket media company.
