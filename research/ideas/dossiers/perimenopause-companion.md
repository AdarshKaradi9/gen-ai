# Perimenopause Strength and Symptom Companion

## One-line pitch
A consumer app for women 40-55 that links daily symptom check-ins (sleep, hot flashes, mood, brain fog) with a simple protein and strength plan, showing which habits move which symptoms, without being a telehealth or HRT prescriber.

## Who exactly
- Beachhead: US and UK women 40-55 in early perimenopause who lift or want to start, and who follow "menopause strength" and protein creators.
- Size: about 2 million US women enter perimenopause each year and about 6,000 reach menopause daily (FemTech Insider, citing research). Flo says one in three US women 35+ do not know whether they are in perimenopause (company research).
- Where they gather: r/Menopause and r/Perimenopause (sizes not verified in this pass), Facebook menopause groups, Instagram and TikTok midlife-fitness creators.

## Demand evidence
- Exploding Topics: "perimenopause app" at 720 monthly searches, +2900% growth (third-party estimate). Small absolute volume.
- Flo/Wakefield survey: 66% of US women 38-50 felt better prepared for puberty than perimenopause; 48% said perimenopause impaired their ability to exercise (company-commissioned survey).
- Capital and big players flooding in: Midi Health raised $100M at over $1B valuation (Feb 2026, press); Flo (80M MAU) launched perimenopause in July 2025 and a paid Perimenopause Score suite in May 2026; Apple announced at WWDC 2026 free perimenopause symptom logging and cycle-pattern notifications for users 40+ in the Health app.
- A cluster of indie midlife strength apps (StrongEra, Simply Strong, Menovation, GetStrong) exists, showing supply, not proven demand.

## Money proof
- Balance (Dr Louise Newson): 33K App Store ratings at 4.75, about 99K MAU, but AppGoblin estimates under $10K/month IAP revenue (third-party estimate, bucketed). The category leader by reviews monetises weakly because core features are free.
- Other menopause apps on AppGoblin (Menopause Symptom Tracker, Menovation, thePause, Owning Your Menopause) all sit in the under $10K/month bucket with tiny MAU (third-party estimates). Health & Her about 14K MAU, revenue not available.
- The money in this space is in telehealth and prescriptions (Midi, Hims & Hers menopause lines, Noom-style bundles), not in consumer subscription apps. I found no menopause consumer app with verified or estimated revenue above $10K/month.

## Competitor weaknesses
- Flo: perimenopause tools are behind Flo Premium; Trustpilot and App Store analyses show recurring subscription-fee and "paywall app" complaints, and a reviewer said it struggles to adapt to perimenopause or hormonal contraception (Kimola, Trustpilot; AI-summarised sources are low confidence).
- Balance: reviews are positive; one aggregator found too few recent reviews to extract complaint themes. Its focus is HRT information, not training or nutrition.
- Midday: described as AI plus Mayo Clinic guidance; I found no review or revenue data.
- Apple Health will offer free symptom logging to every iPhone user, which commoditises plain tracking.

## The wedge
Do not compete on symptom tracking (Apple and Flo own it, free or bundled). Compete on "what to do": a two-session-per-week strength plan, protein target, and a weekly symptom-versus-habit report ("weeks you hit 3 lifts and 100 g protein, your sleep score was 1.4 points better"). Pull symptoms from Apple Health to avoid duplicate logging.

## The 2-second clip
A split screen: a symptom heatmap calendar going from red to mostly green over eight weeks, with a caption "8 weeks of lifting in perimenopause". Readable, but transformation is slow and subjective compared with a weight or photo change.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: a 6-question symptom quiz producing a "perimenopause profile" and a personalised 12-week strength and protein plan.
- Daily: 15-second check-in (sleep, flashes, mood, energy) and protein tick; 2-3 short workouts a week.
- Streak: weekly "strong weeks" streak with freezes, more fitting than a daily streak.
- Shareable artifact: "My 12-week midlife strength" card with lifts and symptom trend.
- Paywall: after the plan reveal; $59.99/yr or $12.99/mo with a 7-day trial.

## Risks
- Platform: Apple Health perimenopause features (shipping late 2026) and Flo's 80M MAU absorb the tracking job for free or inside an existing subscription.
- Monetisation: the most-loved app in the category earns under $10K/month by estimate; users are trained to expect free information.
- Medical: symptoms overlap thyroid, depression and other conditions; any HRT advice drifts into regulated territory.
- Acquisition: older audience is reachable on Facebook and Instagram but CPIs for 40-55 women are typically higher; I did not find category CPI data.
- Retention: perimenopause lasts years, which is good for duration, but weekly rather than daily cadence weakens habit loops.

## Verdict
Skip as a consumer subscription app. Demand and cultural momentum are real, but the dollars go to telehealth prescribers, the leading consumer app monetises at under $10K/month by estimate, and Apple and Flo are commoditising tracking in 2026. A strength-for-midlife-women fitness app is a better framing than a perimenopause app, but that is a fitness play competing with creator apps.
