# Quit Sugar (days-free counter on the Quittr / Puff Count model)

## One-line pitch
A "quit X" app for added sugar: a big days-sugar-free counter, an SOS button for cravings, money saved on sweets and drinks, and a body-recovery timeline ("day 3: cravings peak, day 10: taste buds reset"), sold with a hard onboarding paywall.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: women and men 22 to 40 who already tried a "no sugar for 30 days" challenge and relapsed, plus people cutting sugar for weight loss or skin. Where they gather: r/sugarfree, about 202K members and growing about 8.7% a year (GummySearch, third-party; another GummySearch page shows 186K, so treat as roughly 190K to 200K). The "2-week no added sugar" challenge was reported as a viral TikTok trend (Yahoo, press), but I found no hashtag view counts. Secondary: GLP-1 users and prediabetics, who are better served by the GLP-1 and glucose apps already in our findings.

## Demand evidence
- r/sugarfree at about 200K members with steady growth (GummySearch, third-party).
- A Yahoo/press piece describes a viral 2-week no-added-sugar TikTok challenge (press, no view numbers).
- App Store supply is exploding, which signals founders see demand: No Sugar Challenge, No Sugar 30 Days, Unsweet, Sukr, SugarLog, BYEBYE SUGAR, Sugar Quit, Ai SugarFree and others, many launched in 2025 to 2026 (store listings, MWM).
- Not found: Google Trends numbers or TikTok hashtag counts. This is the gap to close before building.

## Money proof
The quit-X model itself is proven:
- Quittr (porn): reportedly $250K MRR four months after launch, hard paywall, $12.99/$45, 3-day trial (press and case studies, self-reported/third-party; from main findings).
- Puff Count (vaping): Flippa listing says $30K MRR, $360K ARR, but TTM gross only $124,300 and net $49,832; $9/week after free trial; 450K downloads, 50M organic TikTok views, 120K TikTok followers, about 25K downloads/month (self-reported in sale listing; the TTM and ARR figures do not reconcile). Founder says he reached $44K MRR with paid ads after organic stalled near $5K/month (self-reported).
- I Am Sober: 17.8M iOS installs, about 738K MAU, $10K+/month bucket, 356K ratings at 4.86 stars (AppGoblin, third-party estimate; bucket is a floor).
- Smoke Free: company claims 7M+ downloads (self-reported); AppGoblin puts each listing at under $10K/month (third-party estimate).
Sugar-specific money is thin:
- SugarBuddy ("Cal AI for the sugar-addicted", panic button, streaks): 16 active subscriptions, verified via Superwall API on TrustMRR (verified). That is tiny.
- Sugarfree (JoyInApps): about 50K+ Android downloads, 3.5 stars (main findings); monthly subs or one-time purchase; outside top 30 in US Health and Fitness (MWM, third-party).
- No Sugar Challenge (AppsLab): about 5.8K installs, 443 MAU (AppGoblin, third-party estimate).
Conclusion: people pay for quit-X apps when the X feels like an addiction with shame attached. No sugar app has yet shown meaningful revenue.

## Competitor weaknesses
From Sugarfree reviews on the App Store: settings and goal changes are hard to find; barcode scans give wrong sugar values; app glitches send users back to the home screen so one user cancelled her trial the same day ("I would have paid for the yearly subscription as I am very desperate to control my sugar"); ads shown even after paying; "lots of info on what sugar-free eating is, but no help on the psychology driving the addiction"; only one mood per day; layout runs into the Dynamic Island. Pattern: incumbents are food loggers wearing a streak costume. The emotional quit-X layer (urge surfing, reasons, timeline) is weak, and logging accuracy breaks trust.

## The wedge
Do not be a sugar calorie counter. Be the Quittr of sugar: no food logging required, just a binary daily check-in ("sugar-free today?"), a craving SOS that runs a 90-second urge-surf with a timer, a recovery timeline tuned to sugar (cravings, energy dips, skin, taste), and "money not spent on sweets and drinks". Optional photo scan only for the "is this hidden sugar?" question, framed as a quiz, not a log. Clear grams-free definition chosen in onboarding (no added sugar vs no sweets) and editable in one tap, fixing the top complaint.

## The 2-second clip
Phone screen, giant number "Day 21" with a ring filling, and below it a timeline card flipping to "Taste buds reset: fruit tastes sweeter now". Overlay text: "21 days no sugar, the app told me exactly what would happen". Second variant: thumb slams a red SOS button, a breathing circle appears, caption "how I beat 3pm sugar cravings".

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha (under 60 seconds): pick your sugar type (sweets, soda, coffee drinks, all added sugar), daily spend estimate, why you quit. Show a personal plan: "By day 14 you'll save $63 and cravings drop" plus the full timeline preview.
- Daily action: morning pledge, evening yes/no check-in, SOS during cravings.
- Streak and reward: days-free counter with two freezes and a "slip, not reset" option (log a slip without zeroing, per the bend-not-break pattern). Milestone badges at 3, 7, 14, 30, 90 days.
- Shareable artifact: a "Day 30 sugar-free" card with money saved and the next body milestone, watermarked.
- Paywall: last onboarding screen, hard paywall, 3-day trial, about $39.99/year preselected and $6.99/week alternative, matching Quittr's tiers. Honest charge-date copy.

## Risks
- Saturation: dozens of near-identical "quit sugar" apps shipped in 2025 to 2026, many by the same template builders; App Store spam rules (4.3 design spam) can hit template clones.
- Weaker pain than nicotine or porn: sugar quitting is closer to a diet, so willingness to pay and shame-driven urgency are lower. The one verified sugar app has 16 subs.
- Medical claims: avoid "addiction cure" and diabetes language; the body timeline must be hedged. Diabetics need medical apps, not this.
- Retention: diet challenges cluster around January and summer; people "finish" a 30-day challenge and churn.
- Platform dependence: growth depends on TikTok; Puff Count's founder says organic stalled at about $5K/month before paid ads.

## Verdict
Maybe, leaning build only as a fast, cheap test. The quit-X playbook is proven and incumbent sugar apps are buggy food loggers, so there is a real wedge, but no sugar app has yet shown real revenue and the shelf is filling with clones. Validate with two weeks of TikTok posts on the SOS and day-21 formats before writing much code.
