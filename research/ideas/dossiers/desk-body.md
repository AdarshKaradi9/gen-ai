# Desk Body (2-minute desk-worker reset)

## One-line pitch
A 2-minute reset between meetings: the camera checks your neck and shoulder position, you do three guided moves and an eye break, and the camera shows the difference.

## Who exactly (the beachhead audience and where they gather online, with sizes)
Remote and hybrid knowledge workers, software engineers first, who sit 8+ hours and feel neck and upper-back stiffness. They gather on X/Twitter and LinkedIn (where a solo Mac posture app found its first buyers), Hacker News, r/cscareerquestions and ergonomics and posture subreddits, and TikTok "tech neck" and desk-stretch content. Subreddit and hashtag sizes could not be checked (Reddit blocked our fetcher; no TikTok counts found). Stretching content clearly travels: Bend's TikTok account drew about 42.5M views (Social Growth Engineers, third-party).

## Demand evidence (searches, communities, trend signals, with sources)
- Mainstream health press runs steady "tech neck" stretch guides (Popular Science, Fit&Well, Built In), a sign of persistent search demand.
- At least nine posture apps were compared in 2026 roundups (Backed, Posture AI, Posture Pal, Upright GO, APECS, Bodiometer, SpineSeek, SitSense, Posture Reminder AI), many written by the apps themselves, which signals both interest and an SEO land-grab.
- Bend ranks top 3 for 728 App Store keywords including "posture stretch" and "daily stretches" (Growth Hacking Lab, third-party).
- No Google Trends figures found.

## Money proof (competitors with revenue/downloads, pricing, labelled by evidence type)
- **Bend:** estimates range from about $500K to $600K/month on about 100K monthly downloads (Social Growth Engineers and Growth Hacking Lab) up to about $1.1M/month (tasu teardown, as quoted in search results) (all third-party estimates). Said to hold over 86% of stretching-category revenue. Pricing $13.99/month or $39.99/yr. Over 10M users (self-reported).
- **Wakeout:** Apple App of the Year 2020; requires a subscription, US price about $29.99/yr or $6.49/month (store listing). No revenue figure found.
- **StretchIt:** about $19.99 to $24.99/month or $159.99 to $179.99/yr (comparison sites); no revenue found.
- **Posture apps:** Backed $38.99/yr; Posture AI $39.99/yr; Posture Pal $24.99/yr or $19.99 one-time; Upright GO 2 device about $79 (store listings via Backed's comparison). No revenue figures found.
- **Solo Mac webcam posture app:** about $1,000 in its first week, launched through X, LinkedIn, Threads and Reddit (Starter Story, self-reported).

## Competitor weaknesses (what 1-2 star reviews and complaints say)
- Wakeout: users expected free and hit a subscription wall after sign-in; "can't afford another subscription just to have my phone tell me to do some leg lifts at my desk"; one paying user said the app would not open and support did not reply; too expensive in India.
- Bend: long 22-screen onboarding, a review request before any value, and a 67% discount that appears when you close the paywall (teardowns). Sessions are general stretching, not tied to the workday.
- Posture trackers (Posture Pal, wearables) only nag; they do not fix anything. Upright GO cannot detect forward head posture (SitSense comparison). Camera apps like Backed have few ratings (27).

## The wedge (the specific angle that beats incumbents)
Workday-shaped and measurable. Bend is a 5 to 20 minute flexibility routine on your phone; posture apps only nag. Desk Body is a Mac menu-bar plus iPhone app that suggests a 2-minute reset in calendar gaps, uses the webcam to measure head-forward angle and shoulder height before and after, and folds in a 20-20-20 eye break. The measured before/after is the proof and the content engine. Later, sell team plans to remote companies (a B2B path Bend does not chase).

## The 2-second clip
Side view of an engineer at a laptop with a skeleton overlay: a red line from ear to shoulder reads "Head forward 4.1 cm". Cut to after three chin tucks and a wall angel: the line snaps green, "0.9 cm". Caption: "2 minutes between standups."

## Core loop sketch
- **Onboarding aha:** a 10-second webcam posture scan that shows your number and a side-profile overlay, then one 2-minute reset with an immediate re-scan.
- **Daily action:** 3 to 5 resets per workday, nudged in calendar gaps (notification about 23.5 hours after yesterday's first reset).
- **Streak/reward:** workday streak (weekends do not break it), weekly "posture trend" chart.
- **Shareable artifact:** 30-day side-profile before/after card and a team leaderboard of resets, not scores.
- **Paywall:** after the first re-scan, framed outcome first. $39.99/yr (annual preselected) or $7.99/month, 7-day trial; hard paywall is plausible because the scan value is shown first. Team plan per seat later.

## Risks (regulatory, platform, saturation, retention)
- **Medical claims:** must stay educational; avoid promising pain relief. The link between measured posture and pain is debated, so frame as comfort and habit, not treatment.
- **Privacy:** webcam use at work needs on-device processing and a clear statement.
- **Saturation:** Bend dominates stretching ASO and paid ads; many posture apps exist, though none has broken out.
- **Retention:** break reminders are easy to dismiss; weekends and holidays dent habits.
- **Platform:** a Mac app is outside the usual iOS-only paywall playbook; cross-platform billing adds work.

## Verdict
Maybe, leaning build. The money is proven by Bend and the measured before/after gives a real demo and wedge, but the category is crowded with posture nags and Bend owns the ads. Build if the founder can reach engineers through X and LinkedIn launch posts (as the Mac posture app did) and keep the claims modest.
