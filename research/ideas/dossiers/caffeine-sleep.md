# Caffeine Cutoff (caffeine and sleep tracker)

## One-line pitch
Log a coffee in one tap and see a half-life curve showing how much caffeine will still be in you at bedtime, with a personal "last coffee" cutoff time and, over weeks, how late caffeine actually changes your sleep from Apple Health data.

## Who exactly
Beachhead: 25 to 40 year old knowledge workers who sleep badly, wear an Apple Watch or Oura ring, and drink 2+ coffees or energy drinks a day. Where they gather: r/decaf, about 52K members (GummySearch, third-party; Rolling Stone earlier counted 40K+), sleep and Oura communities, and the "caffeine minimalist" wave Bloomberg covered (via pulse.bot summary, press). Secondary: shift workers (CaffSense already targets them) and pregnant users (200mg limit; medical sensitivity).

## Demand evidence
- A Drive Research survey press release says sleep quality became the top motivation for Americans planning to cut caffeine in 2026 (company press release, self-reported survey).
- Bloomberg-described "caffeine minimalist" movement, with wearables like Oura quantifying sleep effects (press, via summary).
- r/decaf about 52K members; withdrawal is the second most common topic (GummySearch, third-party).
- Rise Science, a sleep app claiming 5M to 10M users (self-reported), ships a daily "when to stop caffeine" cutoff as a core habit, showing the need is recognised.
- Not found: Google Trends or TikTok hashtag numbers for "caffeine half-life" or "caffeine cutoff".

## Money proof
- RISE: Sleep Tracker: about 333K MAU, $50K+/month bucket (43% IAP, 57% ads), 115K ratings at 4.66 (AppGoblin, third-party estimate). Price $69.99/year with a 14-day trial (company page and Tom's Guide). Raised $15.5M (press, 2021). Caffeine cutoff is a feature inside a sleep app, not the product.
- HiCoffee: about 83.3K installs, about 5K MAU, under $10K/month, 1.7K ratings at 4.78, installs declining (AppGoblin, third-party estimate). A reviewer paid $8.99 one-time.
- Caffeine App - Daily Tracker (since 2015): about 1.4K MAU, under $10K/month, 581 ratings (AppGoblin, third-party estimate).
- New entrants cluster at low prices: Caff ($0.99), CaffeineTrack (one-time purchase, "no subscriptions" as selling point), SleepWindow ($19.99/year), plus Caffeine Flow, Caffeine Clock, CaffSense, Coffee Cutoff, Caffix with few or no ratings (store listings).
Conclusion: a decade of caffeine trackers has produced no known app above the $10K/month bucket. The money sits in broad sleep apps (Rise), not in caffeine tracking.

## Competitor weaknesses
HiCoffee reviews (App Store): no "what can I still drink and sleep by 10pm?" planner, the curve only updates after you log; drinks are absorbed instantly instead of over the time it takes to sip; missing chains (Wawa, regional fast food) and sodas; no caffeine from medicines or chocolate; Apple Watch sync failures after paying; one user "confused as to what I'm supposed to do with the information"; buyers prefer one-time purchases and dislike subscriptions. The last two are the real problem: the insight is a one-time "oh" and the audience resists subscriptions.

## The wedge
Make it about sleep outcomes, not milligrams. (1) A forward planner: "a latte now leaves 60mg at 11pm, a small drip leaves 25mg". (2) Personal sensitivity: after 14 nights, correlate cutoff time with Apple Health or Oura sleep and say "for you, coffee after 1:40pm costs 22 minutes of deep sleep". (3) A branded drink database (Starbucks, Dunkin, Celsius, Monster, Wawa) with sip-duration modelling. This is what HiCoffee reviewers ask for and what Rise only does generically.

## The 2-second clip
A phone showing a smooth orange curve: a 3pm cold brew spike that is still above a dotted "bedtime" line at 11pm, with a red label "120mg still in you at bedtime". Caption: "This is why you can't sleep." Strong hook, but it is a one-time reveal.

## Core loop sketch
- Onboarding aha: enter usual drinks and bedtime; instantly show yesterday's curve crossing bedtime and a personal cutoff time.
- Daily action: one-tap log from widget or Watch; morning "how did you sleep" pulled from Health.
- Streak and reward: "clean bedtime" streak (under 50mg at lights out), weekly report showing sleep on clean vs late-caffeine nights.
- Shareable artifact: "My caffeine at bedtime" curve card, or a "Coffee Wrapped" monthly total (cups, mg, money).
- Paywall: soft paywall; free curve and cutoff, paid sleep-correlation insights and planner. Realistic price $14.99 to $24.99/year or $6.99 lifetime-style one-time, given SleepWindow and HiCoffee price points. A hard paywall at Rise-like $69.99 seems unlikely to work for a single-feature app.

## Risks
- Low willingness to pay: market anchors at one-time $0.99 to $8.99; best-known indie earns under $10K/month (AppGoblin estimate).
- Feature, not product: Rise, Oura, Whoop, Apple Health and Bevel-style apps can add a caffeine curve at any time; Rise already has the cutoff.
- Retention: once users learn their cutoff, they stop logging. The insight decays fast.
- Saturation: at least eight caffeine-sleep apps launched or updated in 2025 to 2026 with near-identical curves.
- Health claims: pregnancy and heart conditions need careful disclaimers; half-life varies widely (genetics, smoking, pills), so precise mg numbers overstate certainty.

## Verdict
Skip as a standalone subscription business. The aha is excellent and makes a great free hook or a feature inside a broader sleep or energy app, but ten years of caffeine trackers show low revenue, users who prefer one-time payment, and insights that decay once learned. Consider it as a free lead magnet (web calculator or widget) for a larger sleep product.
