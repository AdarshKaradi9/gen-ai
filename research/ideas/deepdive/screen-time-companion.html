The verdict

Build it, but go in with clear eyes. One person can build this, the demand is real, and nobody ships the exact combination yet. But the category is crowded, the platform is fragile, and the economics only work if growth is organic.

Question Answer
Does anyone do exactly this? No. Every piece exists somewhere, but no app combines a healing character, friends alerted on bypass, and a student focus
Biggest threat BePresent, not Opal. It already has friends, group sessions, money stakes and the "years" framing, and it's growing fastest in the category
Can one person build it? Yes, on iPhone only, in roughly 8 to 12 weeks
Hardest part Making Apple's Screen Time API work reliably across iOS 26 versions
Can paid ads drive growth? No. At $34.99 a year, an install earns about $0.89 in year one but costs $3.50 to $8 to buy
Year-one outcome, base case About 88K installs and about $7K a month in revenue by month 12. The range is $0.6K to $45K
Launch timing Beta in February 2027 and public launch in March, ahead of spring finals in April and May

The plan below is designed around those facts.

2. The sharpened angle

The round-one idea led with "years of your life back". The research kills that as a differentiator, because Brainrot and BePresent both already use it. Keep the number in onboarding, but don't sell the app on it.

Positioning: The phone companion that heals when you do, and the two friends who'll notice if you cheat. Built for finals.

There are three pillars, in this order.

  1. A companion that heals. The character responds to your day. It brightens on good days, dims on bad ones, and recovers when you recover. A broken streak can be earned back. Nothing dies.
  2. Two friends, gentle visibility. When you bypass a block, one or two chosen friends get a gentle note, such as "Sam unlocked TikTok at 1:14am. Send a nudge?". This is the least-taken flavour of accountability: friends see, but they don't approve or deny.
  3. Finals mode. A "lock in" session with a shareable count of study hours, campus groups, and content timed to the exam calendar.

Honest billing runs underneath all three. One price, a 7-day trial, a reminder on day 5, a visible charge date, and no exit-discount tricks.

3. Who it's for

The first audience is US college students aged 18 to 24, sold around the exam calendar.

  • They feel the problem. In Pew's 2026 survey, 70% of 18-to-29-year-olds say they spend too much time on their phone, and 42% say they're on it "almost constantly". About 58% of 18-to-49-year-olds tried to cut back in the past year.
  • They choose their own apps and live in dense friend groups, which is exactly what the friend feature needs.
  • They have fixed panic dates: fall finals in December, spring finals in April and May.
  • They spend less, so the annual price stays under $40.
  • The lane is contested. Opal says 70% of its 10 million users are students (self-reported). We win on the friend mechanic and finals content, not by calling ourselves a student blocker.

Second audience: young professionals, after the student loop is proven.

Not now: - High schoolers through their parents. That's a different buyer and a different product, with minors' privacy rules. - Marketing to ADHD adults. That invites scrutiny of medical claims. Keep ADHD-friendly design as a principle, not a label.

4. What the science supports

These findings set both the design and the marketing claims.

Finding Evidence Design consequence
Self-set limits work best Letting people set their own limits cut use of targeted apps by 22 minutes a day, or 17% (American Economic Review, 2022) Users set their own limits in onboarding
A pause before opening an app works one sec's 2023 study cut target-app opens by 57%, with 36% of attempts abandoned at the pause (peer-reviewed) Use a pause, but vary the prompts and lengthen it after repeated bypasses, because users learn to tap through
Friend accountability is unproven No trial found for phone use Treat it as an A/B test from day one, and never claim it's proven
Guilt-only mechanics backfire Broken streaks lead people to quit (CHI 2016); reviews favour moderation over full detox The character heals, streaks can be repaired, and goals are moderation targets
Cutting back helps mood and focus Blocking mobile internet for two weeks improved mental health and attention (PNAS Nexus, 2025). Capping social media at about 30 minutes a day reduced loneliness in undergraduates (2018) Say "linked to better mood, focus and sleep". Never say "raise your GPA" or "cures anxiety"

The "years" number, done honestly. The formula is daily hours on the chosen apps ÷ 24 × remaining life expectancy. A 20-year-old has about 58 years left. At 5.5 hours a day, that's about 13 calendar years.

  • Count only the apps the user picks.
  • Say "at this pace".
  • Show the formula.
  • Never say "lost years of life".

5. Product spec, version 1

The core loop

Moment What happens
Onboarding, about 20 screens Pick your three worst apps, enter daily hours, see "At this pace: 13 years on these apps", set your own limits, meet the companion, add one or two friends, and grant Screen Time access. The paywall comes last.
The first aha The first block fires on a chosen app, and the companion reacts on the shield screen.
Daily The companion's mood follows usage events through the day. Each unlock goes through a pause and an intention prompt. An evening check-in shows "today vs your limit".
On a bypass The shield sends a notification. Tapping it opens our unlock screen, the unlock is logged, and the chosen friends get a gentle note.
Streak Days under your limit, with two freezes. A missed day can be earned back with a good day.
Weekly A share card with the companion, the hours reclaimed, and the streak, sized for Instagram stories.
Finals mode A timed "lock in" session with all distracting apps shielded, a study-hours counter, and group sessions with friends.

6. Technical plan

Platform: iPhone only. Android means a separate build, and since January 2026 Google Play has reviewed the main blocking method more strictly.

Stack: - Expo with a custom development build and the react-native-device-activity library (version 0.6.1, February 2026) for picking apps, scheduling and shielding. - Hand-written Swift for the three extensions: the shield appearance, the shield buttons and the activity monitor. - Supabase or Firebase for friends, notifications and a server heartbeat. - RevenueCat for the paywall and subscriptions.

What Apple's Screen Time API can and can't do:

Need Feasibility
Block chosen apps on a schedule Yes
Custom block screen with the companion Yes, through the shield configuration
Know which app was opened, or exact usage No. Apple returns hidden tokens. Usage reports can be drawn on screen but not exported or sent to a server.
Companion mood from usage Partly. It has to use rough threshold events, such as one per 15 minutes of use.
"Years" number Only from a figure the user enters during onboarding
Detect a sanctioned unlock and notify friends Yes. The block screen sends a notification, and tapping it opens our unlock screen, so every sanctioned unlock passes through our code.
Detect the user revoking permission or deleting the app No live signal. A server heartbeat can tell friends when the blocker "goes quiet". This design still needs testing.
Stop the user disabling the blocker Possibly. According to one sec, iOS 26.4 lets a Screen Time passcode lock third-party blockers, and a friend could set it. Apple hasn't confirmed this.

Hard parts and risks: - File Apple's permission requests on day one. Apple approves the Family Controls permission separately for the app and each of its three extensions. Developers report waits from one day to about two months, and beta testing is blocked until all four are approved. - Known iOS 26 bugs need guards from day one. Usage events fire with zero usage, schedule callbacks sometimes don't fire, and a schedule set to start "now" can slip to tomorrow. The technical report lists workarounds for each. - The iOS simulator can't run the background extensions. Expect weeks of overnight testing on real phones. - Reliability protects revenue. Users' angriest reviews are about blocks that silently stop and streaks that get wiped.

7. Pricing and paywall

Every choice below follows from the 2026 subscription benchmarks and from competitors' complaints.

Decision Choice Why
Paywall type Hard paywall at the end of onboarding Hard paywalls convert downloads to paid about 5 times better than freemium, with similar retention (RevenueCat 2026)
Trial 7 days, on the annual plan Trials of 5 to 9 days convert at a 37% median, against 26% for 4 days or less. 55% of 3-day-trial cancellations happen on day 0
Price $34.99 a year preselected, $5.99 a month The category band is $20 to $60. Opal's $99.99 draws the most anger
Weekly plan None Weekly plans keep only 1 to 2% of subscribers after a year, and Brainrot reviewers call $5 a week "insane"
Discounts No exit-intent discount Brainrot's "50% off forever" pop-up is called "scammy" in one-star reviews
Honesty A trial reminder on day 5, the charge date visible on the paywall and in settings Surprise trial charges are about 24% of one-to-three-star reviews in this category
Test later A three-friend group plan, such as $59.99 a year for three people Students pay less, and a group plan pulls in invites

8. Launch plan

Timing. The build takes 8 to 12 weeks, so December 2026 finals are too soon. Content starts now and builds an audience, and the product launches ahead of spring finals.

Period Product Growth
Nov 2026, weeks 1-2 File the Apple permission requests. Spike the shield, the unlock and the notification on a real phone. Start one founder-led account on TikTok and Instagram. Post the "I'm building an app that heals when I put my phone down" story daily. Put up a landing page with a waitlist.
Dec 2026 Core blocking, the companion, onboarding and the paywall Ride December finals with "lock in" study content filmed using a prototype. Recruit 30 to 50 student beta testers from the waitlist.
Jan 2027 Friends, finals mode, the weekly card, and guards for the iOS bugs New Year digital-detox content. Recruit campus ambassadors at 3 pilot schools.
Feb 2027 Private beta. Fix reliability. A/B test friend alerts on and off. Pitch campus libraries and counselling centres. One university library mounted a Brick for finals in October 2026, and its counselling centre gave out a free code for another blocker.
Mar 2027 Public launch Six posts a day across two TikTok accounts and Instagram. One $100 to $150 creator test, then 8 to 10 student micro-creators at about $150 a week each. Apple Search Ads at $30 a day on long-tail keywords.
Apr-May 2027 Finals mode campaign Spark Ads behind organic posts that already work. Run group finals sessions on campuses.

Budget. About $12K over the eight launch weeks: - creators - ambassadors at $100 a month plus $1 per install - Apple Search Ads, at a US median of $1.79 per tap - small boosts behind posts that already work

Expected result. 3 to 5 million views and 10,000 to 25,000 installs over eight weeks, if at least one format hits. That's an estimate built on founders' self-reported benchmarks.

Content formats with evidence behind them: 1. Screen-time before-and-after. The screenshot is the proof, and this is the safest format for ads. 2. The companion reacting. Opening TikTok makes it wilt, and a week later it's glowing. 3. The friend-alert skit. "My friend got a notification that I opened TikTok at 1am." 4. "Lock in" study sessions with the study-hours counter on screen. 5. The founder's own story, posted daily. This is how Brainrot and one sec started.

App Store name. Lead with keywords you can win. "Screen time" and "app blocker" are held by apps with 53K to 91K ratings. "Lock in", "study blocker" and "dopamine detox" are within reach. For example: [Name]: Lock In & Study Blocker, with the subtitle Dopamine detox, friend check-ins. Put "screen time" and "app blocker" in the keyword field, and avoid "brainrot".

Stop rule. If no post clears 50,000 views by week 5 of posting, change the hook format, not the posting volume.

9. Financial scenarios

These are the economics agent's own model, using RevenueCat medians, Apple's 15% small-business commission, and refunds. Every assumption is written down in the economics report.

Pessimistic Base Optimistic
Installs in year 1 21K 88K 267K
Net revenue per install $0.34 $0.89 $1.83
Monthly revenue at month 12 ~$0.6K ~$7K ~$45K
Total net revenue in year 1 ~$7K ~$73K ~$450K

What moves the numbers most: - Organic reach, which sets install volume. - Download-to-paid conversion. The Health & Fitness median is 2.9%. Hard-paywall apps reach about 10%. - First annual renewal. Productivity apps have the weakest in the market, at 23% at the median. Cheap annual plans keep about 36%.

Paid ads only pay back if the unit economics get two to four times better. That would need a higher price, conversion above 8%, or strong renewals.

10. Risks and when to stop

Risk Mitigation
BePresent adds a character Move fast. Our edge is students, finals and friend visibility, not the character alone
Apple changes or breaks the Screen Time API Guard against the known bugs, keep a server heartbeat, and keep the core value (the companion and friends) useful even when blocking is degraded
Apple's permission approval is slow File on day one. Build the UI and friends features while waiting
Friend alerts feel creepy or cause shame Keep alerts gentle and opt-in, let the user choose what friends see, and test with alerts on and off
Novelty fades after a week Vary the prompts, lengthen the pause after repeated bypasses, and give the companion new stages and seasonal finals events
Students don't pay Test a three-friend group plan and a lower price in the beta

Stop or change course if: 1. Content: after 8 weeks of daily posting, no post has cleared 50,000 views and the waitlist is under 1,000. 2. Beta retention: fewer than 30% of beta users are still active on day 14. 3. Friends: the friend alert doesn't improve day-14 retention compared with the control group. 4. Conversion: download-to-paid is below 2% after 5,000 installs.

11. The next seven days

  1. Day 1. Enrol in the Apple Developer Program. Create the bundle IDs for the app and its three extensions, and file all four Family Controls requests.
  2. Day 1. Pick a working name and grab the TikTok and Instagram handles and a domain.
  3. Days 2-3. Build a throwaway Expo prototype with react-native-device-activity. It should block one app with a custom shield showing a placeholder companion, then confirm the notification-to-unlock flow on a real iPhone.
  4. Days 2-7. Post one video a day, starting with "Day 1 of building an app that heals when I put my phone down". Put up a landing page with a waitlist and an "add two friends" referral.
  5. Day 4. Sketch the companion: three moods and a recovery animation. Commission an illustrator or use one consistent AI-assisted style.
  6. Day 5. Message 20 students you know and ask them two questions: would they let two friends see when they break their own limit, and what would make that feel supportive rather than shaming?
  7. Day 7. Review the week. Count views per post, waitlist sign-ups, and the students' answers on friend visibility.
